Improper Integrals

The definite integral extended past its definition — where a bound is ±∞, or where the integrand has a vertical asymptote at a bound (or inside the interval). The move is always the same: replace the troublesome bound with a limit, and the integral converges iff every one-sided piece does. The engine runs two independent paths — a symbolic antiderivative with one-sided limits (π kept symbolic) and a numeric sequence of partial integrals whose truncation points march geometrically toward the improper end — and the verdict comes from their agreement. The two traps that defeat a naive implementation are both handled: the slow ln divergence of ∫₁^∞ 1/x (caught by watching the increments between partial sums, not a fixed cutoff), and the Cauchy principal value of ∫₋₁¹ 1/x (the interval is split at the asymptote, so a divergent integral is never reported as a convergent 0).
