Complex Trigonometric & Hyperbolic Functions

On the real line, sin x and cos x never leave [-1, 1] — that boundedness is so familiar it feels like part of their definition. It isn't. Extended to ℂ via sin(x+iy) = sin(x)cosh(y) + i·cos(x)sinh(y), the same function is unbounded off the real axis — sin(iy) = i·sinh(y) grows exponentially. This page plots that growth directly, alongside the domain-coloured plane and the algebraic identities (sin²z + cos²z = 1, cosh²z − sinh²z = 1) that still hold, unchanged, everywhere in ℂ.
