Complex Logarithms & Powers — Branches and the Branch Cut

log(z) and z^w for non-integer w are not functions on ℂ in the usual sense — they're multivalued: every nonzero z has infinitely many logarithms, differing by 2πik. To use them as ordinary functions, one value has to be chosen by convention — the principal branch, using Arg(z) ∈ (−π, π] — and that choice forces a discontinuity somewhere. Here it's the negative real axis: cross it and the value jumps by a full turn. This page never hides which branch it's using, and the jump shows up directly as a seam in the domain colouring, not as an error.
