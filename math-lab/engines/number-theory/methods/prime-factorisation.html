Prime Factorisation

Every integer > 1 splits into primes in exactly one way — but *finding* that split is the whole game. Three methods, run on the same number side by side: trial division (plodding, but catches small factors first), Fermat's method (fast when the factors are close), and Pollard's rho (probabilistic, fast when factors are far apart). The operation count is the lesson: the same answer, vastly different work.

Input
Any integer ≥ 2. Semiprimes (two large primes) are the interesting case — the comparison is sharpest there. Operation budget is enforced; a factor that resists is reported, not hidden.
Output

Enter an integer and factorise — three methods, their work counts, and the prime factorisation will appear here.
