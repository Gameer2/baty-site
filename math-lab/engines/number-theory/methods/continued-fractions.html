Continued Fractions of √D

Every irrational square root √D has a *periodic* continued fraction [a₀; a₁, a₂, …, aₖ, 2a₀] — a fact Lagrange proved and that makes √D's arithmetic fundamentally tamer than a generic irrational. Its convergents pₙ/qₙ are the best rational approximations to √D at their size, and — the bridge to the next page — the fundamental solution of Pell's equation x² − D·y² = 1 is always one of them. Watch the convergents lock onto √D, and the period repeat.

Input — D (not a perfect square)
D = 13 → √13 = [3; 1, 1, 1, 1, 6, 1, 1, 1, 1, 6, …], period 5. The convergents (3/1, 4/1, 7/2, 11/3, 18/5, 119/33, …) close in on √13 — and the 5th-but-one solves Pell.
