Pell's Equation

For a non-square D, Pell's equation x² − D·y² = 1 always has a non-trivial integer solution — and a *smallest* one (the fundamental solution) from which all others are generated. Finding it by brute force is hopeless (D = 61 gives x = 1,766,319,049); finding it through the continued fraction of √D is automatic. The convergents of √D march toward the answer, and one of the first period (or two) is the fundamental solution.

Input — D (non-square)
D = 61 is the famous one: the smallest solution is x = 1766319049, y = 226153980 — found here in 21 continued-fraction terms, not a billion trial squares. Try D = 13, 23, or 109.
