Cryptography

RSA

Two large primes p, q, multiplied into a public modulus n = pq. Anyone can encrypt with (n, e); only someone who knows the factorization can compute φ(n) = (p−1)(q−1) and hence the private exponent d. The whole security rests on one fact this engine has already shown you: multiplying primes is easy, factoring the product back is hard. Choose p, q here, generate a key, encrypt a number, and watch it decrypt back — then read why a modulus big enough that trial division visibly fails is the whole point.

Input — two distinct primes p, q
Tiny primes for transparency: n = 61·53 = 3233, a toy. Real RSA uses 1024+ bit primes — large enough that trial-dividing n is hopeless. Use "Generate big primes" to see a realistic modulus (factoring it would need the Pollard-ρ page, not this form).
