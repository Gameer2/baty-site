Distribution of Primes

Primes thin out, but slowly: the prime-counting function π(x) grows like x / ln x (the prime number theorem). Here that's not a claim to prove but a thing to *see* — count exact primes against the smooth approximation, watch the gaps fluctuate, and turn the integers into an Ulam spiral where the primes stubbornly line up on diagonals.

Input
Count and plot primes up to x (sieve is exact; ≤ 10⁶ keeps the page snappy). The Ulam spiral below uses a separate, smaller spiral size.
Output

Enter a bound — the exact prime count, its approximation by x/ln x, the largest gaps, and the Ulam spiral will appear here.
