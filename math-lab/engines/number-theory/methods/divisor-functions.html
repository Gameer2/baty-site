Divisor Functions τ(n), σ(n)

Two functions of the divisors of n: τ(n) counts them, σ(n) sums them. Both are multiplicative — built from the prime factorisation, not by listing. Their most famous consequence is the aliquot sum σ(n) − n, which sorts every integer into deficient, perfect, or abundant; the perfect numbers (6, 28, 496, …) are the rare fixed points.

Input
Any integer ≥ 1. The divisors are generated from the prime factorisation; σ and τ follow, and the aliquot class is read off.
Output

Enter n — the divisor list, τ, σ, and the aliquot classification will appear here.
