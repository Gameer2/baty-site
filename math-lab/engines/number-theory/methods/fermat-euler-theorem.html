Fermat's Little & Euler's Theorem

Two theorems, one engine. Fermat: if p is prime and p ∤ a, then a^(p−1) ≡ 1 (mod p). Euler generalises it: if gcd(a, n) = 1, then a^φ(n) ≡ 1 (mod n). These are the engines of exponentiation — the reason RSA and primality testing work at all. Here you watch them hold (and see exactly where they don't apply).

Input
If n is prime, Fermat applies: a^(n−1) ≡ 1. For any n with gcd(a,n)=1, Euler applies: a^φ(n) ≡ 1. Enter a composite n (e.g. 9) and a coprime base to see Euler where Fermat's prime-only form does not.
Output

Enter a base and modulus — the φ(n) computation and both theorem checks will appear here.
