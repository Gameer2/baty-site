## Grade 5 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

### Semester 1

#### Unit 1: الأعداد الكلّية — Whole Numbers

##### Lesson 1.1: القيمة المنزلية ضمن الملايين — Place Value Within Millions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I determine the place value of a digit in a number within the millions; I read and write numbers within the millions.
- **Key terms:** دورة الملايين (millions period)
- **What's taught:** Extends the ones/thousands "period" grouping (each group of 3 digits) to a third period, millions, using a place-value chart with ones/tens/hundreds columns repeated per period. Students identify a marked digit's place value using the chart, and convert numbers between standard form, word form (الصيغة اللفظية), and expanded form (الصيغة التحليلية) — including real 7-8 digit numbers like Jordan's population or planetary distances.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive place-value chart where dragging digits into columns builds a number live, with each period visually grouped/colored and a toggle that reveals the number simultaneously in standard, word, and expanded form.

##### Lesson 1.2: مقارنة الأعداد وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I compare numbers within the millions, and order them.
- **What's taught:** Students compare multi-digit numbers by stacking them vertically, aligning place values, and scanning digit-by-digit from the left until a difference is found (using >, <, =), then extend this to sorting three or more numbers ascending or descending. Applied to real data (pilgrim counts by year, tourist arrivals, ocean areas).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A digit-by-digit "race" animation that highlights matching leftmost digits as ties and stops/highlights the first differing column that decides the comparison, making the left-to-right scanning rule visible rather than abstract.

##### Lesson 1.3: جمع الأعداد الكلّية وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Whole Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I add or subtract numbers within 7 digits.
- **What's taught:** Vertical addition/subtraction of large numbers using the place-value chart, with explicit regrouping (carrying/borrowing) steps shown per column, plus front-end estimation (rounding each number to its leading digit before adding/subtracting) used both to sanity-check the exact answer and as a real skill in its own right. Applied to profit totals, trade/export data, animal-population counts.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A column-by-column animated addition/subtraction that visibly "carries" or "borrows" a regrouped unit into the next column, synced with a running estimate shown alongside the exact computation so students see both threads at once.

##### Lesson 1.4: الأعداد السالبة — Negative Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize negative numbers, and place them on a number line.
- **Key terms:** العدد السالب (negative number)
- **What's taught:** Introduces negative numbers as representing values below a zero reference point (sea level, ground floor, temperature below freezing), using a vertical number-line model (fish below sea level, a building's basement floors, temperatures). Students read a position as a signed number from a diagram, and place given numbers on a number line, distinguishing negative from positive by their side of zero.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A vertical "sea level" scene (or building floor indicator) where dragging a marker up/down live-updates its signed-number readout, directly linking real-world "below a reference point" situations to negative-number notation.

##### Lesson 1.5: خطة حل المسألة: أنشئ جدولاً — Problem-Solving Strategy: Make a Table
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I solve real-life problems using the "make a table" strategy.
- **What's taught:** A dedicated problem-solving-strategy lesson (Understand/Plan/Solve/Check framework) where students build an explicit table of paired quantities (e.g. green marbles vs. matching red marbles at a fixed ratio, or coin/combination counts) to systematically find a target value or count outcomes, rather than computing directly.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An auto-growing table tool where entering a pattern rule (e.g. "3 red per green") fills in successive rows live, letting students watch the table extend toward the target row instead of manually recomputing each one.

#### Unit 2: الضرب والقسمة — Multiplication and Division

##### Lesson 2.1: الضرب الذهني — Mental Multiplication
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the product of whole numbers mentally using doubling and halving.
- **Key terms:** المضاعفة (doubling), التنصيف (halving)
- **What's taught:** Doubling and halving are introduced as inverse operations that can simplify a multiplication: if one factor is even, halve it and double the other factor repeatedly until the product becomes an easy "basic fact" (e.g. 35×16 → 70×8 → 560). Both doubling and halving of two-digit numbers are also taught via place-value decomposition (splitting into tens+ones and distributing).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "balance" animation showing one factor shrinking by half while the other grows by double, with the product bar staying visually constant in area/length throughout — making concrete why the trick preserves the product.

##### Lesson 2.2: تقدير نواتج الضرب — Estimating Products
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I estimate multiplication products using rounding.
- **Key terms:** الأعداد المتناغمة (compatible numbers)
- **What's taught:** Two estimation strategies: rounding one or both factors to a leading place value before multiplying with basic facts, and choosing "compatible numbers" (nearby numbers that are easy to multiply mentally, e.g. rounding 2491 to 2500 since 2500×4 is easy). Students also reason about whether an estimate is an over- or under-estimate based on rounding direction.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A rounding slider on each factor that shows the estimate updating live alongside the exact product, with a visual "over/under" indicator showing which direction the estimate drifted and why.

##### Lesson 2.3: ضرب الأعداد — Multiplying Whole Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I multiply a whole number by a 1-digit number; I multiply a whole number of up to 3 digits by a 2-digit number.
- **What's taught:** The standard multiplication algorithm, place value column by column (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) with regrouping/carrying, extended to two-digit multipliers (partial products for tens and ones, then summed). Each worked example opens with an estimate for a plausibility check against the exact answer. Applied to real contexts (recycling collection totals, theater seating, distance × trips).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-through algorithm animator that highlights each partial product being computed and placed, then visually sums the shifted partial products — with the pre-computed estimate shown alongside as a running sanity check.

##### Lesson 2.4: تقدير ناتج القسمة — Estimating Quotients
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I estimate quotients using compatible numbers.
- **What's taught:** Estimating a division result by rounding the divisor (and/or dividend) to a compatible pair that divides evenly and mentally (e.g. 418÷82 → 400÷80=5), including choosing between two possible compatible roundings and comparing which is closer to the actual divisor.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual-number-line tool showing the actual divisor and nearby "easy" compatible numbers, letting students drag to pick a rounding and see the resulting quick estimate compute live.

##### Lesson 2.5: قسمة الأعداد — Dividing Whole Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I divide a whole number of up to 3 digits by a number of 1 or 2 digits, using long division; I interpret a remainder.
- **Key terms:** باقي القسمة (remainder)
- **What's taught:** The standard long-division algorithm (estimate the first quotient digit's place, divide, multiply, subtract, bring down, repeat) is taught step by step, always preceded by a compatible-number estimate for a plausibility check, and followed by verifying the answer via multiplication-plus-remainder. A key applied skill is interpreting what a remainder means in context — sometimes it's discarded, sometimes it forces rounding the quotient up by one (e.g. needing one more bus to carry leftover passengers).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated long-division "staircase" that highlights each divide/multiply/subtract/bring-down cycle in sequence, paired with a real-world remainder-interpretation prompt (round up, keep as leftover, or discard) that the student must answer correctly before proceeding to the next problem.

#### Unit 3: خصائص الأعداد — Properties of Numbers

##### Lesson 3.1: قابلية القسمة على 4, 6, 9 — Divisibility by 4, 6, and 9
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I test numbers for divisibility by 4, 6, and 9.
- **Key terms:** قواعد قابلية القسمة (divisibility rules)
- **What's taught:** Building on prior rules for 2, 3, 5, 10, students learn: divisible by 4 if the last two digits (as a two-digit number) are divisible by 4; divisible by 6 if divisible by both 2 and 3 simultaneously; divisible by 9 if the digit sum is divisible by 9. Applied to real equal-sharing contexts (dividing seeds into containers, distributing microscope slides among lab groups) and open-ended digit-completion problems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live checker where typing a number highlights the relevant digits per rule (last two digits for 4, all digits summing for 9, both parity and digit-sum checks for 6) and shows an instant pass/fail per rule.

##### Lesson 3.2: تحليل العدد إلى عوامله الأولية — Prime Factorization
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I factor a number into its prime factors.
- **Key terms:** التحليل إلى العوامل الأولية (prime factorization), شجرة العوامل (factor tree)
- **What's taught:** A composite number is broken into a product of primes via two methods shown side by side on the same number (60=2×2×3×5 either way, just different branch order): the factor tree, splitting into a factor pair repeatedly until every branch is prime; and repeated division, dividing successively by prime factors until reaching 1. Preceded by a concept activity using Venn diagrams to find common factors of two numbers.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive factor tree where tapping a composite node splits it into a chosen factor pair (with immediate feedback if a non-prime pair is chosen for a branch meant to terminate), alongside a toggle showing the same number's repeated-division ladder.

##### Lesson 3.3: العامل المشترك الأكبر — Greatest Common Factor
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the greatest common factor of two numbers.
- **Key terms:** العوامل المشتركة (common factors), العامل المشترك الأكبر (greatest common factor, ع.م.أ)
- **What's taught:** Two methods: listing every factor of each number and picking the largest one they share (reinforced with a Venn-diagram organizer), and — building on the prior lesson — prime-factorizing both numbers and multiplying only the shared prime factors (taking one instance of each matched pair). Applied to real equal-portioning problems (packing dates and water cups into identical boxes, cutting two different-length wood pieces into equal-length segments).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-and-drop Venn diagram where each number's factor list is sorted into overlapping circles, auto-highlighting the shared factors and flagging the largest as the GCF.

##### Lesson 3.4: المضاعف المشترك الأصغر — Least Common Multiple
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find multiples of whole numbers; I find the least common multiple of two numbers.
- **Key terms:** المضاعف (multiple), المضاعفات المشتركة (common multiples), المضاعف المشترك الأصغر (least common multiple, م.م.أ)
- **What's taught:** A multiple is any product of a number with a whole number (excluding zero); students list a number's first several multiples, then find the LCM either by listing both numbers' multiples until the first shared value appears, or by prime-factorizing both and multiplying each prime at its highest occurring power. Real contexts include two interlocking gears realigning at a mark, and stacked boxes of two different heights reaching equal column height.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Animated interlocking gears with different tooth counts that visibly realign at the LCM number of rotations, paired with a dual skip-counting number line highlighting the first shared multiple.

##### Lesson 3.5: مربع العدد والجذر التربيعي — Square of a Number and Square Root
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the square of a number, and the square root of a number.
- **Key terms:** مربع العدد (square of a number), المربع الكامل (perfect square), الجذر التربيعي (square root)
- **What's taught:** Squaring is introduced via a growing dot-pattern (side length increasing 1,2,3,4...), formalized as multiplying a number by itself, with "perfect square" defined for whole-number squares. Square root is the inverse — the number which, multiplied by itself, gives the perfect square — applied to finding a square garden's side length from its area. A multiplication table doubles as a perfect-square/root finder.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive n×n growing tile grid where dragging a side-length slider shows n² tiles building up live, with a reverse mode where entering an area highlights the matching square's side length (its root).

#### Unit 4: الكسور والعمليات عليها — Fractions and Operations on Them

##### Lesson 4.1: الأعداد الكسرية — Mixed Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I write an improper fraction as a mixed number; I write a mixed number as an improper fraction.
- **Key terms:** العدد الكسري (mixed number), الكسر الفعلي (proper fraction), الكسر غير الفعلي (improper fraction)
- **What's taught:** Distinguishes proper fractions (numerator < denominator), improper fractions (numerator ≥ denominator), and mixed numbers (whole + proper fraction). Converting improper→mixed is shown two ways: long division (quotient becomes the whole number, remainder over the original denominator) and marking the value on a number line and counting whole segments. Converting mixed→improper uses multiply-then-add (whole×denominator+numerator, over the same denominator) or repeated addition of whole-number fractions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line tool where dragging a point shows both its improper-fraction and mixed-number label simultaneously updating, plus a "pie stack" model where whole circles combine with a partial circle to visually total the improper fraction.

##### Lesson 4.2: مقارنة الكسور والأعداد الكسرية وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Fractions and Mixed Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I compare fractions and mixed numbers, and order them.
- **What's taught:** For same-denominator fractions, the larger numerator wins; for same-numerator fractions, the smaller denominator wins (fewer, bigger pieces). For unlike fractions, students find the LCM of the denominators, convert both to equivalent fractions with that common denominator, then compare numerators. For mixed numbers, whole-number parts are compared first, with fractional parts as a tiebreaker. Concept activities using colored fraction-bar shading precede the formal rules.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Stacked fraction bars of adjustable denominator that shade to represent each fraction, auto-aligning to a common denominator grid so the size comparison is directly visible before the symbolic answer is shown.

##### Lesson 4.3: جمع الكسور — Adding Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I add unlike fractions; I add unlike mixed numbers.
- **What's taught:** For two fractions where one denominator is a multiple of the other, students convert the smaller-denominator fraction to an equivalent fraction with the larger (shared) denominator, then add straightforwardly. For mixed numbers, fractional parts are converted and added first, then whole-number parts are added, with the final answer simplified. Circle-model diagrams verify each result visually.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual-circle "pie" addition animator where one circle's slices resize to match the other's denominator before the two pie-fills visually combine into the sum.

##### Lesson 4.4: طرح الكسور — Subtracting Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I subtract unlike fractions and unlike mixed numbers, where one denominator is a multiple of the other.
- **What's taught:** Mirrors the addition lesson's method — rewrite one fraction using an equivalent fraction sharing the other's denominator, then subtract like fractions (and whole-number parts separately for mixed numbers), simplifying the result.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "take away" pie-slice animator where slices of the resized fraction are removed one by one from the larger pie, visually landing on the difference.

##### Lesson 4.5: ضرب الكسور — Multiplying Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the product of fractions in simplest form.
- **What's taught:** Multiply numerators together and denominators together, discovered via a concept activity beforehand; students learn to simplify either fraction first (canceling a shared factor between a numerator and the other fraction's denominator) before multiplying, to keep numbers small, then simplify the final product. Applied to real proportional-quantity contexts (fraction of a watermelon's mass that's water, scaling a glue recipe, splitting land between two crop types).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An overlapping-rectangle area model where one fraction's shaded rows cross another's shaded columns, and the doubly-shaded overlap region visually is the product — directly showing why numerators and denominators multiply.

##### Lesson 4.6: قسمة الكسور — Dividing Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I divide a fraction by a fraction.
- **Key terms:** المقلوب (reciprocal)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity dividing fractions using shaded models (asking "how many sixths fit in two-thirds?"), which is then connected to the "invert and multiply" shortcut: two numbers are reciprocals if their product is 1, and dividing by a fraction equals multiplying by its reciprocal.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "how many fit" tiling animation where a divisor fraction's unit is repeatedly laid inside the dividend's shaded region, counting how many copies fit (including partial copies) to build the division-as-measurement intuition before showing the invert-and-multiply shortcut as a faster equivalent.

#### Unit 5: تمثيل البيانات وتفسيرها — Representing and Interpreting Data

##### Lesson 5.1: السؤال الإحصائي — The Statistical Question
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I distinguish the statistical question.
- **Key terms:** السؤال الإحصائي (statistical question), السؤال غير الإحصائي (non-statistical question)
- **What's taught:** A statistical question is one that produces varied answers across different people (e.g. "how do you get to school?"); a non-statistical question has one fixed answer for everyone (e.g. "does Earth orbit the Sun?"). Students classify given questions, write their own statistical and non-statistical questions about a scenario (zoo visitors, sports fans), and distinguish subtly similar question pairs (e.g. "how many trees in the school garden" vs. "how many trees in your home garden" — one is fixed once posed to a specific place, one varies by respondent).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sorting activity where sample questions are dragged into "statistical" vs. "not statistical" bins with instant feedback and a reason shown; a simulated set of virtual respondents that "answer" a typed-in question live to reveal whether responses vary.

##### Lesson 5.2: المستوى الإحداثي — The Coordinate Plane
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I read points in the coordinate plane, and plot them.
- **Key terms:** المستوى الإحداثي (coordinate plane), المحور x / المحور y (x-axis / y-axis), نقطة الأصل (origin), زوج مرتب (ordered pair)
- **What's taught:** First-quadrant-only coordinate plane: two perpendicular numbered axes meeting at the origin (0,0); any point corresponds to an ordered pair (x,y) reached by moving right along x then up along y from the origin. Students read coordinates from labeled points (including reading a map-style scene — lake, forest, hospital, house), plot given ordered pairs, and are cautioned that order matters (x always comes before y).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive grid where clicking places a point and its (x,y) label appears live, or dragging an existing point updates its coordinate readout in real time; a clickable "map" where landmarks reveal their coordinates on click.

##### Lesson 5.3: التمثيل بالخطوط — Line Graph Representation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I represent data with line graphs, then read and interpret them.
- **Key terms:** التمثيل بالخطوط (line graph)
- **What's taught:** A line graph shows how data changes over time: time goes on the horizontal axis, the measured quantity on the vertical axis, one point per time period, points connected by straight segments. Students build a graph from a data table (child's height by age, population by year, temperature over a week, plant growth, rainfall by month) and read it back — identifying max/min, describing change between two points, comparing specific readings. A "jagged break" convention for an axis that doesn't start near zero is introduced.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-to-plot line-graph builder where placing points from a data table auto-draws the connecting line; a playback animation that "draws" the line left to right over time with a readout marker showing the exact value at any point along it.

##### Lesson 5.4: التمثيل بالأعمدة المزدوجة — Double Bar Graph Representation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I represent two sets of data with double bars, then read and interpret them.
- **Key terms:** التمثيل بالأعمدة المزدوجة (double bar graph)
- **What's taught:** Extends single bar graphs to represent two related data sets side by side per category (e.g. favorite hobbies of 4th vs. 5th graders, homework minutes on two different days) for direct comparison: choose a scale spanning both data sets, draw two adjacent differently-colored bars per category, add a legend. Students read values from both bars in a category, identify which is higher/lower, and sum values across categories.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive builder where assigning two data series to categories renders paired colored bars with a live legend, and hovering/clicking a category surfaces the numeric gap between its two bars.

### Semester 2

#### Unit 6: الكسور العشرية والنسبة المئوية — Decimals and Percentage

##### Lesson 6.1: أجزاء الألف — Thousandths
- **Objective:** I determine the place value of a digit in a decimal within the thousandths; I write decimals in standard, word, and expanded form.
- **What's taught:** Extends the place-value chart rightward past hundredths to thousandths, with the decimal point sitting between ones and tenths. Students identify a marked digit's value and convert between standard/word/expanded forms for numbers with up to three decimal places.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An extended place-value chart where dragging digits past the decimal point live-builds the number and shows all three representations simultaneously.

##### Lesson 6.2: التحويل بين الكسور والأعداد العشرية — Converting Between Fractions and Decimals
- **Objective:** I convert fractions to decimals and decimals to fractions.
- **What's taught:** Fraction→decimal uses either scaling to an equivalent fraction with denominator 10/100/1000, or long division. Decimal→fraction writes the decimal directly over the matching power of ten and simplifies. Applied to real mixed-unit contexts (flag dimensions, alloy composition, stadium seating).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live dual-display where typing a fraction shows its long-division steps animate into a decimal, and vice versa, with a simplification step highlighted.

##### Lesson 6.3: مقارنة الأعداد العشرية وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Decimals
- **Objective:** I compare decimals and order them.
- **What's taught:** Extends whole-number left-to-right digit comparison to decimals, using the place-value chart or a number line, including comparing decimals with different numbers of decimal places (padding with trailing zeros conceptually).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A zoomable number line that lets students place two close decimals and visually see which is further right (larger).

##### Lesson 6.4: تقريب الأعداد العشرية — Rounding Decimals
- **Objective:** I round decimals to a given place value.
- **What's taught:** Standard rounding rule (look at the digit to the right; 5+ rounds up) applied to tenths/hundredths/whole numbers, in real contexts like chemical composition percentages.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider on a zoomed number line showing a decimal's exact position between two rounding targets, snapping to whichever it's closer to.

##### Lesson 6.5: تقدير نواتج جمع الأعداد العشرية وطرحها — Estimating Sums and Differences of Decimals
- **Objective:** I estimate sums and differences of decimals by rounding.
- **What's taught:** Round each decimal to a convenient place value (usually whole numbers) before adding/subtracting mentally, used for plausibility-checking exact answers, in contexts like Earth's rotation speed.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A rounding slider on each addend showing the estimate updating live alongside the exact sum.

##### Lesson 6.6: جمع الأعداد العشرية وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Decimals
- **Objective:** I add and subtract decimals.
- **What's taught:** Vertical addition/subtraction aligning decimal points (padding with zeros so both numbers have equal decimal places), carrying/borrowing as with whole numbers, decimal point dropped straight down into the answer. Applied to melting-point differences and similar comparisons.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A column-aligned animator that snaps two decimals' points into alignment before stepping through the addition/subtraction, visually padding missing decimal places with zero-tiles.

##### Lesson 6.7: ضرب الأعداد العشرية وقسمتها على 10, 100, 1000 — Multiplying and Dividing Decimals by 10, 100, 1000
- **Objective:** I multiply and divide decimals by 10, 100, and 1000.
- **What's taught:** The decimal point shifts right (multiplying) or left (dividing) by as many places as there are zeros in the power of ten, adding zeros as placeholders when digits run out. Applied to unit-price and packaging contexts (bottles per box).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated decimal point that visibly "jumps" the correct number of places across a digit strip as the power of ten is dialed in.

##### Lesson 6.8: النسبة المئوية — Percentage
- **Objective:** I recognize percentage, and solve problems involving it.
- **Key terms:** النسبة المئوية (percentage)
- **What's taught:** A percentage is a fraction out of 100, written with the % symbol. Students convert a shaded portion of a 100-grid to a percentage and back, and apply percentages to real proportions (test scores out of 100, sport preferences, crop composition, gender ratios).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A 100-cell grid where clicking cells shades them and the percentage/fraction/decimal readout updates live in all three forms simultaneously.

#### Unit 7: المقادير الجبرية والمعادلات — Algebraic Expressions and Equations

##### Lesson 7.1: إيجاد قيمة المقدار الجبري — Evaluating an Algebraic Expression
- **Objective:** I write an algebraic expression using symbols, and find its value.
- **Key terms:** التعويض (substitution)
- **What's taught:** An algebraic expression (e.g. x−2, 2x) stands for an unknown quantity; substituting a given value for the variable evaluates it, modeled physically with algebra tiles (unit squares + a variable rectangle). Students translate word phrases into expressions and back, and apply this to real quantity-tracking contexts (calorie totals, ages, running totals during a pandemic case count).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider on the variable's value that live-updates the expression's computed result and a matching tile-count animation.

##### Lesson 7.2: معادلات الجمع والطرح — Addition and Subtraction Equations
- **Objective:** I solve addition and subtraction equations.
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity using an "equation mat" and algebra tiles to physically balance both sides. Equations like x+4=9 or x−11=7 are solved via mental math (fact families) or the inverse addition/subtraction relationship, always checked by substitution. Real contexts: donated toys, leftover ribbon length, river-length comparisons.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A balance-scale/equation-mat animator where removing or adding the same number of unit tiles from both sides visually isolates the variable.

##### Lesson 7.3: معادلات الضرب والقسمة — Multiplication and Division Equations
- **Objective:** I write and solve an equation involving multiplication or division.
- **What's taught:** Mirrors lesson 7.2 for equations like 8x=32 or x÷10=4, solved via mental math or the inverse multiplication/division relationship, with the equation-mat/algebra-tile model (grouping unit squares under each variable tile) as the concept bridge. Applied to production-rate and equal-distribution contexts (candy packaging, computer lab allocation).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive equation mat where dragging to "split" the variable-side tiles into equal groups matching the constant side visually solves for x.

##### Lesson 7.4: خطة حل المسألة: أرسم نموذجًا — Problem-Solving Strategy: Draw a Model
- **What's taught:** A dedicated problem-solving-strategy lesson (Understand/Plan/Solve/Check) where students draw a bar/tape-diagram model of a word problem (e.g. two people's donation counts, one a known amount more/less than the other) to derive the equation, rather than jumping straight to symbols.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-to-build tape-diagram tool where students construct bar segments representing each quantity and their relationship, which then auto-generates the corresponding equation.

#### Unit 8: الأشكال الهندسية والتحويلات — Geometric Shapes and Transformations

##### Lesson 8.1: مجموع الزوايا على مستقيم وحول نقطة — Sum of Angles on a Line and Around a Point
- **Objective:** I recognize that angles on a straight line sum to 180° and angles around a point sum to 360°.
- **What's taught:** Angles forming a straight line always sum to 180°; angles sharing a vertex and forming a full turn sum to 360°. Students solve for an unknown angle given the others (using the addition/subtraction relationship), and classify whether a given set of angle measures could represent angles on a line or around a point.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-angle diagram on a line or around a point where one angle's measure updates live and the remaining unknown angle recalculates automatically, visually reinforcing the fixed-sum constraint.

##### Lesson 8.2: المضلَّعات — Polygons
- **Objective:** I recognize a polygon and a regular polygon.
- **Key terms:** المضلَّع (polygon), المضلَّع المنتظم (regular polygon)
- **What's taught:** A polygon is a closed 2D shape with 3+ non-crossing straight sides. Students sort shapes into polygon/non-polygon, name polygons by side count, and distinguish regular (all sides and angles congruent) from irregular polygons.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sorting game dragging shapes into polygon/non-polygon bins with an explanation of which rule was broken (curved side, open shape, crossing sides).

##### Lesson 8.3: تصنيف المثلَّثات حسب أطوال أضلاعها — Classifying Triangles by Side Length
- **Objective:** I classify triangles by their side lengths.
- **Key terms:** متطابق الأضلاع (equilateral), متطابق الضلعين (isosceles), مختلف الأضلاع (scalene)
- **What's taught:** Triangles are sorted into equilateral (all 3 sides congruent), isosceles (2 sides congruent), and scalene (no sides congruent) based on given side-length markings or measurements.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex triangle where side lengths are shown live and the triangle auto-labels itself equilateral/isosceles/scalene as vertices are moved.

##### Lesson 8.4: تصنيف المثلَّثات حسب قياسات زواياها — Classifying Triangles by Angle Measure
- **Objective:** I classify triangles by their angle measures.
- **Key terms:** حاد الزوايا (acute), قائم الزاوية (right), منفرج الزاوية (obtuse)
- **What's taught:** Every triangle has at least two acute angles; the third angle determines its class (acute, right, or obtuse). When one angle is unknown, students first use the 180° angle-sum (from lesson 8.1's underlying fact, introduced via a concept activity) to find it, then classify.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex triangle with live angle readouts that auto-classifies as acute/right/obtuse, letting students drag toward each boundary case to feel where classification flips.

##### Lesson 8.5: تصنيف الأشكال الرباعية — Classifying Quadrilaterals
- **Objective:** I classify quadrilaterals by their sides and angles.
- **Key terms:** شبه المنحرف (trapezoid), متوازي الأضلاع (parallelogram), المستطيل (rectangle), المعين (rhombus), المربع (square)
- **What's taught:** Quadrilaterals are classified hierarchically by side/angle properties: trapezoid (exactly one pair of parallel sides), parallelogram (both pairs parallel and congruent), rectangle (parallelogram with right angles), rhombus (parallelogram with all sides congruent), square (both). Students classify shapes into every applicable category (not just one), and evaluate true/false relationship statements (e.g. "every rhombus is a rectangle" — false) using a hierarchy diagram/Venn diagram.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex quadrilateral that live-labels itself with every applicable category as its shape changes, plus an interactive nested Venn/hierarchy diagram that highlights which region a dragged shape currently falls into.

##### Lesson 8.6: الانسحاب — Translation
- **Objective:** I recognize translation of a shape.
- **Key terms:** الانسحاب (translation)
- **What's taught:** Translation moves every point of a shape the same direction and distance while preserving side lengths and angle measures, without rotating it — demonstrated by sliding a rectangle's vertices a fixed number of grid squares and confirming the image is congruent to the original.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A grid-based drag tool where sliding a shape shows its image tracing behind it in real time, with side-length and angle readouts confirming they stay unchanged throughout the slide.

##### Lesson 8.7: المنشور والهرم — Prism and Pyramid
- **Objective:** I recognize prisms and pyramids and their nets.
- **Key terms:** الشكل ثلاثي الأبعاد (3D shape), المنشور (prism), الهرم (pyramid), الوجه (face), الحرف (edge), الرأس (vertex), الشبكة (net)
- **What's taught:** A prism has two parallel congruent polygonal bases connected by rectangular side faces, named by its base shape; a pyramid has one base with triangular side faces meeting at a single apex point, also named by its base. Students count faces/edges/vertices for given solids, and identify which solid results from folding a given 2D net (or which net is invalid for forming any closed solid).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated fold/unfold tool where a 3D prism or pyramid unfolds into its flat net and refolds on demand, with face/edge/vertex counts tallied live as the student clicks each part.

#### Unit 9: القياس — Measurement

##### Lesson 9.1: وحدات قياس الكتلة — Units of Mass
- **Objective:** I recognize compound mass units, and convert between them.
- **Key terms:** الوحدة المركَّبة (compound unit)
- **What's taught:** Grams, kilograms, and tonnes relate by factors of 1000 (×1000 going down, ÷1000 going up). Students convert between units, and handle "compound" mixed-unit measurements (e.g. 2 kg, 84 g) by converting to a single unit before comparing, adding, or ranking masses — applied to comparing twin birth weights and truck cargo loads.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A digital scale readout that toggles between single-unit and compound-unit display live as a slider changes the measured mass, reinforcing the conversion factor visually.

##### Lesson 9.2: وحدات قياس السعة والطول — Units of Capacity and Length
- **Objective:** I convert between units of capacity and length, and solve problems with compound units.
- **What's taught:** mL↔L (×1000/÷1000) and the km/m/cm/mm chain (×1000/×100/×10 stepping down) are converted and combined as compound measurements (e.g. 7 m, 56 cm), applied to real perimeter problems (fencing a garden) requiring unit unification before computing.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A unit-ladder slider (km→m→cm→mm) where dragging a value up or down the ladder animates the decimal point shifting by the correct power of ten at each step.

##### Lesson 9.3: الزمن — Time
- **Objective:** I calculate time using its different units.
- **Key terms:** توقيت 24 ساعة (24-hour time)
- **What's taught:** Year/day/hour/minute/second relate via ×365/×24/×60/×60 (and reverse), used to express a duration in compound units (e.g. 88 hours = 3 days, 16 hours, found via long division with the remainder as leftover hours) and to compute event end-times (e.g. a football match's total duration including halves and break).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive digital/analog clock-and-calendar combo where dragging an elapsed-time slider live-converts the duration into compound day/hour/minute form.

##### Lesson 9.4: محيط الشكل المركَّب ومساحته — Perimeter and Area of a Compound Shape
- **Objective:** I find the perimeter and area of compound shapes.
- **Key terms:** الشكل المركَّب (compound shape)
- **What's taught:** A compound shape results from combining or cutting rectangles/squares. Perimeter of an L-shaped cut corner equals the original rectangle's perimeter (a non-obvious shortcut shown visually). Area is found either by decomposing the shape into rectangles/squares and summing their areas, or by subtracting a "missing" rectangular region from a larger enclosing rectangle — both methods demonstrated on the same shape for comparison.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive compound-shape tool where dragging a dividing line splits the shape into named sub-rectangles with live individual and running-total area labels, toggleable against a "complete the rectangle and subtract" overlay mode.

#### Unit 10: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

##### Lesson 10.1: الوسط الحسابي — The Mean
- **Objective:** I calculate the mean of single-value data.
- **Key terms:** الوسط الحسابي/المعدَّل (mean)
- **What's taught:** Mean = sum of values ÷ count of values. Students compute it for raw data lists and for frequency-table data (multiplying each value by its frequency before summing), applied to test scores, shoe sizes, newborn masses.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable set of value-tiles on a number line where the mean is shown as a computed marker that recalculates live as tiles are added, removed, or moved.

##### Lesson 10.2: الوسيط والمنوال — The Median and Mode
- **Objective:** I calculate the median and mode of single-value data.
- **Key terms:** الوسيط (median), المنوال (mode)
- **What's taught:** Median found by sorting data and crossing off from both ends toward the middle (averaging the two middle values if the count is even); mode is the most frequent value(s) — a data set can have one, several, or no mode. Real contexts: contest participant ages, favorite colors/sports (no numeric mean possible, so mode is the only usable measure).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sortable row of data tiles where clicking "find median" animates pairs being crossed off simultaneously from each end until the middle is reached; a frequency-count overlay that highlights the tallest stack(s) as the mode.

##### Lesson 10.3: المدى — The Range
- **Objective:** I find the range of single data and of data represented graphically or in a frequency table.
- **Key terms:** المدى (range)
- **What's taught:** Range = largest value − smallest value, describing how spread out data is. Applied to weekly temperature swings, farm production variation, COVID case counts, and reading range directly off a bar graph or frequency table.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line span tool where dragging endpoints to the data's min and max shows the range as the highlighted interval length, live-updating as data changes.

##### Lesson 10.4: فرص الحدوث — Chances of Occurrence
- **Objective:** I distinguish between random-experiment outcomes based on whether their chances of occurring are equal or unequal.
- **Key terms:** الحادث (event/outcome), فرص متساوية (equal chances), فرص غير متساوية (unequal chances)
- **What's taught:** Precursor to formal probability: outcomes of a random experiment (dice roll, spinner, drawing from a bag/collection) have equal chance if their share of the total is equal (e.g. a fair die's 6 equally-sized faces) and unequal chance otherwise (e.g. a bag with more red candies than others). Students rank outcomes by likelihood without computing numeric probabilities yet.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A configurable spinner/bag builder where changing the proportion of each color/outcome instantly shows (via repeated random draws) whether outcomes feel equally or unequally likely, bridging into formal probability.

## Grade 6 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

### Semester 1

#### Unit 1: الأعداد الصحيحة والعمليات عليها — Integers and Operations on Them

##### Lesson 1.1: الأعداد الصحيحة والقيمة المطلقة — Integers and Absolute Value
- **Objective:** I distinguish integers and their opposites; I find the absolute value of an integer.
- **Key terms:** عدد صحيح موجب/سالب (positive/negative integer), معكوس (opposite), القيمة المطلقة (absolute value)
- **What's taught:** Integers extend whole numbers below zero (floors below ground, temperatures below freezing). Two numbers are opposites if equidistant from zero on opposite sides (zero is its own opposite, neither positive nor negative). Absolute value is the distance from zero, always non-negative, applied to real vertical-distance problems (bird above vs. fish below sea level, summed via |a|+|b|).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A vertical or horizontal draggable number-line marker showing live distance-from-zero as its absolute value, with a mirrored "opposite" marker that moves symmetrically on the other side.

##### Lesson 1.2: مقارنة الأعداد الصحيحة وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Integers
- **Objective:** I compare integers and order them.
- **What's taught:** On a number line, values increase rightward/upward and decrease leftward/downward, so positive integers are always greater than negative ones; among negatives, the one closer to zero is greater. Applied to comparing burial/hole depths and similar below-ground measurements.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A vertical number line with draggable depth markers that auto-compare and display which is "deeper" (more negative) in real time.

##### Lesson 1.3: جمع الأعداد الصحيحة — Adding Integers
- **Objective:** I add two integers; I solve real-life addition problems.
- **Key terms:** النظير الجمعي (additive inverse)
- **What's taught:** Same-sign addition sums the absolute values and keeps the shared sign; different-sign addition subtracts the smaller absolute value from the larger and takes the sign of the larger-magnitude number. A number plus its additive inverse (opposite) equals zero. Every result is verified by tracing hops on a number line. Real contexts: net position after running forward/backward, diving then ascending toward the surface.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated number-line "hop" tool chaining two colored arrows (one per addend) tip-to-tail, landing exactly on the sum, with same-sign hops visually reinforcing and opposite-sign hops visually canceling.

##### Lesson 1.4: طرح الأعداد الصحيحة — Subtracting Integers
- **Objective:** I subtract two integers.
- **What's taught:** Subtraction is redefined as "add the opposite" (a−b = a+(−b)), covering subtracting a positive (moves left the amount) and subtracting a negative (moves right, i.e. subtracting a negative increases the value). Preceded by a counter-tile concept activity linking removal-based subtraction to the add-the-opposite rule. Applied to temperature differences between Earth and Mars, crane-lowering distances, and archaeological depth problems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line tool where "subtracting b" visibly flips into "adding −b" as an animated direction-reversal, making the rule's mechanism (not just the shortcut) visible.

##### Lesson 1.5: ضرب الأعداد الصحيحة وقسمتها — Multiplying and Dividing Integers
- **Objective:** I multiply and divide integers.
- **What's taught:** Multiplication is introduced as repeated addition (3×(−2) = (−2)+(−2)+(−2) = −6), modeled with counter tiles and number-line hops, then generalized to sign rules for both multiplication and division (same signs → positive result, different signs → negative result). Applied to real constant-rate contexts (submarine descent rate × time).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A repeated-hop number-line animator showing identical negative jumps stacking to build the product visually, plus a sign-rule "combination lock" quiz pairing +/− factors to predict the result's sign before revealing it.

#### Unit 2: الكسور والعمليات عليها — Fractions and Operations on Them

##### Lesson 2.1: جمع الكسور وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Fractions
- **Objective:** I find the sum/difference of fractions in simplest form.
- **What's taught:** For unlike fractions, find the LCM of both denominators, rewrite each fraction with that common denominator (scaling numerator and denominator together), then add/subtract numerators and simplify — extending beyond grade 5's "one denominator is a multiple of the other" restriction to any two denominators. Applied to comparing planetary diameters as fractions of Earth's, and measuring-cup recipe adjustments.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual fraction-bar model where both bars re-partition live to a shared common-denominator grid before the shaded regions combine/subtract.

##### Lesson 2.2: جمع الأعداد الكسرية وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Mixed Numbers
- **Objective:** I find the sum/difference of mixed numbers.
- **What's taught:** Extends 2.1 to mixed numbers: convert fractional parts to a common denominator, add/subtract fractional and whole-number parts separately (regrouping when a fractional subtraction would go negative), simplify. Real contexts likely include measurement and scheduling problems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A stacked whole-circle + fraction-bar model where regrouping (borrowing a whole as extra fraction parts) is animated explicitly when subtracting a larger fractional part from a smaller one.

##### Lesson 2.3: ضرب الكسور والأعداد الكسرية — Multiplying Fractions and Mixed Numbers
- **Objective:** I find the product of fractions and mixed numbers in simplest form.
- **What's taught:** Multiply numerators and denominators directly for fractions; convert mixed numbers to improper fractions first, then multiply and simplify (canceling common factors before multiplying where possible). Applied to comparing mountain-peak heights as a fraction of another peak's height.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An overlapping area-model grid (rows × columns) generalized to mixed numbers by splitting the grid into whole-part and fraction-part blocks.

##### Lesson 2.4: قسمة الكسور والأعداد الكسرية — Dividing Fractions and Mixed Numbers
- **Objective:** I find the quotient of fractions and mixed numbers in simplest form.
- **What's taught:** Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions, then divide by multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor, simplifying the result. Applied to rate problems (train speed in km per minute, solving for time to cover a given distance).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "how many fit" measurement animation extended to mixed-number divisors, alongside the invert-and-multiply shortcut shown as an equivalent fast path.

#### Unit 3: العمليات على الأعداد العشرية — Operations on Decimals

##### Lesson 3.1: ضرب الأعداد العشرية — Multiplying Decimals
- **Objective:** I multiply decimals.
- **What's taught:** Multiply as if both numbers were whole numbers, then place the decimal point so the result has as many decimal places as the two factors combined (padding with a leading zero if needed). A second method factors a multiple of 10/100/1000 out of one factor first. Also covers evaluating algebraic expressions (3x) by substituting a decimal for x. Applied to tree-growth-rate and cycling-speed problems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live decimal-point placement animator: multiply the digit strings as whole numbers, then watch the point "count in" from the right by the combined decimal-place total.

##### Lesson 3.2: قسمة الأعداد العشرية — Dividing Decimals
- **Objective:** I divide decimals.
- **What's taught:** Dividing decimal quantities (e.g. total sales ÷ ticket price), likely via shifting both dividend and divisor's decimal points to clear the divisor to a whole number, then standard long division.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated decimal-point "shift" on both numbers simultaneously before the long-division staircase proceeds normally.

##### Lesson 3.3: حل مسائل تتضمن وحدات قياس مختلفة — Solving Problems with Different Measurement Units
- **Objective:** I solve problems requiring decimal operations on mixed measurement units.
- **What's taught:** Before computing, unify all quantities to one unit using known conversion relationships (mL↔L, etc.), then simplify/cancel the shared unit, applied to paint-coverage-per-box calculations.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A unit-conversion "funnel" where dragging mismatched-unit quantities into a shared-unit workspace auto-converts them before the calculation proceeds.

##### Lesson 3.4: خطة حل المسألة: حل مسألة أبسط — Problem-Solving Strategy: Solve a Simpler Problem
- **What's taught:** A problem-solving-strategy lesson: replace a decimal-heavy word problem with a simplified version using rounder numbers, solve that, then apply the same method to the original, checking via the inverse operation (multiplication checks division) and via estimation. Applied to scaling a distance-traveled rate by a decimal number of hours.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A side-by-side "simplified vs. original" problem pair where adjusting the simplified numbers' roundness live-previews how the solving method transfers to the real problem.

#### Unit 4: التحويلات والإنشاءات الهندسية — Geometric Transformations and Constructions

##### Lesson 4.1: المستوى الإحداثي — The Coordinate Plane
- **Objective:** I recognize the coordinate plane, and locate points on all four quadrants.
- **Key terms:** الربع (quadrant), نقطة الأصل (origin), الأرباع الأربعة (four quadrants)
- **What's taught:** Extends grade 5's first-quadrant-only plane to all four quadrants (numbered counterclockwise from top-right), with negative coordinates now meaningful. Students read a point's coordinates and identify its quadrant (or axis, if a coordinate is zero) from a diagram, and plot given ordered pairs by moving from the origin, applied to a zoo map and city-location contexts.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A full four-quadrant grid where clicking anywhere drops a point with live coordinate and quadrant-name readout, reinforcing the counterclockwise quadrant-numbering convention.

##### Lesson 4.2: الانسحاب في المستوى الإحداثي — Translation in the Coordinate Plane
- **Objective:** I draw a translation in the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** الانسحاب (translation), الصورة (image)
- **What's taught:** A translation slides every point of a shape the same horizontal and vertical amount without rotating, producing a congruent image. Students describe a given translation from a diagram, and execute one by moving each vertex a stated number of units right/left and up/down, both by hand and via coordinates ((x,y)→(x+a, y+b)).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A grid-based drag tool with separate horizontal/vertical sliders (a, b) that live-translates a shape and updates each vertex's new coordinates simultaneously.

##### Lesson 4.3: الانعكاس في المستوى الإحداثي — Reflection in the Coordinate Plane
- **Objective:** I draw a reflection in the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** الانعكاس (reflection)
- **What's taught:** A reflection flips a shape across a line (axis), producing a mirror image; students identify which of several tiles represents a horizontal reflection of a given one, then reflect shapes across the x-axis or y-axis in the coordinate plane.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable mirror-line tool where dragging a shape's vertex shows its reflected image update in real time on the opposite side of the line, with coordinate labels confirming the sign-flip rule.

##### Lesson 4.4: علاقة القطر بنصف القطر — Relationship Between Diameter and Radius
- **Objective:** I recognize the relationship between a circle's diameter and radius.
- **Key terms:** نصف القطر (radius), القطر (diameter), المركز (center)
- **What's taught:** Basic circle vocabulary (center, radius, diameter) and the fixed relationship diameter = 2×radius, explored via a circular garden/fountain context (equal distance from center to any point on the circle).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable point on a circle's edge with a live radius line from the center, plus a diameter line spanning through the center, showing the 2:1 length relationship update as the circle is resized.

##### Lesson 4.5: الإنشاءات الهندسية — Geometric Constructions
- **Objective:** I perform geometric constructions using a straightedge and compass.
- **Key terms:** الإنشاءات الهندسية (geometric constructions)
- **What's taught:** Constructions use only an unmarked straightedge and compass (no ruler measurement, no protractor) — e.g. constructing a perpendicular bisector of a given segment, and other basic constructions (likely including angle bisection or copying a segment/angle), built from arcs and intersecting circles rather than measured drawing.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A simulated compass-and-straightedge tool where dragging the compass width and swinging arcs from two points shows their intersection defining the perpendicular bisector live, demystifying why the classical construction works.

### Semester 2

#### Unit 5: المقادير الجبرية والمعادلات — Algebraic Expressions and Equations

##### Lesson 5.1: القوى والأسس — Powers and Exponents
- **Objective:** I use powers and exponents to write multiplication expressions.
- **Key terms:** الأس (exponent), الأساس (base), القوة (power), التربيع (squared), التكعيب (cubed)
- **What's taught:** Repeated multiplication of a number by itself is written compactly with an exponent (base^exponent = "power"); students convert between exponential form and standard (expanded product) form, read powers aloud ("seven squared," "seven cubed"), and note that a number with no visible exponent is implicitly to the power 1. Applies to variables too (j×j×j×j = j⁴).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive "stack builder" where dragging repeated copies of a factor into a stack auto-generates the exponential notation and computes the standard-form value live.

##### Lesson 5.2: الجذر التربيعي والجذر التكعيبي — Square Root and Cube Root
- **Objective:** I find square roots and cube roots of numbers.
- **Key terms:** الجذر التربيعي (square root), الجذر التكعيبي (cube root)
- **What's taught:** The square root of a perfect square is the whole number that, multiplied by itself, gives it (√9=3 since 3×3=9); the cube root of a perfect cube is the number multiplied by itself three times to give it (∛8=2 since 2×2×2=8). Applied to finding a square game board's side length from its area.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual grid/cube-stack model — an n×n tile grid for square roots and an n×n×n stacked-cube model for cube roots — where entering the area/volume highlights the matching root visually.

##### Lesson 5.3: أولويات العمليات الحسابية — Order of Operations
- **Objective:** I use order of operations to evaluate numerical expressions.
- **What's taught:** A strict priority order — parentheses first, then all exponents and roots, then multiplication/division left-to-right, then addition/subtraction left-to-right — applied to expressions mixing all of these, including expressions representing shaded-region areas written with exponents.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-through expression evaluator highlighting which sub-expression resolves at each stage, letting students predict the next step before it's revealed.

##### Lesson 5.4: الخصائص الجبرية — Algebraic Properties
- **Objective:** I use algebraic properties to simplify expressions.
- **Key terms:** الخاصية التبديلية (commutative property), الخاصية التجميعية (associative property), خاصية التوزيع (distributive property)
- **What's taught:** The commutative property (order doesn't matter for +/×), associative property (grouping doesn't matter for +/×), and distributive property (a(b+c)=ab+ac) are used to simplify and rewrite algebraic expressions, building toward the two-step-equation concept activity that follows.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-and-regroup tile interface where reordering or regrouping terms visually demonstrates that the total stays the same, and a distributive "unfold" animation splitting a(b+c) into ab+ac.

##### Lesson 5.5: المعادلات — Equations
- **Objective:** I verify whether a given value solves an equation; I solve equations.
- **What's taught:** An equation's solution is a variable value that makes both sides equal, verified by substitution. Building on a concept activity for two-operation equations (using properties from 5.4), students solve equations requiring more than one step, isolating the variable via inverse operations.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step equation solver showing each inverse-operation move applied to both sides simultaneously, with a live substitution check confirming the final answer.

##### Lesson 5.6: المتتاليات — Sequences
- **Objective:** I extend a sequence's terms following its rule.
- **Key terms:** المتتالية (sequence), الحد (term)
- **What's taught:** A sequence is an ordered list of numbers following a rule linking consecutive terms; students find that rule (constant addition/subtraction, shown via a growing geometric dot/shape pattern) and extend the sequence to find further terms — without yet deriving a general algebraic nth-term formula (that comes in grade 7).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive growing-pattern visual synced to a term list, where dragging a "next term" control extends both the visual pattern and the numeric sequence together.

#### Unit 6: النسبة والنسبة المئوية — Ratio and Percentage

##### Lesson 6.1: النسبة — Ratio
- **Objective:** I write and simplify a ratio.
- **Key terms:** النسبة (ratio)
- **What's taught:** A ratio compares one quantity to another, written three equivalent ways (a:b, a/b, "a to b"); simplified like a fraction by dividing both terms by their GCF. Applied to a juice-concentrate-to-water mixing scenario.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A mixing-jar visual where dragging concentrate/water amounts live-displays and simplifies the resulting ratio in all three notations.

##### Lesson 6.2: النسب المتكافئة — Equivalent Ratios
- **Objective:** I find ratios equivalent to a given ratio.
- **Key terms:** النسب المتكافئة (equivalent ratios)
- **What's taught:** Equivalent ratios describe the same relationship between two quantities and can be written as equivalent fractions (scaling both terms by the same factor). Applied to comparing two paint-mixing recipes to determine if they produce the same color.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A side-by-side color-mixing simulator where two ratio recipes render as actual mixed colors, visually confirming or refuting equivalence.

##### Lesson 6.3: النسبة المئوية والكسور العادية — Percentage and Common Fractions
- **Objective:** I convert between percentages and common fractions.
- **What's taught:** Percentages convert to fractions over 100 (then simplified) and fractions convert to percentages by finding an equivalent fraction with denominator 100 or via division.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A 100-grid shading tool where shading cells to match a fraction simultaneously displays the equivalent percentage.

##### Lesson 6.4: النسبة المئوية والكسور العشرية — Percentage and Decimal Fractions
- **Objective:** I convert between percentages and decimals.
- **What's taught:** Percentages convert to decimals by dividing by 100 (shifting the decimal point two places); decimals convert to percentages by multiplying by 100.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated decimal-point shift synced with a percentage readout, showing both representations update together as a slider changes.

##### Lesson 6.5: النسبة المئوية من عدد — Percentage of a Number
- **Objective:** I find a percentage of a number.
- **What's taught:** Convert the percentage to a fraction or decimal, then multiply by the given number — applied to real discount-pricing problems (e.g. finding a bicycle's price after a percentage-off deduction).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A price-tag calculator where dragging a discount-percentage slider live-recomputes and displays the discount amount and final price.

#### Unit 7: الهندسة والقياس — Geometry and Measurement

##### Lesson 7.1: الأشكال الرباعية — Quadrilaterals
- **Objective:** I use the fact that a quadrilateral's angles sum to 360° to find an unknown angle.
- **What's taught:** Building on grade 5's quadrilateral classification, students now use the interior-angle-sum property (360°) to solve for a missing angle given the other three, applied to a real four-legged table/drafting-board shape.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex quadrilateral with live angle readouts summing to a fixed 360°, so changing one angle visibly forces the others to compensate.

##### Lesson 7.2: مساحة متوازي الأضلاع — Area of a Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I find the area of a parallelogram.
- **Key terms:** القاعدة (base), الارتفاع (height)
- **What's taught:** Area = base × height, where height is the perpendicular distance between the base and its opposite side (not the slanted side length) — applied to a vegetable-cutting board shaped as a parallelogram.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A shear-transform animation showing a parallelogram "straightening" into a rectangle of the same base and height, visually justifying why the area formula matches a rectangle's.

##### Lesson 7.3: مساحة المثلث — Area of a Triangle
- **Objective:** I find the area of a triangle.
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity showing two congruent triangles combine into a parallelogram, giving Area = ½ × base × height.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "duplicate and rotate" animation where a copy of the triangle rotates 180° to combine with the original into a parallelogram, visually deriving the ½ factor.

##### Lesson 7.4: مساحة شبه المنحرف — Area of a Trapezoid
- **Objective:** I find the area of a trapezoid.
- **Key terms:** ارتفاع شبه المنحرف (trapezoid height)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a hands-on geometric activity deriving the formula, Area = ½ × (b1+b2) × h, where b1 and b2 are the two parallel sides. Applied to finding a building facade's visible area using a tape measure.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animation showing two congruent trapezoids combining (one rotated 180°) into a parallelogram with base (b1+b2), deriving the ½ factor visually as in the triangle lesson.

##### Lesson 7.5: حجم المنشور الرباعي ومساحة سطحه — Volume of a Rectangular Prism and Its Surface Area
- **Objective:** I find the volume and surface area of a rectangular prism.
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity (likely filling a box with unit cubes) motivating V=length×width×height; surface area is the sum of all six face areas (three pairs of congruent rectangles).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive box that fills with visible unit cubes as length/width/height sliders change, with a synchronized net-unfold view showing the six faces summing to the surface area.

#### Unit 8: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

##### Lesson 8.1: جمع البيانات — Collecting Data
- **Objective:** I classify collected data.
- **Key terms:** بيانات عددية (numerical data), بيانات نوعية (categorical data), بيانات منفصلة (discrete data), بيانات متصلة (continuous data)
- **What's taught:** Data splits into numerical (measurable, can be computed on and ordered) and categorical (non-numeric, observed only); numerical data further splits into discrete (countable exact values) and continuous (measurable, not exactly countable, often rounded). Applied to survey-design contexts like estimating how many students bring packed lunches.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sorting activity where sample data points (a count, a color, a measured length) are dragged into the correct classification bins with instant feedback on which property (numeric? countable?) determined the answer.

##### Lesson 8.2: الجداول التكرارية — Frequency Tables
- **Objective:** I organize data in a frequency table.
- **What's taught:** Tally/count how often each distinct value occurs in a raw data list (e.g. goals scored per match) and organize into a frequency table, the basis for further graphical representation.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live tally tool where dragging raw data values into a table auto-increments frequency counts and builds a matching bar chart alongside.

##### Lesson 8.3: الجداول والمخططات التكرارية ذات الفئات — Frequency Tables and Charts with Class Intervals
- **Objective:** I organize continuous data into class intervals and represent it graphically.
- **What's taught:** For continuous data with many distinct values (e.g. fish lengths to the nearest tenth of a cm), grouping into equal-width class intervals (ranges) makes a frequency table/chart meaningful, since tallying every exact value would be impractical.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive histogram-builder where dragging the class-interval width live-regroups raw data points and redraws the frequency bars, showing how interval choice affects the shape of the distribution.

##### Lesson 8.4: القطاعات الدائرية — Circle Sectors (Pie Charts)
- **Objective:** I read, interpret, and solve problems involving data represented by circle sectors (pie charts).
- **Key terms:** القطاع الدائري (circle sector), زاوية القطاع الدائري (sector angle)
- **What's taught:** Each sector's size represents a category's share of the total (proportion), making pie charts best for comparing a category against the whole — as opposed to bar graphs, better for comparing categories against each other. Students read fractions/percentages directly off sector sizes.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive pie chart where dragging a sector's boundary live-updates its angle, fraction, and percentage labels, and a toggle comparing the same data as a bar graph to highlight when each representation is more useful.

##### Lesson 8.5: الاحتمالات — Probabilities
- **Objective:** I describe the probability of an event using a numerical value on the probability scale.
- **Key terms:** الحادث (event), احتمال الحادث (event probability), مقياس الاحتمال (probability scale)
- **What's taught:** Probability is a number from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain) placed on a probability scale, with labeled regions (impossible, unlikely, equally likely, likely, certain). Students judge and place events on this qualitative-to-quantitative scale, extending the informal "equal/unequal chance" idea from grade 5 toward numeric probability.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable marker on a 0–1 probability scale with labeled zones, where selecting a real-world event (drawing a colored ball, rolling a die) auto-places the marker and explains why.

## Grade 7 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

### Semester 1

#### Unit 1: الأعداد النسبيَّة — Rational Numbers

##### Lesson 1.1: العَدَدُ النِّسْبي — The Rational Number
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize the rational number and represent it on the number line.
- **Key terms:** العَدَدُ النِّسْبيّ (the rational number)
- **What's taught:** Rational numbers are defined formally as numbers expressible as a/b, where a and b are integers and b ≠ 0. Students practice converting mixed numbers, terminating decimals, and percents into this a/b fraction form, and vice versa. The lesson also covers plotting rational numbers — positive and negative fractions, mixed numbers, and decimals — on a number line with a suitably chosen scale/interval. Real-life examples (stock price changes, temperature, measurements) are used to motivate the number type.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive number line where students drag a point to the correct position for a given fraction/decimal/percent, with instant feedback; a live converter widget that shows a value simultaneously as a fraction, decimal, and percent as the student edits one representation.

##### Lesson 1.2: كتابةُ العددِ النسبيِ بالصورةِ العشريّة — Writing a Rational Number in Decimal Form
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I write the rational number in decimal form.
- **Key terms:** كَسرٌ عشريٌّ مُنْتَهٍ (terminating decimal), كَسرٌ عشريٌّ دَوْرِيٌّ (repeating decimal)
- **What's taught:** Two methods for converting a fraction to a decimal are taught: (1) finding an equivalent fraction with denominator 10, 100, or 1000, and (2) long division of the numerator by the denominator. Students learn to distinguish terminating decimals (division ends in a zero remainder) from repeating decimals (a digit or block of digits repeats forever), and use bar notation over the repeating digit(s). Practice includes converting improper fractions and mixed numbers, and real-life measurement problems (distances, depths).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step animated long-division tool that shows each subtract/bring-down-a-zero step and automatically detects and highlights when a remainder repeats (triggering the repeating-decimal bar notation); a side-by-side "equivalent fraction scaling" animation for the ×10/×100 method.

##### Lesson 1.3: مقارَنةُ الأعدادِ النِسبيَةِ وترتيبُها — Comparing and Ordering Rational Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I compare rational numbers and order them.
- **What's taught:** Students compare two rational numbers using mental-math benchmark values (0, 1/2, 1) to judge which is closer to which reference point. They also compare by converting all numbers to decimal form and placing them on a number line, and by converting to fractions with a common denominator (via LCM) and comparing numerators. The lesson covers ordering mixed sets of fractions, decimals, percents, negative numbers, and absolute values in ascending or descending order.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A shared number line with draggable benchmark markers (0, 1/2, 1) that snaps values into place for instant visual comparison; a sortable table where students enter numbers in different formats and see them auto-align and re-sort by true value.

##### Lesson 1.4: جَمْعُ الأعدادِ النِسبِيَةِ وطَرْحُها — Adding and Subtracting Rational Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I add rational numbers and subtract them.
- **Key terms:** النَّظيرُ الجَمعيُّ (the additive inverse)
- **What's taught:** Addition and subtraction are first modeled as hops on a number line (moving right for positive, left for negative), then generalized: finding the LCM of denominators to add/subtract fractions with unlike denominators, handling mixed numbers by converting to improper fractions or by adding whole-number and fractional parts separately, and adding/subtracting decimals. The additive inverse property (a number plus its opposite equals 0) and "subtracting a negative equals adding" are established and applied. Real-life word problems include diving depth relative to sea level and the elevation difference between the Dead Sea and Mount Everest.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Animated number-line "hop" arrows that visually chain together for multi-step signed addition/subtraction; a vertical elevation diagram (sea level as zero) with draggable markers above/below the line to build intuition for signed differences.

##### Lesson 1.5: ضَرْبُ الأعدادِ النِسبِيَةِ وقِسمَتُها — Multiplying and Dividing Rational Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I multiply rational numbers and divide them.
- **Key terms:** النَّظيرُ الضَّربيُّ (the multiplicative inverse)
- **What's taught:** Multiplication of fractions is taught as numerator×numerator over denominator×denominator, with sign-of-product rules from integer multiplication, and simplifying by common factors before multiplying (cross-cancellation) to keep numbers small. This extends to mixed numbers and decimals (multiply as whole numbers, then place the decimal point by counting decimal places). The multiplicative inverse (reciprocal) is defined, and division of rational numbers is taught as multiplying by the reciprocal of the divisor; decimal division uses long division after shifting the decimal point in both numbers to clear it from the divisor.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An area-model animation for multiplying two fractions (overlapping rectangle grids) that visually shows why numerators and denominators multiply; a sign-rule interactive quiz; a decimal-point-shift animation for long division of decimals.

##### Lesson 1.6: خطةُ حلِّ المسألةِ: الحلُّ العكسِي — Problem-Solving Strategy: Working Backward
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I solve problems using the "working backward" strategy.
- **What's taught:** This is a dedicated problem-solving-strategy lesson built around the four-phase framework أفهم / أخطط / أحل / أتحقق (Understand / Plan / Solve / Check). Students learn that when a final result after a chain of operations (additions, subtractions of quantities) is known and the starting value is unknown, they can start from the final value and reverse each operation in opposite order (undoing addition with subtraction and vice versa) to recover the original amount. The core worked example involves fuel consumed/added during a road trip; further practice problems apply the same reversal logic to money, prices, ages, and abstract "think of a number" puzzles.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A reversible flowchart/pipeline animation showing a chain of operation "boxes" (e.g., −6.3, +15, −11.8, ...) that the student can run forward from a start value or "invert and replay backward" from the final value to solve for the unknown start.

#### Unit 2: الأسسُ الصحيحةُ والمقاديرُ الجبريَّة — Integer Exponents and Algebraic Expressions

##### Lesson 2.1: قوانينُ الأسسِ الصحيحة — Laws of Integer Exponents
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I identify exponents and powers, and their multiplication and division rules.
- **Key terms:** أساسٌ (base), أُسٌّ (exponent), الصيغَةُ الأُسِّيَّةُ للعددِ (exponential form of a number), الصيغَةُ القياسيةُ للعددِ (standard form of a number)
- **What's taught:** Repeated multiplication of a number by itself is written compactly using exponents (base and exponent together form a "power"); students convert between exponential form and standard (expanded product) form. The lesson derives and applies the product rule (aᵐ×aⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ), quotient rule (aᵐ÷aⁿ=aᵐ⁻ⁿ), power-of-a-power rule ((aᵐ)ⁿ=aᵐˣⁿ), power-of-a-product rule, and power-of-a-quotient rule. It then extends the pattern (via a table of repeated division by 10) to define the zero exponent (a⁰=1) and negative exponents (a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ), including scientific/exponential expressions from real life (bacteria length, pollen grain size).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive "exponent pattern builder" showing a table of powers of a fixed base that the student extends by repeatedly dividing, watching the pattern flow naturally into the zero and negative exponent cases; draggable tiles for building up aᵐ×aⁿ as stacked repeated-multiplication blocks that merge into aᵐ⁺ⁿ.

##### Lesson 2.2: أولويّاتُ العمليّاتِ الحسابيّة — Order of Operations
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I use the order of operations and the laws of exponents to simplify numerical expressions.
- **Key terms:** أولويّاتُ العمليّاتِ الحسابيّةِ (order of operations)
- **What's taught:** Students apply a strict priority order — parentheses (innermost first), then exponents, then multiplication/division left-to-right, then addition/subtraction left-to-right — to evaluate numerical expressions that mix signed (rational) numbers, nested parentheses, exponents, and fraction bars acting as division. Worked examples combine this ordering with the exponent laws from Lesson 2.1 (e.g., simplifying (−3)⁶/(−3)⁴ before multiplying), and problems are framed in contexts like diver depth changes.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-through expression evaluator that highlights which sub-expression is being resolved at each stage (innermost parenthesis lighting up first, then exponents, then × /÷, then + /−), letting students predict the next step before revealing it.

##### Lesson 2.3: الحدودُ والمقاديرُ الجبريّة — Terms and Algebraic Expressions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I identify the terms, coefficients, and constants in an algebraic expression.
- **Key terms:** متغيِّرٌ (variable), حدٌّ جبريٌّ (algebraic term), معامِلٌ (coefficient), حدٌّ ثابتٌ (constant term), مقدارٌ جبريٌّ (algebraic expression)
- **What's taught:** A variable is introduced as a symbol standing for an unknown value, and an algebraic expression as a combination of terms (numbers, variables, or products of numbers and variables) connected by operations. Students identify the terms, coefficients, and constant term of a given expression; translate short verbal phrases ("a number added to 7," "12 subtracted from twice a number") into algebraic expressions; and evaluate expressions for given variable values by substituting and applying the order of operations, including real-life pricing/cost scenarios.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A color-coded "anatomy of an expression" widget where clicking a term highlights its coefficient and variable part separately; a substitution slider where dragging a variable's value live-updates the expression's computed value.

##### Lesson 2.4: جمعُ المقاديرِ الجبريَةِ وطَرْحُها — Adding and Subtracting Algebraic Expressions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I simplify algebraic expressions by adding and subtracting like terms.
- **Key terms:** حدودٌ جبريَةٌ متشابهةٌ (like algebraic terms), أبسطُ صورةٍ للمقدارِ الجبريِّ (simplest form of an algebraic expression)
- **What's taught:** Like terms — those sharing the same variable(s) raised to the same exponent(s) — are combined by adding or subtracting only their coefficients while keeping the variable part unchanged. Students simplify sums/differences of monomials and polynomials, including cases requiring the distributive property to remove parentheses preceded by a minus sign (distributing −1 and flipping every sign inside), and write algebraic expressions for perimeters of shapes with algebraic side lengths.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "sorting bins" interaction where algebraic tiles/terms must be dragged into matching-like-term bins before their coefficients are summed automatically; an animated parenthesis-removal effect showing signs flipping when a leading negative is distributed.

##### Lesson 2.5: ضَرْبُ المقاديرِ الجبريّة — Multiplying Algebraic Expressions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I multiply algebraic expressions and simplify them.
- **What's taught:** Building from multiplying a number by a monomial (multiply the coefficient, keep the variable) and monomial×monomial (multiply coefficients, apply exponent rules to like variables), the lesson extends to monomial×polynomial via the distributive property, and binomial×binomial (e.g., (x+4)(x+3)) using two parallel methods: an area/rectangle model (splitting a rectangle into four regions whose areas sum to the product) and repeated distribution. Applications include finding a rectangular field's planted area and a room's carpet area in terms of a variable, and evaluating the resulting expression at given values.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable/resizable area-model rectangle split into four labeled sub-rectangles (x², x, x, constant) whose areas visually sum to the binomial product, directly mirroring the textbook's own diagram; sliders on side-length expressions that recompute the total area live.

##### Lesson 2.6: حالاتٌ خاصةٌ من ضربِ المقاديرِ الجبريةِ — Special Cases of Multiplying Algebraic Expressions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I learn the rules for finding the square of a sum of two terms, and the product of the sum and the difference of two terms. *(objective inferred from lesson content; no explicit فكرة الدرس box was captured in the extracted text)*
- **What's taught:** Three special binomial-product identities are derived using algebra-tile/area-model diagrams and then stated formally: (a+b)²=a²+2ab+b², (a−b)²=a²−2ab+b² (derived by rewriting a−b as a+(−b)), and (a+b)(a−b)=a²−b² (difference of squares, shown via a square with a smaller square notch removed). Students apply these directly to expand expressions with monomial terms involving exponents (e.g., (y²+3)², (4x²+d⁵)(4x²−d⁵)) without having to multiply term-by-term.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated algebra-tile diagram (matching the textbook's own a+b square split into a², ab, ab, b² regions) that the student can resize by dragging a and b, watching the total area recompute and match the expanded formula; a similar "notched square" animation for the difference-of-squares identity.

#### Unit 3: المعادلات والمتتاليات — Equations and Sequences

##### Lesson 3.1: حَلُّ المعادلات — Solving Equations
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I solve an equation with one variable on one of its sides using the properties of equality.
- **What's taught:** Students solve linear equations in one variable that require multiple steps: distributing a multiplied constant across parentheses (including when the parenthetical expression is itself subtracted, e.g. 3(x−5) = −(5+x)), collecting variable terms by adding/subtracting them from both sides, then isolating the variable via inverse operations, and finally checking the solution by substituting back into the original equation. Includes writing an equation from a real-world situation (two people's pencil boxes containing equal amounts) and solving it, plus comparing two students' different-but-valid solution paths to the same equation.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A balance-scale animation where each algebraic step (distribute, add/subtract same term to both sides, divide) visibly keeps both pans level, making "doing the same thing to both sides" concrete; a step-order puzzle where students arrange scrambled solution steps into the correct sequence.

##### Lesson 3.2: الكسور العشرية الدورية — Repeating Decimals (as fractions)
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I convert a repeating decimal into a proper fraction or mixed number.
- **Key terms:** كسر عشري دوري (repeating decimal)
- **What's taught:** A genuinely algebraic technique: represent the repeating decimal as x, multiply both sides by 10 (one repeating digit), 100 (two repeating digits), etc. so the repeating part lines up, subtract the original x-equation from the scaled one to cancel the infinite repeating tail, then solve the resulting simple equation for x as a fraction and simplify. Covers both purely repeating decimals (0.4̄) and decimals with a non-repeating part before the repeating block (4.13̄), plus a real-life application (finding a count of students from a repeating-decimal pass rate).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated "align and subtract" tool showing the two scaled decimal expansions stacked so students visually see the repeating tails cancel when subtracted, then watch the resulting simple equation solve itself.

##### Lesson 3.3: المتتاليات — Sequences
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I write sequence terms, and find their general term.
- **Key terms:** متتالية (sequence), الحدّ (term), الحدّ العامّ (general/nth term)
- **What's taught:** A sequence is defined as an ordered list of numbers (terms) following a rule linking each term to the next; students find that connecting rule (constant add/subtract) from given terms, extend the sequence, and — the core skill — derive an algebraic general-term formula Tn (in terms of the term's position n) by relating each term's value to its position (e.g. Tn = (1/4)n + 27/4), then use it to jump directly to any term (e.g. the 15th) without listing every prior term. Includes geometric growing-pattern contexts (dot/square arrangements in a figure sequence) where the same algebra is applied to count elements in a given figure number, plus real contexts (well-drilling cost per meter, weekly savings, hourly wages).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive growing-pattern visual (the textbook's own dot/square figures) where dragging a "figure number" slider shows the pattern build up and its count update live, side by side with the general-term formula computing the same value — directly linking the visual pattern to the algebra.

#### Unit 4: الزوايا والمضلَّعات والتحويلات الهندسية — Angles, Polygons, and Geometric Transformations

##### Lesson 4.1: العلاقات بين الزوايا — Relationships Between Angles
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize the relationships between angles, and use them to solve problems.
- **Key terms:** الزاويتان المتجاورتان (adjacent angles), الزاويتان المتقابلتان بالرأس (vertical angles), الزاويتان المتتامّتان (complementary angles), الزاويتان المتكاملتان (supplementary angles)
- **What's taught:** Four special angle-pair relationships are defined and illustrated: adjacent angles (share a vertex and a side, don't overlap), vertical angles (formed by two intersecting lines, always congruent), complementary angles (sum to 90°), and supplementary angles (sum to 180°). Students identify each pair type from a labeled diagram, then use these relationships together with algebraic equations to solve for unknown angle measures (including angle expressions like (4y−42)° set equal to another via the vertical-angle property), extending to sums of several adjacent angles around a point (360°) or on a line (180°).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-line intersection diagram where moving one line updates all four resulting angles live, with toggleable highlights for each relationship type (vertical/complementary/supplementary/adjacent) so students see which pairs stay equal or summed as the lines move.

##### Lesson 4.2: المستقيمات المتوازية والقاطع — Parallel Lines and a Transversal
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize the relationships between the angles formed when a transversal crosses two parallel lines.
- **Key terms:** المستوى (plane), القاطع (transversal), زاويتان متناظرتان (corresponding angles), زاويتان متبادلتان داخليًّا (alternate interior angles), زاويتان متبادلتان خارجيًّا (alternate exterior angles), زاويتان داخليّتان في جهة واحدة (same-side/co-interior angles)
- **What's taught:** When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, it creates eight angles with special congruence/supplementary relationships: corresponding angles are equal, alternate interior angles are equal, alternate exterior angles are equal, and same-side (co-interior) interior angles are supplementary. Students identify each angle-pair type in a labeled figure, and use the relevant equal/supplementary relationship — sometimes combined with algebraic equations — to solve for unknown angle measures given just one known angle, applied to real contexts like fences and hospital bed rails.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive transversal-cutting-two-parallel-lines diagram where dragging the transversal's angle updates all 8 angles simultaneously, with a filter to highlight only one relationship type at a time (corresponding/alternate/co-interior) to build pattern recognition.

##### Lesson 4.3: زوايا المثلَّث — Triangle Angles
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I justify the relationships between the interior angles and exterior angles of a triangle.
- **Key terms:** الزاوية الداخلية (interior angle), الزاوية الخارجية (exterior angle)
- **What's taught:** Using a parallel line drawn through one vertex (parallel to the opposite side) and the alternate-interior-angle facts from the previous lesson, students derive and prove that a triangle's three interior angles always sum to 180°. They then define a triangle's exterior angle (formed by one side and the extension of an adjacent side) and derive the exterior angle theorem: an exterior angle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent (remote) interior angles. Both relationships are applied to solve multi-step problems for unknown angle measures in triangles, including real-life truss/swing-support contexts.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex triangle with a "cut the corners and lay them on a line" animation proving the 180° sum concretely, plus a toggle showing the exterior angle and highlighting how it visually equals the sum of the two remote interior angles.

##### Lesson 4.4: زوايا المُضلَّع — Polygon Angles
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the sum of a given polygon's interior angle measures; I find the interior and exterior angle measure of a regular polygon.
- **Key terms:** المضلَّع المنتظَم (regular polygon)
- **What's taught:** Built from the triangle-angle-sum fact, students derive the general polygon interior-angle-sum formula S=(n−2)×180° by decomposing an n-sided polygon into (n−2) triangles from one vertex, then apply it both to find a polygon's total interior angle sum and, via algebra, to work backward from a known interior/exterior angle to find the number of sides. For regular polygons specifically, they derive and apply the per-angle formulas: interior angle = (n−2)×180°/n, and exterior angle = 360°/n (since the n exterior angles, one per vertex, always sum to 360° regardless of polygon).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive polygon where a slider for the number of sides n redraws the shape, auto-triangulates it from one vertex, and live-updates the interior/exterior angle sum and per-angle values as n changes — making the (n−2) triangle count and the 360°/n exterior pattern visible rather than memorized.

##### Lesson 4.5: الدوران — Rotation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I draw a rotation on the coordinate plane; I recognize rotational symmetry and its order.
- **Key terms:** الدوران (rotation), مركز الدوران (center of rotation)
- **What's taught:** A rotation moves every point of a figure by a fixed angle and direction around a fixed center point while preserving side lengths and angles. For rotations centered at the origin, students learn and apply three coordinate rules: 90° counterclockwise (or 270° clockwise) sends (a,b)→(−b,a); 180° sends (a,b)→(−a,−b); 270° counterclockwise (or 90° clockwise) sends (a,b)→(b,−a). They plot rotated images of polygons on the coordinate plane using these rules (first practiced physically with a tracing-paper method), and separately learn "rotational symmetry" — a shape has it if it maps onto itself before completing a full 360° turn — and its "order" (how many times it self-aligns during one full rotation), determined by inspecting a shape under repeated partial turns.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A coordinate-plane rotation tool with a draggable-angle dial around a center point that animates a polygon's rotation in real time and displays the transformed coordinates live using the matching rule; a separate "spin and count" widget that rotates a shape continuously and flashes/counts each time it realigns with its start position to demonstrate rotational-symmetry order.

### Semester 2

#### Unit 5: التناسُب وتطبيقاته — Proportion and Its Applications

##### Lesson 5.1: المعدَّل ومعدَّل الوحدة — Rate and Unit Rate
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the unit rate from fractional ratios.
- **Key terms:** المعدَّل (rate), معدَّل الوحدة (unit rate)
- **What's taught:** A rate compares two quantities with different units; simplifying it so the denominator is 1 gives the unit rate. Students compute unit rates from ratios whose numerator and/or denominator are themselves fractions/mixed numbers (e.g. distance walked in 3¾ hours), using either a visual "bar divided into equal time slices" model or fraction division (multiply by the reciprocal), then apply unit rates to real comparisons — speed, heart rate scaled to a different time span, vitamin-content-per-gram comparisons between two foods (comparing numerators once denominators match) — to decide which of two options is "better" per unit.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A partitioned bar/strip diagram where dragging to change the fractional time interval live-recomputes the per-hour unit rate, directly mirroring the textbook's own "divide the rectangle into quarters" model; a side-by-side comparison tool where entering two different-unit rates auto-converts both to unit rate for instant comparison.

##### Lesson 5.2: التناسُب — Proportion
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize a proportion from two given ratios, and solve it.
- **Key terms:** التناسُب (proportion), طرفا التناسُب (extremes), نسبتان متكافئتان (equivalent ratios), وسطا التناسُب (means), الضرب التبادلي (cross multiplication), حلّ التناسُب (solving a proportion)
- **What's taught:** A proportion is an equality between two ratios; students test whether two given ratios form a proportion either by simplifying both to lowest terms and comparing, or by computing both unit rates and comparing them. The core new tool is cross multiplication (in a:b=c:d, a×d=b×c) for solving a proportion when one term is unknown, including cases requiring the distributive property after cross-multiplying (e.g. 32(x−2)=12(x+8)). Applied to real ratio-based problems (staffing ratios by department, scaling a recipe/mixture) by setting up and solving a proportion equation.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "balance" visualization of a:b=c:d where dragging one of the four values live-recalculates the cross-products and shows them as equal-area rectangles that must match; a mixture-scaling tool where changing one ingredient amount auto-scales the others to keep the ratio constant.

##### Lesson 5.3: العلاقات التناسُبية — Proportional Relationships
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize a proportional relationship, and represent it on the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** علاقة التناسُب (proportional relationship)
- **What's taught:** A proportional relationship is one where every corresponding pair of quantities has the same unit rate. Students test this three ways: build a table and check that the unit rate (ratio) is identical for every row; plot the pairs as coordinates and check the points form a straight line through the origin; or, given a graph, check directly whether it's a line through the origin. Applied across many real contexts (reading rate, savings, admission pricing with a fixed family fee that breaks proportionality, water filling a tank) including a non-example (entry fee has a fixed per-family surcharge, so it's not proportional) to sharpen the concept's boundary.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live table-to-graph tool where entering pairs of values instantly plots them and flags whether they're collinear-through-origin (proportional) or not, with the "why" (constant vs. changing unit rate) shown side by side.

##### Lesson 5.4: التغيّر الطردي — Direct Variation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I distinguish direct variation, and write its equation by finding the constant of variation.
- **Key terms:** ثابت التغيّر (constant of variation), التغيّر الطردي (direct variation)
- **What's taught:** Direct variation is formalized as y=kx, where k (the constant of variation) equals the unit rate y/x for every pair. Students verify direct variation from a table (checking y/x is constant), find k, and write the equation, then use it to predict unmeasured values; they also extract k directly from a graph (either reading the y-value at x=1, or computing y/x from any labeled point) and apply the concept to real linear-through-origin contexts (snow accumulation rate, plant growth rate, exercise calories vs. time).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider for k that live-redraws the line y=kx through the origin next to a synced data table, so students see how steepness and the table's ratio are the same thing; a "predict the value" quiz mode using the fitted line.

##### Lesson 5.5: التغيّر العكسي — Inverse Variation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I distinguish inverse variation, and write its equation by finding the constant of variation.
- **Key terms:** التغيّر العكسي (inverse variation)
- **What's taught:** Inverse variation is defined as xy=k (equivalently y=k/x): as one quantity increases the other decreases proportionally, contrasted directly against direct variation using the same lesson structure. Students verify it from a table by checking that x×y is constant across all pairs, find k, write the equation, and use it to interpolate missing values; extract k from a graph (a curve, not a line) by reading any point and computing k=xy; and distinguish direct vs. inverse vs. neither from tables, graphs, and equations (e.g. xy=8 is inverse, y=x+9 is neither). Real contexts include speed vs. travel time for a fixed distance, ocean depth vs. temperature, and workers vs. time to finish a fixed job.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable point on a hyperbola (xy=k) where dragging along the curve shows x and y trading off while their product stays fixed and visibly displayed; a side-by-side direct-vs-inverse graph comparison toggle for the same k value to make the shape contrast (line vs. curve) obvious.

##### Lesson 5.6: التقسيم التناسُبي — Proportional Division
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I use proportional division to solve real-life problems.
- **Key terms:** التقسيم التناسُبي (proportional division)
- **What's taught:** Proportional division splits a total quantity according to a given ratio (e.g. profit-sharing by investment amount, inheritance division). The method: sum the ratio's parts, divide the total quantity by that sum to get the value of "one part," then multiply each party's ratio number by that unit-part value — with a verification step summing all shares back to the original total. Applied to business-profit splitting by capital contribution, land division, inheritance law problems (a spouse's fixed fraction taken first, then the remainder split by a sub-ratio among children), and mixture problems (solvent:solute ratio given total volume).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A stacked-bar or pie visualization where dragging ratio-part boundaries redistributes a fixed total live, with each segment's resulting value and percentage labeled — directly showing "one part" scaling to every share simultaneously.

##### Lesson 5.7: تطبيقات مالية — Financial Applications
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I solve financial problems involving buying/selling and price comparison.
- **Key terms:** التكلفة (cost), سعر البيع (sale price), الربح (profit), الخسارة (loss), التكلفة الكلية (total cost), سعر الصرف (exchange rate)
- **What's taught:** Covers four related money-math skills: profit/loss (profit = sale price − total cost, where total cost includes purchase price plus extra expenses like registration or shipping fees); percentage-based tax and discount calculations (computing a tax/discount amount as a percentage of a base price, then adding/subtracting it, including converting a fixed periodic charge to a monthly rate); and currency exchange using y=k×x with a given exchange rate, applied to comparing a product's price across different countries/currencies to find the genuinely cheapest option (converting each to a common currency before comparing) — including a return to the lesson's own opening "which perfume price is best" problem.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "shopping calculator" widget where entering purchase price, extra fees, and sale price live-computes and visually bars profit vs. loss; a multi-currency price comparison tool where typing a price in any of several currencies auto-converts all of them using live exchange-rate sliders to reveal the true best deal.

#### Unit 6: التطابق والتشابه — Congruence and Similarity

##### Lesson 6.1: التطابق — Congruence
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize congruent polygons, and solve problems based on the concept of congruence.
- **Key terms:** الأضلاع المتناظرة (corresponding sides), الزوايا المتناظرة (corresponding angles), مضلَّعات متطابقة (congruent polygons)
- **What's taught:** Two polygons are congruent when their corresponding sides and corresponding angles are all congruent (linking back to translation/rotation/reflection preserving shape and size). Students write formal congruence statements (matching vertex order, e.g. ABCD≅EFGH implies ∠A≅∠E, AB≅EF, etc.), use congruence to find unknown angle/side measures (including combining with the triangle angle-sum fact first), and set up/solve algebraic equations from congruent expressions (e.g. 2x+4=100).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "matching game" where dragging one polygon onto another (via slide/turn/flip) confirms congruence live and auto-generates the correspondence statement with matched arc/tick-mark notation.

##### Lesson 6.2: مقياس الرسم — Scale Drawing
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I solve problems using scale drawing.
- **Key terms:** مقياس الرسم (scale drawing), مقياس النموذج (scale model), عامل المقياس (scale factor)
- **What's taught:** A scale (with units, e.g. 1cm:2m, or unitless as a scale factor like 1:200 when units match) relates drawing/model measurements to real ones proportionally. Students compute a scale from a known real and drawn length; use a given scale plus a proportion to find real-world distances from a map/drawing measurement (including reading a ruler on an actual map) or, conversely, a model's dimension from a known real dimension; and convert a scale-with-units into a unitless scale factor. Real contexts: carpet design, national map distances, rocket/car/building models, architectural plans.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive map/blueprint where dragging a ruler between two points auto-computes the real-world distance using a settable scale, and a scale slider that resizes a model image live while displaying the resulting real dimensions.

##### Lesson 6.3: التشابه — Similarity
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize similar polygons, and solve problems based on the concept of similarity.
- **Key terms:** أشكال متشابهة (similar figures), مضلَّعات متشابهة (similar polygons)
- **What's taught:** Similar polygons have congruent corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides (same shape, not necessarily same size); explored first via a GeoGebra activity dilating a triangle and measuring both angles and side ratios. Students write similarity statements and ratio equations, verify similarity by checking both angle congruence (using the triangle angle-sum where needed) and equal side ratios, solve proportions for unknown side lengths given similar figures, and use the key perimeter theorem: similar polygons' perimeters are in the same ratio as their corresponding sides (the scale factor) — applied to real contexts like scaled swimming pools and windows.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A GeoGebra-style live construction where dragging one similar shape's vertex rescales it uniformly while a synced measurement table shows angle and side-ratio values updating in real time, reinforcing which quantities stay fixed (angles, ratios) vs. scale (lengths, perimeter).

##### Lesson 6.4: التكبير — Enlargement (Dilation)
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I draw the image of a shape under an enlargement with a positive whole-number scale factor.
- **Key terms:** التكبير (enlargement), معامل التكبير (scale factor of enlargement), مركز التكبير (center of enlargement)
- **What's taught:** An enlargement scales a shape's dimensions by a constant factor k from a fixed center, producing a similar image. Students draw enlargements two ways: physically, by ruling lines from the center through each original vertex and marking new points at k times the original distance (using grid paper or a ruler); and via coordinates, for enlargements centered at the origin, using the rule (x,y)→(kx,ky). They also work backward — given a shape and its enlarged image, find k by comparing corresponding side lengths or corresponding coordinates — and apply this to real magnifying-lens problems (finding an object's true size given its magnified image and the magnification factor).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A coordinate-plane tool with a scale-factor slider (k) and draggable center point where a polygon's enlarged image redraws live, plus rays drawn from the center through each vertex to make the "same ray, k times the distance" construction method visible and manipulable.

##### Lesson 6.5: خطة حل المسألة: الرسم — Problem-Solving Strategy: Drawing a Diagram
- **What's taught:** A dedicated problem-solving-strategy lesson using the four-phase framework (Understand/Plan/Solve/Check): given a real situation describable by two similar triangles (e.g. a person's height and shadow length vs. a building's height and shadow length), students sketch a labeled diagram capturing the given measurements, set up a proportion from the resulting similar-triangle relationship, solve it, and verify by substitution. Reinforces indirect measurement via shadows as a practical application of similarity.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive "shadow measurement" scene where dragging a sun-angle slider casts shadows of a known-height figure and an unknown-height building simultaneously, with a live proportion equation solving for the building's height as the shadows change.

#### Unit 7: المساحات والحجوم — Areas and Volumes

##### Lesson 7.1: محيط الدائرة — Circumference of a Circle
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I calculate the circumference of a circle.
- **Key terms:** محيط الدائرة (circumference), النسبة التقريبية (pi, π)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a GeoGebra activity where students draw circles, measure diameters and circumferences, and discover the ratio C/d is constant (≈3.14). This gives the formulas C=πd and C=2πr (using π≈22/7 or 3.14 depending on the diameter's factors), applied to compute circumference directly, and — via solving the formula as an equation — to work backward from a known circumference to find the radius or diameter. Real contexts: ring sizing, roundabout lap distance, steering wheels, clock hands' sweep distance, thread wound around a spool.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive circle where dragging the radius live-updates both the diameter and computed circumference, with an animated "unrolling" of the circle's edge into a straight segment to make C=πd visually concrete (echoing the GeoGebra discovery activity itself).

##### Lesson 7.2: مساحة الدائرة — Area of a Circle
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I calculate the area of a circle.
- **Key terms:** مساحة الدائرة (area of a circle)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity cutting a circle into 16 sectors and rearranging them into an approximate rectangle (length ≈ half the circumference, width ≈ radius), leading to A=πr². Students compute area directly, solve backward (given area, find radius/diameter, including taking a square root), and apply it to real contexts (coins, pizza value-comparison via price-per-area, no-smoking signs, fans made of quarter-circle blades, shaded-region problems combining circle area with other shapes).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated "cut into sectors and unroll into a rectangle" tool with a slider for number of sectors — as sector count increases, the arrangement approaches a true rectangle, visually deriving A=πr² rather than asserting it.

##### Lesson 7.3: حجم المنشور والأُسطوانة — Volume of a Prism and a Cylinder
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the volume of a prism and a cylinder.
- **Key terms:** الحجم (volume), المنشور (prism), الأُسطوانة (cylinder)
- **What's taught:** Volume is defined as the space a solid occupies. Both prism and cylinder volume reduce to the same idea, V=Bh (base area times height) — for a cylinder this becomes V=πr²h, justified by imagining the solid sliced into thin layers, each with area equal to the base. Students compute volume for rectangular, triangular, and other-based prisms and for cylinders, apply unit conversion (1m³=1000L) to real capacity problems (vertical-farming buildings, fish tanks, silos, swimming pools — including "how long to fill at a given flow rate"), and solve composite/backward problems (given volume, find a missing dimension).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "stack of layers" animation where a prism or cylinder visibly builds up from identical thin base-shaped slices, with a height slider showing volume accumulate layer by layer — directly motivating why V=Bh works for any prism shape.

##### Lesson 7.4: حجم الهَرَم والمخروط — Volume of a Pyramid and a Cone
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the volume of a pyramid and a cone.
- **Key terms:** المخروط (cone)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a hands-on activity comparing a pyramid and prism (and by extension cone/cylinder) with equal base and height: filling the pyramid with sand and pouring it into the prism shows it takes exactly three fillings, giving V=(1/3)Bh for a pyramid and V=(1/3)πr²h for a cone. Students compute volumes directly (including for pyramids with rectangular, square, or triangular bases), apply the formulas to real contexts (the Great Pyramid of Khufu, greenhouse domes, salt cone piles, funnels), and solve backward for a missing dimension given the volume.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A literal "pour and count" simulation — a pyramid-shaped container repeatedly fills and empties into a same-base-and-height prism, visibly showing it takes exactly 3 fills, turning the 1/3 factor into an observed result rather than a memorized constant.

##### Lesson 7.5: المساحة الجانبية والمساحة الكلّية لسطح المنشور والأُسطوانة — Lateral and Total Surface Area of a Prism and a Cylinder
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the lateral and total surface area of a prism's and a cylinder's surface.
- **Key terms:** المساحة الجانبية (lateral area), المساحة الكلّية (total surface area)
- **What's taught:** Lateral area is the sum of a solid's side-face areas (excluding top/bottom); total area adds the two base areas. For a prism, L.A=Ph (base perimeter times height) and S.A=L.A+2B; for a cylinder, thinking of its net (an unrolled rectangle plus two circles) gives L.A=2πrh (or πdh) and S.A=2πrh+2πr². Applied to real problems: skyscraper cladding, road-paving-roller coverage per revolution, gift wrapping.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An "unfold the net" animation where a 3D prism or cylinder peels open flat into its net (rectangle(s) + base shapes), with the lateral rectangle's length visibly equal to the base's perimeter/circumference — making the L.A formula's origin tangible instead of abstract.

##### Lesson 7.6: مساحة سطح الهَرَم والمخروط — Surface Area of a Pyramid and a Cone
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the lateral and total surface area of a regular pyramid's and a cone's surface.
- **Key terms:** هَرَم منتظم (regular pyramid), الارتفاع الجانبي (slant height)
- **What's taught:** For a regular pyramid, rearranging its congruent triangular faces forms half a rectangle, giving L.A=(1/2)Pℓ (half the base perimeter times slant height ℓ) and S.A=L.A+B. For a cone, analogously, L.A=πrℓ and S.A=L.A+B. Students compute both areas given base and slant-height measurements, apply the formulas to real objects (a light-fixture cone, a pyramidal roof needing a wood-panel count, lab filter funnels, traffic cones, a hexagonal lampshade), and reason about how changing radius or slant height affects the resulting area without recomputing fully.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An unfolding-net animation for a cone (sector unrolling from the lateral surface) and for a pyramid (triangular faces fanning open into a half-rectangle), with a slant-height slider that live-updates both the net shape and the computed lateral area.

#### Unit 8: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

##### Lesson 8.1: الوسط الحسابي — The Mean
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I describe the effect of an outlier on a data set's mean.
- **Key terms:** مقاييس النزعة المركزية (measures of central tendency), الوسط الحسابي (mean), القيمة المتطرِّفة (outlier)
- **What's taught:** The mean is introduced both conceptually (the balance point where the sum of distances to values above it equals the sum of distances to values below, shown via a number-line/dot diagram) and computationally (sum divided by count). Students compute the mean, verify the "balance" property with a diagram, identify an outlier (a value far from the rest) and describe how it pulls the mean toward itself, and use a known mean algebraically to solve for a missing data value (mean × count − sum of known values).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable number-line "balance beam" where each data point is a weight and the mean is the fulcrum position that keeps it level — dragging in an outlier visibly tips the balance and shows the mean shifting toward it in real time.

##### Lesson 8.2: الوسيط والمِنوال والمَدى — The Median, Mode, and Range
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I calculate the median, mode, and range, and determine the most suitable measure to describe a data set.
- **Key terms:** الوسيط (median), المِنوال (mode), المَدى (range)
- **What's taught:** The median (middle value of sorted data, or average of the two middle values for even counts) and mode (most frequent value) are introduced as alternative center-describing measures, and range (max−min) as a spread measure — larger range means more scattered data. The key judgment skill: choosing the right measure per situation — mode for qualitative/categorical data (colors, etc.), median when outliers are present (since it resists their pull, unlike the mean, illustrated with a "one manager's salary among ten employees" example), range for comparing consistency/homogeneity between two data sets.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A side-by-side dashboard showing mean, median, mode, and range simultaneously updating as data points are dragged on a number line, with the outlier's differential pull on mean vs. median visually highlighted to build the intuition for when each measure is trustworthy.

##### Lesson 8.3: التمثيل بالساق والورقة — Stem-and-Leaf Plot
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I represent data with a stem-and-leaf plot, and test a hypothesis based on given data.
- **Key terms:** مخطط الساق والورقة (stem-and-leaf plot), الفرضية (hypothesis)
- **What's taught:** Splits each data value into a "stem" (leading digit(s)) and "leaf" (last digit), organized into a two-column display that preserves every individual value while showing distribution shape, and keeps data sorted so median/mode/range can be read directly off the plot. Students build plots from raw data lists, read values back off existing plots, and — the second half of the lesson — apply the four-step hypothesis-testing framework (state a hypothesis → collect data → represent and compute → conclude accept/reject) using stem-and-leaf-organized data as evidence, e.g. testing a coach's claim about how many players can finish a lap under a time threshold.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive stem-and-leaf builder where dragging raw data values sorts them live into stems/leaves, plus a "test my hypothesis" mode where typing a claim (e.g. "more than half are above X") highlights the relevant leaves and computes whether the data supports it.

##### Lesson 8.4: الاحتمالات — Probability
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I calculate the probability of events occurring.
- **Key terms:** الفضاء العينيّ (sample space), الحادث (event), احتمال الحادث (event probability), الجدول ذو الاتجاهين (two-way table)
- **What's taught:** Introduces the sample space (all possible outcomes), an event (one or more outcomes), and probability as (favorable outcomes)/(total outcomes) for equally-likely outcomes, plus the complement rule P(not A)=1−P(A). Students compute probabilities of simple and compound ("or") events from spinners/bags of objects, apply the complement rule to real contexts (rain, sports outcomes, ownership rates), and build/read two-way frequency tables (two categorical variables crossed in rows/columns with margin totals) to compute probabilities from real survey-style data (e.g. probability a randomly chosen child is a boy who prefers blue).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive spinner/bag simulator where students configure outcome counts and see the computed theoretical probability live, plus a two-way table builder where adjusting cell counts recalculates every conditional probability question instantly.

##### Lesson 8.5: الاحتمال التجريبي — Experimental Probability
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the experimental probability of an event.
- **Key terms:** الاحتمال النظري (theoretical probability), الاحتمال التجريبي (experimental probability)
- **What's taught:** Distinguishes theoretical probability (computed from equally-likely outcome counts) from experimental probability (estimated from actually repeating a trial and counting outcomes, e.g. tossing a coin or rolling a die many times). Students compute experimental probability from tally tables, compare two people's experimental results against theoretical expectations to judge whether a die/spinner is likely "fair" (unbiased), and use experimental probability predictively — scaling a small-sample defect rate up to predict outcomes in a much larger population (e.g. quality-control sampling, expected rainy days).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A repeatable coin-flip/die-roll simulator where running more and more trials shows the experimental probability converging toward the theoretical value live (a direct, hands-on law-of-large-numbers demonstration), alongside a fairness-tester comparing two simulated dice's experimental distributions.

### Semester 1

#### Unit 1: الأعداد الحقيقية — Real Numbers

##### Lesson 1.1: الجذور التربيعية — Square Roots
- **Objective:** I find the square root of a number, and use it to solve real-life problems.
- **Key terms:** الجذر التربيعي (square root), الجذر التربيعي الرئيس (principal square root), المجذور (radicand)
- **What's taught:** Every positive number has two square roots (positive/principal and negative), notated ±√ with the radicand under the root. Students find principal and both square roots of whole numbers/decimals, and solve equations of the form x²=c by taking ±√c and verifying by substitution.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive "unsquare" tool where dragging a square's area slider shows its side length update live, plus a step-by-step equation solver visually splitting x²=c into ±√c with a substitution check.

##### Lesson 1.2: الجذور الصمّاء — Irrational (Surd) Roots
- **Objective:** I estimate the value of a square root.
- **Key terms:** الجذور الصمّاء (surds)
- **What's taught:** Surds (roots with no exact decimal value, e.g. √3) are distinguished from roots of perfect squares; students estimate a surd's value using a number line (bounding it between consecutive perfect squares) and a calculator, applied to a wave-speed physics formula.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable number-line tool that snaps a surd between its bounding perfect squares and compares the estimate to a live calculator readout.

##### Lesson 1.3: نظرية فيثاغورس — Pythagorean Theorem
- **Objective:** I use the Pythagorean theorem to find an unknown side length in a right triangle.
- **Key terms:** نظرية فيثاغورس (Pythagorean theorem), الوتر (hypotenuse), الساقان (legs)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity, c²=a²+b² is applied to find a missing leg or hypotenuse by solving the resulting equation and taking the square root (rejecting the negative root since lengths are positive).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A right-triangle tool where dragging two leg lengths (or a leg and hypotenuse) live-computes the third side and overlays the a², b², c² area squares.

##### Lesson 1.4: الأعداد الحقيقية — Real Numbers
- **Objective:** I distinguish between rational and irrational numbers.
- **Key terms:** العدد غير النسبي (irrational number), العدد الحقيقي (real number)
- **What's taught:** An irrational number cannot be written as a/b (integers, b≠0); students classify numbers as rational/irrational, applied to a real slope-length context (skate ramp base length) where a computed value must be tested for rationality.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive nested Venn diagram (naturals→integers→rationals→reals with irrationals branching off) where clicking a number sorts it into place.

##### Lesson 1.5: النسبة المئوية — Percentage
- **Objective:** I solve real-life problems involving percentages.
- **Key terms:** النسبة المئوية للتغير (percentage change), نسبة الزيادة/النقصان المئوية (percentage increase/decrease), النسبة المئوية العكسية (reverse percentage)
- **What's taught:** Percentages compare a number to 100 (over 100% if the number exceeds 100, under 1% if less than 1). Applied to olive-oil production year-over-year change (119% of a prior year's total), extending to percentage increase/decrease and reverse-percentage problems (finding an original value from a final percentage-adjusted one).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider-driven bar/strip diagram showing an "original" bar stretching or shrinking to a "new" bar as the percentage changes, with the change formula updating live.

#### Unit 2: تحليل المقادير الجبرية — Factoring Algebraic Expressions

##### Lesson 2.1: التحليل بإخراج العامل المشترك الأكبر — Factoring by GCF
- **Objective:** I factor algebraic expressions by extracting the greatest common factor.
- **Key terms:** الصورة التحليلية (factored form), التحليل (factoring), التجميع (grouping)
- **What's taught:** The GCF concept extends from numbers to algebraic terms (shared numeric and variable factors), factored out via the distributive property in reverse. Applied to finding an unknown screen dimension from an area expression, and to grouping-based factoring for four-term expressions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive area-model rectangle where dragging out a common "width" factor from multiple terms visually reveals the remaining factor as the rectangle's other side.

##### Lesson 2.2: تحليل ثلاثيات الحدود x²+bx+c — Factoring Trinomials x²+bx+c
- **Objective:** I factor trinomials of the form x²+bx+c.
- **What's taught:** Since (x+m)(x+n) = x²+(m+n)x+mn, factoring reverses this: find two numbers whose product is c and sum is b, giving the binomial factors. Applied to finding a greenhouse's unknown side length from its area expression.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An area-model rectangle split into four labeled regions (x², mx, nx, mn) that a student rearranges to match a given trinomial, discovering m and n by trial.

##### Lesson 2.3: تحليل ثلاثيات الحدود ax²+bx+c — Factoring Trinomials ax²+bx+c
- **Objective:** I factor trinomials of the form ax²+bx+c where a≠1.
- **What's taught:** Find two numbers whose product is a×c and sum is b, using their signs (matching c's sign to determine whether both factors share or differ in sign, and b's sign to determine which), then use those numbers to split the middle term and factor by grouping.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A guided factor-pair search table where students test candidate number pairs against the required product and sum, with the tool marking a match and auto-completing the grouping.

##### Lesson 2.4: حالات خاصة من التحليل — Special Cases of Factoring
- **Objective:** I factor an expression representing a difference of two squares; I factor a perfect-square trinomial.
- **What's taught:** Two identities are applied in reverse to factor quickly without the general method: a²−b² = (a+b)(a−b) (difference of squares), and a²±2ab+b² = (a±b)² (perfect square trinomial) — applied to a physics expression for pressure difference across an airplane wing (½dv²−½du²).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "notched square" animation showing a²−b² as a large square with a smaller square removed, rearranging into an (a+b)×(a−b) rectangle to justify the identity visually.

#### Unit 3: المعادلات الخطية بمتغيرين — Linear Equations in Two Variables

##### Lesson 3.1: المعادلة الخطية بالصورة القياسية — The Linear Equation in Standard Form
- **Objective:** I identify a linear equation, and write it in standard form.
- **What's taught:** A linear equation's standard form is Ax+By=C; students rearrange equations (like y=6−5x) into this form and determine whether a given equation qualifies as linear, applied to reading a water-draining tank's rate/intercept from a line graph.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live equation-rearranger where dragging terms between sides shows the equation morphing into standard form step by step.

##### Lesson 3.2: ميل المستقيم — Slope of a Line
- **Objective:** I find the slope of a line.
- **What's taught:** Slope can be positive, negative, zero, or undefined depending on the line's direction (visualized as "walking" left-to-right along it); computed from rise/run between two points or read from a graph.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable two-point line tool with a live rise/run triangle overlay and slope value, letting students drag toward horizontal/vertical to see zero and undefined slope emerge.

##### Lesson 3.3: معادلة المستقيم بصيغة الميل والمقطع — Equation of a Line in Slope-Intercept Form
- **Objective:** I write and use the equation of a line in slope-intercept form.
- **What's taught:** y=mx+b, where m is the slope and b the y-intercept; students write this form from a graph or given slope/intercept, and graph a line from its equation.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Slider controls for m and b that redraw the line live, visually separating the two parameters' individual effects (tilt vs. vertical shift).

##### Lesson 3.4: معادلة المستقيم بصيغة الميل ونقطة — Equation of a Line in Point-Slope Form
- **Objective:** I write the equation of a line given its slope and a point on it.
- **What's taught:** y−y₁=m(x−x₁) constructs a line's equation from one known point and the slope, useful when the y-intercept isn't directly known; convertible to slope-intercept or standard form.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable point-and-slope-handle tool where moving either live-rewrites the point-slope equation and redraws the line.

##### Lesson 3.5: المستقيمات المتوازية والمتعامدة — Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
- **Objective:** I write the equation of a line parallel or perpendicular to a given line through a given point.
- **What's taught:** Parallel lines share the same slope; perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals. Applied to a computer-controlled drill needing a path perpendicular to a marble surface line through a specified point.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive tool where dragging a point and toggling parallel/perpendicular mode auto-generates and draws the corresponding line relative to a fixed reference line, with slopes labeled live.

#### Unit 4: المثلثات المتطابقة — Congruent Triangles

##### Lesson 4.1: تطابق المثلثات (SSS, SAS, HL) — Triangle Congruence (SSS, SAS, HL)
- **Objective:** I prove triangle congruence using SSS and SAS; I prove right-triangle congruence using HL.
- **Key terms:** المسلمة (postulate), النظرية (theorem), البرهان (proof), البرهان السهمي (flow proof), البرهان ذو العمودين (two-column proof), الزاوية المحصورة (included angle)
- **What's taught:** Formal introduction to postulates, theorems, and two proof formats (two-column and flow/arrow proofs). SSS (three sides match), SAS (two sides and the included angle match), and HL (hypotenuse-leg, for right triangles only) are used to prove two triangles congruent, applied to a tree-staking wire-support real-world setup.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-to-match triangle overlay tool where matching marked sides/angles between two triangles highlights which congruence postulate applies as pieces align.

##### Lesson 4.2: تطابق المثلثات (ASA, AAS) — Triangle Congruence (ASA, AAS)
- **Objective:** I prove triangle congruence using ASA and AAS.
- **What's taught:** ASA (two angles and the included side match) and AAS (two angles and a non-included side match) extend the congruence toolkit from lesson 4.1, applied through further two-column/flow proofs.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** The same overlay-matching tool extended to angle-side combinations, with a "which postulate applies" quiz mode across all five criteria (SSS/SAS/ASA/AAS/HL).

##### Lesson 4.3: المثلثات المتطابقة الضلعين والمثلثات المتطابقة الأضلاع — Isosceles and Equilateral Triangles
- **Objective:** I use properties of isosceles and equilateral triangles to solve problems.
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a hands-on activity (folding a paper isosceles triangle along its axis of symmetry) demonstrating that the two base angles are congruent; extended to equilateral triangles (all three angles congruent, each 60°). Students apply these properties in proofs and to solve for unknown angles/sides.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A fold-simulation animation matching the textbook's own paper-folding activity, plus a draggable isosceles triangle where the base angles stay locked equal as the apex angle changes.

### Semester 2

#### Unit 5: المتباينات الخطية — Linear Inequalities

##### Lesson 5.1: كتابة المتباينات وتمثيلها — Writing and Representing Inequalities
- **Objective:** I recognize an inequality, and represent it on a number line.
- **Key terms:** المتباينة (inequality), حل المتباينة (solution of an inequality)
- **What's taught:** An inequality compares two quantities using <, >, ≤, or ≥; students translate real statements (a speed-camera's enforced limit) into inequality symbols and represent solution sets as shaded rays on a number line (open vs. closed circles for strict vs. inclusive bounds).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line tool where selecting an inequality symbol live-shades the correct region with the correct circle style (open/filled) at the boundary.

##### Lesson 5.2: حل المتباينات بالجمع والطرح — Solving Inequalities by Addition and Subtraction
- **Objective:** I solve one-step inequalities using addition or subtraction.
- **What's taught:** Adding or subtracting the same value from both sides of an inequality preserves the inequality direction, mirroring equation-solving, with solutions represented on a number line.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A balance-scale-style animator (tilted, not level, to represent inequality) that stays consistently tilted as equal amounts are added/removed from both sides.

##### Lesson 5.3: حل المتباينات بالضرب والقسمة — Solving Inequalities by Multiplication and Division
- **Objective:** I solve one-step inequalities using multiplication or division.
- **What's taught:** Multiplying or dividing both sides by a positive number preserves the inequality direction; doing so by a negative number flips it — the key rule distinguishing inequalities from equations.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sign-toggle demo where multiplying a sample inequality by a slider value (positive or negative) shows the inequality arrow flip in real time exactly when the multiplier crosses zero.

##### Lesson 5.4: حل المتباينات متعددة الخطوات — Solving Multi-Step Inequalities
- **Objective:** I solve inequalities requiring more than one step.
- **What's taught:** Combines the addition/subtraction and multiplication/division rules in sequence to isolate the variable, applied to a real weight-loss-rate problem (solving for number of weeks needed to reach a target).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step inequality solver highlighting each transformation applied to both sides, flagging the moment (if any) a sign-flip is triggered by a negative multiplier/divisor.

#### Unit 6: أنظمة المعادلات الخطية — Systems of Linear Equations

##### Lesson 6.1: حل نظام من معادلتين خطيتين بيانيًّا — Solving a System of Two Linear Equations Graphically
- **Objective:** I solve a system of two linear equations by graphing.
- **Key terms:** نظام المعادلات الخطية (system of linear equations)
- **What's taught:** A system is two or more linear equations sharing the same variables; graphing both lines and finding their intersection point gives the solution, applied to finding when two trees growing at different constant rates reach the same height. A GeoGebra lab activity accompanies this lesson for graphing systems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual-line grapher where adjusting each equation's slope/intercept live-updates the intersection point, directly visualizing the system's solution.

##### Lesson 6.2: حل نظام من معادلتين خطيتين بالتعويض — Solving a System of Two Linear Equations by Substitution
- **Objective:** I solve a system of two linear equations by substitution.
- **What's taught:** Solve one equation for one variable, substitute that expression into the other equation to solve for the remaining variable, then back-substitute to find the first.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step substitution animator showing an expression "swapping into" the second equation in place of the solved variable.

##### Lesson 6.3: حل نظام من معادلتين خطيتين بالحذف — Solving a System of Two Linear Equations by Elimination
- **Objective:** I solve a system of two linear equations by elimination.
- **What's taught:** Multiply one or both equations by constants (if needed) so a variable's coefficients match or are opposites, then add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable, solve for the remaining one, and back-substitute.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A side-by-side equation-stacking tool where scaling one equation by a chosen multiplier live-shows matching coefficients align before the elimination (add/subtract) step executes.

#### Unit 7: الأشكال ثنائية الأبعاد — Two-Dimensional Shapes

##### Lesson 7.1: إثبات توازي المستقيمات وتعامدها — Proving Lines Parallel and Perpendicular
- **Objective:** I identify parallel lines based on relationships between angle pairs formed by two lines cut by a transversal.
- **What's taught:** Reviews the parallel-line angle postulates/theorems (corresponding, alternate interior, co-interior, alternate exterior) from grade 7, then proves and applies their *converses*: congruent corresponding angles, congruent alternate angles, or supplementary co-interior angles each imply the two lines are parallel. Also covers the perpendicular-transversal theorem and its converse. Applied to a ladder's rungs and side supports.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive transversal-and-two-lines diagram where dragging one line's angle updates all 8 angles live, letting students toggle which angle-pair relationship forces parallelism and see it confirmed instantly.

##### Lesson 7.2: متوازي الأضلاع — Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I identify the properties of a parallelogram's sides, angles, and diagonals.
- **What's taught:** From the definition (opposite sides parallel), students derive and apply theorems: opposite sides congruent, opposite angles congruent, consecutive angles supplementary, diagonals bisect each other — used in two-column/flow proofs and to solve for unknown sides/angles.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex parallelogram where manipulating one vertex shows opposite sides/angles/diagonal segments update simultaneously while always staying equal.

##### Lesson 7.3: تمييز متوازي الأضلاع — Identifying (Proving) a Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I determine the conditions that confirm a quadrilateral is a parallelogram.
- **What's taught:** Teaches the converses of lesson 7.2's theorems as a multi-way test: a quadrilateral is a parallelogram if both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, or both pairs of opposite sides are congruent, or both pairs of opposite angles are congruent, or the diagonals bisect each other, or one pair of sides is both parallel and congruent.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A checklist-style interactive quadrilateral where students pick which condition to test on a draggable shape, with the tool visually confirming/denying parallelogram status.

##### Lesson 7.4: حالات خاصة من متوازي الأضلاع — Special Cases of a Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I determine the properties of the rectangle, rhombus, and square, and classify a given parallelogram.
- **What's taught:** Adds distinguishing theorems: a parallelogram is a rectangle iff its diagonals are congruent; a rhombus iff its diagonals are perpendicular (and iff each diagonal bisects the angles it connects); a square is both. Presented with a Venn-diagram hierarchy of parallelogram/rectangle/rhombus/square.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A single draggable parallelogram with live diagonal-length and diagonal-angle readouts that visually "snaps" a classification label as it's dragged toward each special condition.

##### Lesson 7.5: تشابه المثلثات — Similar Triangles
- **Objective:** I identify similar triangles using the AA, SSS, and SAS similarity criteria.
- **What's taught:** Extends grade 7's similar-polygon definition to formal triangle-similarity shortcuts: AA (two matching angle pairs suffice), SSS similarity (all three side ratios equal), SAS similarity (two side ratios equal with the included angle congruent). Applied to indirect-measurement problems like finding a house facade's height.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A pair of triangles where resizing/rotating one overlays matching side-ratio labels and angle-arc highlights in real time, showing AA/SSS/SAS conditions being satisfied or broken.

##### Lesson 7.6: التمدّد — Dilation
- **Objective:** I draw the image of a polygon under a dilation in the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** التمدّد (dilation), مركز التمدّد (center of dilation), معامل التمدّد (scale factor)
- **What's taught:** A dilation scales a figure from a fixed center by a scale factor k (k>1 enlargement, 0<k<1 reduction); for dilations centered at the origin, (x,y)→(kx,ky). Applied to redrawing a kite shape after a 2.5-factor enlargement in the coordinate plane.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A coordinate-plane tool with a scale-factor slider and draggable center point where a polygon's dilated image redraws live as k changes.

#### Unit 8: الأشكال ثلاثية الأبعاد — Three-Dimensional Shapes

##### Lesson 8.1: رسم الأشكال ثلاثية الأبعاد — Drawing Three-Dimensional Shapes
- **Objective:** I draw 3D shapes using isometric drawing and orthographic views.
- **Key terms:** الرسم المتساوي (isometric drawing), المسقط العلوي/الأمامي/الجانبي (top/front/side projection view)
- **What's taught:** Isometric drawing represents a 3D shape on 2D triangular/dotted paper; students draw and interpret unit-cube structures this way, and separately produce top/front/side orthographic views of the same solid, plus count the unit cubes composing a given structure.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A 3D cube-stacking builder synced to a live isometric-grid drawing and to auto-generated top/front/side view panels, so students see all representations of the same solid update together.

##### Lesson 8.2: المقاطع والمجسّمات الدورانية — Cross-Sections and Solids of Revolution
- **Objective:** I identify the cross-section resulting from a plane intersecting a solid; I recognize solids formed by rotation.
- **Key terms:** المقطع (cross-section), المقطع العرضي (cross-section parallel to the base)
- **What's taught:** The shape formed where a plane slices through a solid is a cross-section; when that plane is parallel to the base it's specifically called a cross-section (as opposed to an angled slice), illustrated with prisms yielding triangular/rectangular/hexagonal cross-sections depending on cut angle. Also covers solids of revolution (a 3D solid generated by rotating a 2D shape around an axis).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slicing-plane tool where dragging a plane through a 3D solid at any angle live-reveals the resulting 2D cross-section shape, plus a spin-animation showing a 2D profile sweeping into a 3D solid of revolution.

##### Lesson 8.3: حجم الكرة ومساحة سطحها — Volume and Surface Area of a Sphere
- **Objective:** I find the volume and surface area of a sphere.
- **What's taught:** Surface area S.A=4πr² and volume V=(4/3)πr³, applied directly to compute a sphere's measurements from its radius, rounded to a given precision.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive sphere with a radius slider showing surface area and volume recompute live, alongside a visual comparison to a cylinder of the same radius/height to hint at the 2/3 volume relationship.

#### Unit 9: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

##### Lesson 9.1: الرُّبَيعيات — Quartiles
- **Objective:** I find the interquartile range and relate it to data spread; I represent data with a box-and-whisker plot.
- **Key terms:** مقاييس التشتت (measures of spread), الرُّبَيعيات (quartiles), المدى الرُّبَيعي (interquartile range), الرُّبَيع الأدنى/الأعلى (lower/upper quartile)
- **What's taught:** Data is split at the median into lower and upper halves, each with its own median (the lower/upper quartile); the interquartile range (upper−lower quartile) measures spread resistant to outliers, represented visually as a box-and-whisker plot. Applied to testing a hypothesis about daily water-cup consumption.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive dot-plot-to-box-plot builder where dragging data points live recomputes and redraws the five-number summary (min, Q1, median, Q3, max) as a box-and-whisker diagram.

##### Lesson 9.2: اختيار التمثيل الأنسب — Choosing the Most Suitable Representation
- **Objective:** I choose the most appropriate graphical representation for a given data set.
- **What's taught:** Compares representations learned across grades (bar graph, line graph, pie chart, box-and-whisker plot, etc.) and gives criteria for which suits a given data type/question — e.g. box plots for spread and outlier-resistant comparison, pie charts for part-to-whole, line graphs for change over time.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "same data, every representation" toggle where one data set renders simultaneously as a bar graph, line graph, pie chart, and box plot, letting students see which reveals the requested insight most clearly.

##### Lesson 9.3: عَدّ النواتج — Counting Outcomes
- **Objective:** I determine the outcomes of a sample space and their count.
- **Key terms:** النواتج (outcomes), الحادث (event), الفضاء العيني (sample space), مخطط الشجرة (tree diagram), مخطط الاحتمال (probability chart)
- **What's taught:** All possible outcomes of a random experiment form the sample space; students count combined-choice outcomes (e.g. major × university combinations) using tree diagrams and the fundamental counting principle (multiplying the number of choices at each stage).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive tree-diagram builder where adding choices at each branching level auto-expands the tree and tallies the total outcome count, cross-checked against the multiplication shortcut.

##### Lesson 9.4: احتمال الحوادث المركّبة — Probability of Compound Events
- **Objective:** I find the probability of compound events.
- **Key terms:** الحادث البسيط (simple event), الحادث المركّب (compound event)
- **What's taught:** A simple event has one outcome; a compound event combines two or more simple events (e.g. guessing a two-digit code with a parity constraint on each digit). Students compute compound-event probabilities by combining the sample-space-counting techniques from 9.3 with the basic probability ratio P(A) = favorable/total outcomes.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A two-stage random-choice simulator (e.g. spinner + coin) where students build the compound sample space via a tree diagram and watch the theoretical probability compare against many simulated trials.

## Grade 9 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

### Semester 1

#### Unit 1: المتباينات الخطية — Linear Inequalities

##### Lesson 1.1: المجموعات والفترات — Sets and Intervals
- **Objective:** I express inequalities using sets and interval notation.
- **Key terms:** مجموعة (set), عنصر (element), المجموعة الخالية (empty set), المجموعة المنتهية/غير المنتهية (finite/infinite set), رمز الفترة (interval notation), اللانهاية (infinity), الفترة غير المحدودة (unbounded interval)
- **What's taught:** Reviews set notation (roster form, defining property) then introduces interval notation as a compact way to describe solution sets of inequalities — bounded intervals (open, closed, half-open) and unbounded intervals using the infinity symbol, connecting each interval to its number-line picture.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable number-line tool where students shade a region and the interval notation (and set-builder notation) updates live, and vice versa — typing an interval renders the shaded line.

##### Lesson 1.2: حل المتباينات المركّبة — Solving Compound Inequalities
- **Objective:** I express compound inequalities using intervals.
- **Key terms:** متباينة بسيطة (simple inequality), متباينة مركّبة (compound inequality), تقاطع (intersection), اتحاد (union), فترة محدودة (bounded interval)
- **What's taught:** A compound inequality combines two simple inequalities with "and" (intersection of solution sets) or "or" (union of solution sets); students solve and graph both types on the number line, e.g. modeling an acceptable water-temperature range for aquarium fish as an "and" compound inequality.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Two overlapping draggable shaded regions on a number line that a toggle combines via intersection or union, showing the resulting compound solution set update in real time.

##### Lesson 1.3: تمثيل المتباينات الخطية بمتغيّرين بيانيًا — Graphing Linear Inequalities in Two Variables
- **Objective:** I graph a linear inequality in two variables.
- **Key terms:** المتباينة الخطية بمتغيّرين (linear inequality in two variables), منطقة الحلول الممكنة (feasible/solution region), المستقيم الحدودي (boundary line)
- **What's taught:** A linear inequality in two variables (ax+by<c, ≤, >, or ≥) has as its solution set an infinite collection of ordered pairs, graphed as a shaded half-plane bounded by a line (dashed for strict inequalities, solid otherwise); applied to a scheduling problem (assembling two types of microwaves under a weekly hour limit). Includes a GeoGebra lab extension for graphing these inequalities dynamically.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive plane where dragging the boundary line's slope/intercept or toggling strict vs. non-strict and the inequality direction live-updates the shaded feasible region, with draggable test points that show which side satisfies the inequality.

#### Unit 2: العلاقات والاقترانات — Relations and Functions

##### Lesson 2.1: الاقترانات — Functions
- **Objective:** I determine the domain and range of a function.
- **Key terms:** علاقة (relation), مجال (domain), مدى (range), اقتران (function), اقتران متّصل/منفصل (continuous/discrete function), اختبار الخط الرأسي (vertical line test), الاقتران الخطي/غير الخطي (linear/non-linear function)
- **What's taught:** Distinguishes relations from functions (each input maps to exactly one output), introduces domain/range identification from mappings, tables, graphs, and equations, and the vertical line test for verifying a graph is a function; applied to a light-speed-distance function d(t)=300000t.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A function-vs-relation sorter where students drag point sets or curves and get instant vertical-line-test feedback, plus a domain/range highlighter that shades the input and output axes as a graph is traced.

##### Lesson 2.2: تفسير التمثيلات البيانية — Interpreting Graphical Representations
- **Objective:** I interpret graphical representations of relationships (e.g. distance/time or position/time graphs).
- **Key terms:** منحنيات التحويل (conversion graphs), منحنى الموقع-الزمن (position-time graph)
- **What's taught:** Extends earlier unit-conversion work using conversion-graph curves, and teaches reading qualitative story information (motion, rest, direction change) directly off position-vs-time graphs, e.g. determining how long a car trip lasted and how long the car was stopped from a graph shape.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "graph storyteller" where students drag a marker along a position-time curve and watch an animated car move correspondingly, reinforcing that slope segments correspond to motion phases (constant speed, stopped, reversing).

##### Lesson 2.3: الاقتران التربيعي — The Quadratic Function
- **Objective:** I graph a quadratic function in the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** الاقتران التربيعي (quadratic function), الصورة القياسية (standard form), الاقتران الرئيس (parent function), قطع مكافئ (parabola), محور التماثل (axis of symmetry), الرأس (vertex), نقطة القيمة الصغرى/العظمى (minimum/maximum point)
- **What's taught:** Introduces the parabola shape of quadratic functions, standard form f(x)=ax²+bx+c, how to find the axis of symmetry, vertex, and min/max point, and reading these features off a graph; applied to modeling a bridge's arch-shaped support and checking ship clearance underneath.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Sliders for a, b, c on f(x)=ax²+bx+c that redraw the parabola live while a marker locks onto and labels the vertex and axis of symmetry as coefficients change.

##### Lesson 2.4: التحويلات الهندسية للاقترانات التربيعية — Geometric Transformations of Quadratic Functions
- **Objective:** I graph curves of quadratic functions resulting from applying one or more geometric transformations to the parent function's graph.
- **Key terms:** التحويل الهندسي (geometric transformation), الانسحاب الرأسي/الأفقي (vertical/horizontal translation), التمدّد (stretch), الانعكاس (reflection), صيغة الرأس (vertex form)
- **What's taught:** Shows how changing the parent function f(x)=x² by translations (up/down, left/right), reflections, and stretches produces the family of quadratic curves in vertex form, comparing g(x)=x²−2 to f(x)=x² as an introductory example. Includes a GeoGebra lab exploring these transformations interactively.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Independent sliders for vertical shift, horizontal shift, stretch factor, and a reflection toggle applied to y=x², animating the transformation step-by-step and displaying the resulting vertex-form equation.

#### Unit 3: حل المعادلات — Solving Equations

##### Lesson 3.1: حل المعادلات التربيعية بيانيًا — Solving Quadratic Equations Graphically
- **Objective:** I solve a quadratic equation graphically.
- **Key terms:** المعادلة التربيعية (quadratic equation), جذور المعادلة (roots of the equation), أصفار الاقتران (zeros of the function)
- **What's taught:** Links a quadratic equation ax²+bx+c=0 to its associated quadratic function f(x)=ax²+bx+c, and shows that the equation's roots are the x-intercepts (zeros) of the function's graph; applied to finding how long a dolphin stays above water using h(t)=−5t²+10t.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A parabola grapher where dragging the curve (via coefficient sliders) highlights its x-axis crossings live and labels them as the equation's roots, reinforcing the graph-to-equation link.

##### Lesson 3.2: حل المعادلات التربيعية بالتحليل — Solving Quadratic Equations by Factoring
- **Objective:** I solve quadratic equations algebraically by factoring.
- **Key terms:** خاصية الضرب الصفري (zero-product property)
- **What's taught:** Introduces the zero-product property (if a product of factors equals zero, at least one factor must be zero) as the algebraic tool for solving factorable quadratics, moving from the previous lesson's graphical method to an algebraic one; applied to a kangaroo-jump height model h(t)=−16t²+7t.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A factoring workbench where students drag terms to build the factored form and the tool visually confirms each factor's zero against the graph's x-intercepts from lesson 3.1.

##### Lesson 3.3: حل المعادلات التربيعية بإكمال المربّع — Solving Quadratic Equations by Completing the Square
- **Objective:** I solve quadratic equations by completing the square.
- **Key terms:** إكمال المربّع (completing the square)
- **What's taught:** Builds on solving (x+m)²=n by square roots, showing that x²+2mx+m² is the expanded form of the perfect square (x+m)², so any quadratic can be rewritten in that form by adding a constructed term, then solved by taking square roots; applied to a projectile height model h(t)=−5t²+8t+1.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A geometric "completing the square" area-model animation where a rectangle representing x²+2mx is physically rearranged and a small square of area m² is added to complete a perfect square, tying the algebra to a visual proof.

##### Lesson 3.4: حل المعادلة التربيعية باستعمال القانون العام — Solving Quadratic Equations Using the Quadratic Formula
- **Objective:** I solve a quadratic equation using the general (quadratic) formula.
- **Key terms:** القانون العام (the quadratic formula), المميّز (the discriminant)
- **What's taught:** Derives the quadratic formula from the completing-the-square method applied to the general form ax²+bx+c=0, then applies it directly to any quadratic without factoring; applied to a disc-throwing game modeled by f(x)=−0.04x²+0.84x+2, solving for horizontal landing distance. Introduces the discriminant as a way to predict the number of real solutions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A formula calculator where entering a, b, c auto-plugs into the quadratic formula step-by-step (discriminant computed first, then both roots), paired with a live parabola showing the roots as intercepts and how the discriminant sign predicts 0, 1, or 2 intersections.

#### Unit 4: الهندسة الإحداثية — Coordinate Geometry

##### Lesson 4.1: المسافة في المستوى الإحداثي — Distance in the Coordinate Plane
- **Objective:** I find the midpoint of a line segment in the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** المسافة (distance), الإحداثي (coordinate), نقطة المنتصف (midpoint)
- **What's taught:** Reviews the distance formula between two points (derived from the Pythagorean theorem applied to coordinates) and introduces the midpoint formula, averaging x- and y-coordinates; applied to determining whether a high-resolution camera can photograph a seagull within its range on a coordinate grid, and finding a city midpoint on a map with GeoGebra.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable two-point coordinate-plane tool that draws the connecting segment, right-triangle legs, and live-computes both distance (via Pythagorean overlay) and midpoint as either point moves.

##### Lesson 4.2: البعد بين نقطة ومستقيم — Distance Between a Point and a Line
- **Objective:** I find the distance between two parallel lines.
- **Key terms:** (builds on) البعد بين نقطة ومستقيم (distance between a point and a line)
- **What's taught:** Defines the distance from a point to a line as the length of the perpendicular segment from that point to the line (the shortest such distance), then extends this to finding the distance between two parallel lines by dropping a perpendicular between them; applied to finding the shortest jump distance across a stream modeled by two points/lines.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive plane where dragging a point off a line auto-draws candidate segments to the line and highlights the perpendicular one as shortest, with a numeric distance readout, extendable to two parallel lines.

### Semester 2

#### Unit 5: العلاقات في المثلّثات والنسب المثلّثية — Relationships in Triangles and Trigonometric Ratios

##### Lesson 5.1: الأجزاء المتناسبة في المثلّثات — Proportional Parts in Triangles
- **Objective:** I identify proportional parts in a triangle and use them to find unknown measures.
- **Key terms:** القطعة المنصّفة في المثلث (triangle midsegment/bisecting segment)
- **What's taught:** Uses similar-triangle relationships (from earlier grades) to show that a segment parallel to one side of a triangle divides the other two sides proportionally, and applies this to solve for unknown side lengths. Includes a GeoGebra lab extension exploring the "midsegment triangle."
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable triangle with a parallel cross-segment where sliding the segment's position live-updates the proportional side ratios and unknown-length calculations.

##### Lesson 5.2: منصّفات في المثلّث — Bisectors in a Triangle
- **Objective:** I identify angle bisectors, perpendicular bisectors, and their special points in a triangle.
- **Key terms:** المنصّف العمودي (perpendicular bisector), مركز الدائرة الخارجية للمثلث (circumcenter), مركز الدائرة الداخلية للمثلث (incenter)
- **What's taught:** Constructs perpendicular bisectors and angle bisectors of a triangle's sides/angles, showing they are concurrent at the circumcenter (equidistant from vertices, center of the circumscribed circle) and incenter (equidistant from sides, center of the inscribed circle) respectively.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A triangle where toggling perpendicular-bisector or angle-bisector construction on each side animates the concurrency point forming, then auto-draws the circumscribed or inscribed circle centered there.

##### Lesson 5.3: القطع المتوسّطة والارتفاعات في المثلّث — Medians and Altitudes in a Triangle
- **Objective:** I identify medians and altitudes in a triangle and their special points.
- **Key terms:** القطعة المتوسّطة للمثلث (median), مركز المثلث (centroid), ارتفاع المثلث (altitude), ملتقى الارتفاعات (orthocenter)
- **What's taught:** Defines medians (vertex to opposite midpoint, concurrent at the centroid, the triangle's balance point) and altitudes (vertex perpendicular to opposite side, concurrent at the orthocenter), distinguishing both from the bisectors of lesson 5.2. Includes a concept activity on medians.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A triangle-vertex-dragging tool that simultaneously animates medians converging at the centroid and altitudes converging at the orthocenter, letting students see how these points move (and can leave the triangle) as its shape changes.

##### Lesson 5.4: النسب المثلّثية — Trigonometric Ratios
- **Objective:** I identify sine, cosine, and tangent of an acute angle as ratios between the sides of a right triangle.
- **Key terms:** النسب المثلّثية (trigonometric ratios), الجيب (sine), جيب التمام (cosine), الظل (tangent), معكوس النسبة المثلّثية (inverse trig ratio), متطابقة فيثاغورس (Pythagorean identity)
- **What's taught:** Defines sin, cos, tan of an acute angle in a right triangle as ratios of opposite/hypotenuse, adjacent/hypotenuse, and opposite/adjacent respectively; introduces inverse trig ratios to find angle measures and the Pythagorean identity sin²θ+cos²θ=1. Preceded by a concept activity introducing trig ratios.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable right triangle with a fixed hypotenuse where changing the acute angle live-updates the three side ratios as numeric sin/cos/tan values, visually reinforcing which sides pair with which ratio.

##### Lesson 5.5: تطبيقات النسب المثلّثية — Applications of Trigonometric Ratios
- **Objective:** I use trigonometric ratios to find unknown measures in a right triangle.
- **Key terms:** زاوية الارتفاع (angle of elevation), زاوية الانخفاض (angle of depression)
- **What's taught:** Applies sine, cosine, tangent, and their inverses to real-world right-triangle problems involving angles of elevation and depression — e.g. finding heights or distances that can't be measured directly, such as building heights or distances to distant objects.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A scenario simulator (e.g. flagpole or cliff) where dragging an observer's position changes the angle of elevation/depression live and shows the trig calculation solving for the unknown height or distance.

#### Unit 6: المقادير الأُسّية والمقادير الجذرية — Exponential and Radical Expressions

##### Lesson 6.1: تبسيط المقادير الأُسّية — Simplifying Exponential Expressions
- **Objective:** I use properties of integer exponents to simplify exponential expressions.
- **What's taught:** Reviews and extends the laws of integer exponents (product, quotient, power, zero, and negative exponent rules) from earlier grades to simplify more complex exponential expressions, including those with variables. Includes a unit project connecting solids, exponential, and radical expressions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An exponent-rule matching tool where dragging expressions onto the correct rule (e.g. product-of-powers, power-of-a-power) animates the simplification step and shows the resulting simplified exponent.

##### Lesson 6.2: العمليات على المقادير الجذرية — Operations on Radical Expressions
- **Objective:** I perform operations on radical expressions.
- **Key terms:** المقادير الجذرية (radical expressions), إنطاق المقام (rationalizing the denominator), المرافق (conjugate)
- **What's taught:** Covers adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing radical (root) expressions, including simplifying radicals and rationalizing denominators using conjugates; applied to finding a triangle's height given its area, expressed as a radical.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step radical simplifier where students drag factors out from under a root symbol, with a rationalizing-the-denominator mode that shows multiplying by the conjugate to clear the radical from the denominator.

##### Lesson 6.3: حلّ المعادلات الجذرية — Solving Radical Equations
- **Objective:** I solve equations containing radical expressions.
- **Key terms:** المعادلات الجذرية (radical equations), الحلول الدخيلة (extraneous solutions)
- **What's taught:** Solves equations containing square roots by isolating the radical and squaring both sides, then stresses checking all solutions in the original equation to discard extraneous solutions introduced by squaring.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A solver that shows both candidate solutions plotted against the original radical function's graph, visually flagging which solution is extraneous (doesn't actually lie on the curve).

#### Unit 7: المقادير الجبرية النسبية — Rational Algebraic Expressions

##### Lesson 7.1: ضرب المقادير الجبرية النسبية وقسمتها — Multiplying and Dividing Rational Algebraic Expressions
- **Objective:** I multiply and divide rational algebraic expressions.
- **Key terms:** المقدار الجبري النسبي (rational algebraic expression), الكسر الجبري المركّب (complex algebraic fraction)
- **What's taught:** Extends fraction multiplication/division rules (multiply straight across; divide by multiplying by the reciprocal) to algebraic fractions, requiring factoring numerators/denominators first to simplify common factors; applied to a computer-screen rectangle-dimension problem.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A factor-and-cancel animation where matching factors in numerator and denominator visually "cancel" when a student drags them together, building intuition for why simplification works.

##### Lesson 7.2: جمع المقادير الجبرية النسبية وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Rational Algebraic Expressions
- **Objective:** I add and subtract rational algebraic expressions.
- **What's taught:** Extends fraction addition/subtraction (finding a common denominator, typically the LCD of the factored denominators) to algebraic fractions; applied to finding the glass surface area of an open-top fish tank expressed in terms of x.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A common-denominator builder where dragging factors into a shared denominator "slot" shows both fractions being rescaled simultaneously before combining.

##### Lesson 7.3: حلّ المعادلات النسبية — Solving Rational Equations
- **Objective:** I solve rational equations.
- **Key terms:** المعادلة النسبية (rational equation)
- **What's taught:** Solves equations containing rational (fractional algebraic) expressions by clearing denominators (multiplying through by the LCD), then checks for restricted values that would make any original denominator zero (excluded/extraneous solutions).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A rational-equation solver highlighting excluded domain values (where a denominator is zero) directly on a number line alongside the computed solution set, showing why those values must be rejected.

#### Unit 8: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

##### Lesson 8.1: مقاييس التشتّت — Measures of Dispersion
- **Objective:** I find and interpret the variance and standard deviation of a data set.
- **Key terms:** التباين (variance), الانحراف المعياري (standard deviation), تحويل البيانات (data transformation)
- **What's taught:** Introduces variance and standard deviation as measures of how spread out data is around the mean, building on the earlier interquartile-range measure from grade 8, and examines how shifting or scaling all data values (transformation) affects these measures.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live dot-plot where dragging individual data points recomputes and visually redraws the mean and a standard-deviation band around it, showing spread shrink/grow in real time.

##### Lesson 8.2: الجداول التكرارية ذات الفئات — Grouped Frequency Tables
- **Objective:** I estimate measures of central tendency for grouped frequency tables.
- **What's taught:** Covers organizing continuous data into class intervals within a grouped frequency table, and estimating mean, median, and mode from grouped (rather than raw individual) data using midpoint approximations, applied to timing children completing a puzzle.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A raw-data-to-grouped-table builder where students choose class-interval width and watch the frequency table and estimated central-tendency measures recompute live as the width changes.

##### Lesson 8.3: المدرّجات التكرارية — Frequency Histograms
- **Objective:** I represent continuous grouped data in frequency histograms.
- **Key terms:** المدرّجات التكرارية (frequency histograms), الكثافة التكرارية (frequency density)
- **What's taught:** Builds histograms for continuous grouped data with unequal class widths, introducing frequency density (frequency ÷ class width) as the bar-height measure so that bar area (not height) represents frequency correctly.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A histogram builder where changing class-interval widths automatically recalculates frequency density and bar heights, visually demonstrating why density (not raw frequency) must be used for unequal-width bars.

##### Lesson 8.4: الاحتمالات وأشكال ڤن — Probability and Venn Diagrams
- **Objective:** I find probability using Venn diagrams.
- **Key terms:** الحادث المتمّم (complementary event), الحوادث المتنافية (mutually exclusive events), الحوادث الشاملة (exhaustive events)
- **What's taught:** Uses Venn diagrams to represent sample spaces and events, computing probabilities of complements, unions, and intersections, and distinguishing mutually exclusive from overlapping events, and exhaustive event sets that cover the whole sample space.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive two-circle Venn diagram where students drag outcomes into regions (union, intersection, complement) and the tool live-computes each region's probability from the counts placed.

##### Lesson 8.5: الاحتمال الهندسي — Geometric Probability
- **Objective:** I find geometric probabilities using lengths, areas, and angles.
- **Key terms:** الاحتمالات الهندسية (geometric probabilities)
- **What's taught:** Extends probability beyond counting discrete outcomes to continuous geometric measures — computing the probability of a randomly chosen point/dart landing in a target region as the ratio of the target's length, area, or angle measure to the total length, area, or angle measure available.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dartboard-style random-point simulator where students design target regions of chosen area/angle and compare the theoretical geometric probability to the empirical hit-rate from many simulated random throws.

## Grade 10 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

### Semester 1

#### Unit 1: المعادلات — Equations

##### Lesson 1.1: حل معادلات خاصة — Solving Special Equations
- **Objective:** I solve special equations whose variable's exponent is a positive integer greater than 2.
- **Key terms:** الصورة التربيعية (quadratic-like form)
- **What's taught:** Extends factoring/quadratic-solving techniques to higher-degree equations (cubic and beyond) by recognizing quadratic-like structure (substitution to reduce degree) or factoring out common terms; applied to finding the dimensions of a gift box given its volume and width-dependent expressions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A substitution tool where students pick a variable substitution (e.g. u=x²) and watch a higher-degree equation visually collapse into a quadratic, then expand back to find all original solutions.

##### Lesson 1.2: حل نظام مكوّن من معادلة خطية ومعادلة تربيعية — Solving a System of a Linear and a Quadratic Equation
- **Objective:** I solve a system made of a linear equation and a quadratic equation.
- **What's taught:** Solves systems combining a line and a parabola using substitution, and interprets the number of solutions (0, 1, or 2) as the number of intersection points between the line and curve; applied to determining whether two roads (one straight, one curved) intersect within a city. Includes a GeoGebra lab for graphically solving systems of equations.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable line-and-parabola graph where moving the line live-updates its intersection points with the curve, cross-checked against the algebraic substitution solution.

##### Lesson 1.3: حل نظام مكوّن من معادلتين تربيعيتين — Solving a System of Two Quadratic Equations
- **Objective:** I solve a system made of two quadratic equations in two variables.
- **What's taught:** Extends the substitution/elimination approach to systems of two quadratics (two parabolas), finding intersection points; applied to a supply-and-demand economics model where two quadratic curves represent price/quantity relationships and their intersection is the market equilibrium point.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A two-parabola grapher with adjustable coefficients for both curves, highlighting intersection points live and linking them to the algebraic system's solution set — directly demonstrating the market-equilibrium interpretation.

#### Unit 2: الدائرة — The Circle

##### Lesson 2.1: أوتار الدائرة وأقطارها ومماساتها — Chords, Diameters, and Tangents of a Circle
- **Objective:** I identify a circle's chords, diameters, and tangents, their properties, and relationships between them; I use these to find unknown lengths and angle measures.
- **Key terms:** الدائرة (circle), مركز الدائرة (center), الوتر (chord), القوس (arc), القطر (diameter), نصف القطر (radius), المماس (tangent), نقطة التماس (point of tangency), القاطع (secant)
- **What's taught:** Establishes core circle vocabulary and key theorems: a radius perpendicular to a chord bisects it, a tangent line is perpendicular to the radius at the point of tangency, and relationships between chord lengths and their distances from the center; applied to finding the center of a circular table to mount an umbrella pole.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive circle where dragging a chord shows the perpendicular bisector from the center snapping into place, and dragging a tangent line demonstrates it staying perpendicular to the radius at the touch point.

##### Lesson 2.2: الأقواس والقطاعات الدائرية — Arcs and Sectors of a Circle
- **Objective:** I calculate arc length and the area of a circular sector, and solve related problems.
- **Key terms:** القوس (arc), القطاع (sector)
- **What's taught:** Derives arc-length and sector-area formulas as fractions of the full circumference/area proportional to the central angle (in degrees or radians); applied to finding the area of a pizza slice cut at a 45° angle.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A pie-slice tool where dragging the central angle live-recomputes and displays both the arc length and sector area, with a visual proportion bar showing the angle's fraction of the full 360°.

##### Lesson 2.3: الزوايا في الدائرة — Angles in a Circle
- **Objective:** I know the relationships between angles in a circle and use them to find unknown angles and solve real-life problems.
- **Key terms:** الزاوية المركزية (central angle), الزاوية المحيطية (inscribed angle), القوس المقابل (intercepted arc), الزاوية المقابلة لقطر الدائرة (angle subtending a diameter), الرباعي الدائري (cyclic quadrilateral), الزاوية المماسية (tangent-chord angle)
- **What's taught:** Covers the central angle–inscribed angle relationship (inscribed angle is half the central angle on the same arc), that an angle inscribed in a semicircle is a right angle, cyclic quadrilateral opposite-angle supplementary property, and tangent-chord angle measures; applied to a five-pointed star inscribed in a circle inside a square.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An inscribed-angle explorer where dragging a point around the circle's circumference keeps the inscribed angle constant for a fixed arc, visually proving the central/inscribed angle theorem, plus a cyclic-quadrilateral mode showing opposite angles always summing to 180°.

##### Lesson 2.4: معادلة الدائرة — The Equation of a Circle
- **Objective:** I write the equation of a circle and find its center and radius from a given circle equation.
- **Key terms:** معادلة الدائرة (equation of a circle), الصورة القياسية (standard form), الصورة العامة (general form)
- **What's taught:** Derives the standard-form circle equation (x−a)²+(y−b)²=r² from the distance formula, and shows converting between standard and general (expanded) form by completing the square; applied to determining whether a radio station's broadcast circle reaches a given house's coordinates.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A coordinate-plane tool where dragging a circle's center or resizing its radius live-updates both its standard-form and general-form equations, with a point-testing feature to check if a coordinate lies inside/on/outside the circle.

#### Unit 3: حسابات المثلثات — Trigonometric Calculations

##### Lesson 3.1: النسب المثلثية — Trigonometric Ratios
- **Objective:** I recognize the standard position of an angle, connect trigonometric ratios to the unit circle, and find them for quadrantal angles; I find the two remaining basic trig ratios given one.
- **Key terms:** ضلع الابتداء (initial side), ضلع الانتهاء (terminal side), الوضع القياسي (standard position), دائرة الوحدة (unit circle), الزاوية الربعية (quadrantal angle)
- **What's taught:** Extends trig ratios beyond acute angles in right triangles (grade 9) to any angle in standard position on the unit circle, defining sine and cosine as the y- and x-coordinates of the terminal side's intersection with the unit circle, and covers quadrantal angles (0°, 90°, 180°, 270°, 360°).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A unit-circle simulator where rotating the terminal side by dragging live-updates the (cos θ, sin θ) coordinates and displays the corresponding right-triangle projection onto the axes.

##### Lesson 3.2: النسب المثلثية للزوايا ضمن الدورة الواحدة — Trigonometric Ratios for Angles Within One Full Revolution
- **Objective:** I find the basic trigonometric ratios for any angle between 0° and 360°, and find the angle if one of its ratios is known.
- **Key terms:** الزاوية المرجعية (reference angle), معكوس النسبة المثلثية (inverse trig ratio)
- **What's taught:** Introduces the reference-angle technique for finding trig ratios of angles in any quadrant by relating them back to an equivalent acute angle, and covers using inverse trig functions to solve for angles when a ratio is known; applied to finding coordinates after rotating a terminal side by a given angle.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A quadrant-color-coded unit circle where dragging the terminal side into each quadrant shows the reference angle being formed with the x-axis and the resulting sign (+/−) of each trig ratio.

##### Lesson 3.3: تمثيل الاقترانات المثلثية — Representing Trigonometric Functions
- **Objective:** I graph trigonometric functions with domain in the interval [0°, 360°].
- **What's taught:** Plots y=sin x and y=cos x (and related functions) over one revolution, examining amplitude, period, and key points (maxima, minima, zeros); applied to modeling water depth at a harbor entrance over time as a sine function.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live grapher where dragging a point around the unit circle simultaneously traces out the corresponding sine/cosine curve point by point, linking the circular and graphical representations.

##### Lesson 3.4: حل المعادلات المثلثية — Solving Trigonometric Equations
- **Objective:** I solve equations involving basic trig ratios, with solution sets restricted to one revolution.
- **Key terms:** المعادلة المثلثية (trigonometric equation)
- **What's taught:** Solves equations like sin x = k or cos x = k for x within one revolution (0°–360°) using reference angles and knowledge of which quadrants give positive/negative values for each ratio; applied to a wall clock problem finding when a clock hand's tip is a given distance from the ceiling, modeled by d=−60cos(30x)+110.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A trig-equation solver overlaying the target horizontal line k on the sine/cosine curve from lesson 3.3, visually showing all solution intersections within the one-revolution window.

#### Unit 4: تطبيقات المثلثات — Applications of Trigonometry

##### Lesson 4.1: الاتجاه من الشمال — Bearing (Direction from North)
- **Objective:** I interpret bearing and find it for a point relative to another point by drawing, measurement, and calculation using angle relationships.
- **Key terms:** الاتجاه من الشمال (bearing)
- **What's taught:** Defines bearing as the clockwise angle from geographic north to a direction line, and uses angle relationships (e.g. alternate angles, supplementary angles) to compute return bearings and relative directions; applied to finding the return bearing of a plane's flight path given its outbound bearing.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A compass-rose map tool where dragging a destination point live-computes and displays the bearing angle from the origin, with a toggle to show the reverse/return bearing.

##### Lesson 4.2: قانون الجيوب — The Law of Sines
- **Objective:** I use the law of sines to find a side length or angle measure in a triangle where two sides and an opposite angle, or two angles and a side, are known.
- **Key terms:** حل المثلث (solving the triangle), قانون الجيوب (law of sines)
- **What's taught:** Extends right-triangle trigonometry (grade 9) to general (oblique) triangles via the law of sines (a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C), including discussion of the ambiguous SSA case; applied to determining triangle measurements between three cities (Jerash, Zarqa, Madaba) on a map given partial side/angle data.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex triangle where entering any two sides and a non-included angle (or two angles and a side) auto-solves and labels all remaining sides/angles via the law of sines, flagging ambiguous-case scenarios with two possible triangles.

##### Lesson 4.3: قانون جيوب التمام — The Law of Cosines
- **Objective:** I use the law of cosines to find a side length or angle measure in a triangle.
- **Key terms:** قانون جيوب التمام (law of cosines)
- **What's taught:** Introduces the law of cosines (c²=a²+b²−2ab·cos C) for solving triangles when the law of sines doesn't directly apply (SAS or SSS cases); applied to finding the distance between two buses that depart from the same station on paths at a known angle to each other.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A triangle solver where students input two sides and the included angle (SAS) and watch the law-of-cosines calculation build the third side step by step, compared against a law-of-sines solver to show when each applies.

##### Lesson 4.4: استعمال جيب الزاوية لإيجاد مساحة المثلث — Using the Sine of an Angle to Find the Area of a Triangle
- **Objective:** I find the area of a triangle given two side lengths and the included angle.
- **What's taught:** Derives the area formula Area=½ab·sin C from the standard base-height triangle-area formula by expressing height in terms of sin C; applied to computing the potato-planting yield needed for a triangular field given two sides and the included angle.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A triangle-area tool where dragging the included angle between two fixed-length sides live-updates the triangle's shape, height, and computed area via the ½ab·sin C formula.

##### Lesson 4.5: حل مسائل ثلاثية الأبعاد — Solving Three-Dimensional Problems
- **Objective:** I find unknown lengths and angle measures in three-dimensional figures using the Pythagorean theorem and trigonometric ratios.
- **What's taught:** Combines the Pythagorean theorem and trig ratios (including the law of sines/cosines) across multiple 2D cross-sections of a 3D solid to solve for lengths and angles that can't be found directly; applied to computing the height of the Great Pyramid of Giza given its base side length and the slant edge length.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A 3D pyramid/prism model that students can rotate and slice into 2D right-triangle cross-sections, with each slice's trig calculation displayed as it's isolated from the solid.

### Semester 2

#### Unit 5: الاقترانات — Functions

##### Lesson 5.1: اقترانات كثيرات الحدود — Polynomial Functions
- **Objective:** I identify polynomial functions, graph them, and perform addition, subtraction, and multiplication on polynomials.
- **Key terms:** وحيد الحد (monomial), كثير الحدود (polynomial), المعامل الرئيس (leading coefficient), الدرجة (degree), الصورة القياسية لكثير الحدود (standard form of a polynomial)
- **What's taught:** Defines polynomial functions by degree and leading coefficient, covers end-behavior/shape patterns by degree, and performs polynomial addition, subtraction, and multiplication, extending prior algebraic-expression operations to full polynomial functions and their graphs.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A polynomial-builder where adjusting degree and leading coefficient live-reshapes the graph's end behavior and turning points, alongside a term-by-term animation of polynomial addition/multiplication.

##### Lesson 5.2: الاقترانات النسبية — Rational Functions
- **Objective:** I divide one polynomial function by another, identify rational functions, and find their domain and range.
- **Key terms:** المقدار الجبري النسبي (rational algebraic expression), الاقتران النسبي (rational function), خط التقارب الأفقي (horizontal asymptote)
- **What's taught:** Introduces polynomial long division and rational functions (ratio of two polynomials), determining domain restrictions (excluded values where the denominator is zero) and horizontal/vertical asymptote behavior.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A rational-function grapher where dragging the numerator/denominator polynomial coefficients live-updates the curve, with asymptotes highlighted as dashed lines and excluded domain points marked with open circles.

##### Lesson 5.3: تركيب الاقترانات — Composition of Functions
- **Objective:** I recognize the concept of a composite function, the condition for composing two functions, evaluate it at a given value, and find its rule.
- **Key terms:** تركيب الاقترانات (function composition), الاقتران المركّب (composite function), المركّبتان (the two component functions)
- **What's taught:** Defines function composition (f∘g)(x)=f(g(x)), the domain condition required for composition to be valid, and procedures for evaluating and finding the algebraic rule of a composite function from two given functions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A two-stage "function machine" where input values visibly pass through function g then function f, with each stage's output shown before feeding into the next, building intuition for composition order.

##### Lesson 5.4: الاقتران العكسي — The Inverse Function
- **Objective:** I recognize the inverse function, find it, and determine its domain and range.
- **Key terms:** العلاقة العكسية (inverse relation), الاقتران العكسي (inverse function), اقتران واحد لواحد (one-to-one function), اختبار الخط الأفقي (horizontal line test), الاقتران المحايد (identity function)
- **What's taught:** Defines the inverse of a function as reflecting input/output pairs, the one-to-one requirement for an inverse to itself be a function (verified via horizontal line test), procedures for algebraically finding f⁻¹(x), and that f and f⁻¹ are reflections of each other across y=x.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A graph where a function and its algebraically-derived inverse are plotted together with the line y=x shown as a mirror line, and dragging points on one curve shows the reflected point appearing on the inverse curve.

##### Lesson 5.5: المتتاليات — Sequences
- **Objective:** I derive the general term rule for quadratic and cubic sequences.
- **Key terms:** المتتالية (sequence), الحد (term), الحد العام (general term)
- **What's taught:** Extends linear sequences (prior grades) to sequences whose term-to-term differences themselves form a pattern (second differences constant → quadratic sequence; third differences constant → cubic sequence), and derives the general term formula from these difference patterns.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sequence-difference table builder where entering terms auto-computes first/second/third differences, visually revealing whether the underlying rule is linear, quadratic, or cubic before the general formula is derived.

#### Unit 6: المشتقات — Derivatives

##### Lesson 6.1: تقدير ميل المنحنى — Estimating the Slope of a Curve
- **Objective:** I estimate the slope of a curve at a point.
- **Key terms:** السرعة المتجهة اللحظية (instantaneous velocity), التسارع اللحظي (instantaneous acceleration)
- **What's taught:** Introduces the idea of a curve's slope at a single point (unlike a line's constant slope) by estimating it via the slope of a secant line through nearby points that approaches a tangent line, connecting this to instantaneous velocity/acceleration as rates of change. Preceded by a GeoGebra lab exploring tangent-line slope.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A secant-line animation where two points on a curve are dragged closer together, visually converging the secant line into the tangent line while the calculated slope value updates toward the true instantaneous slope.

##### Lesson 6.2: الاشتقاق — Differentiation
- **Objective:** I find the derivative of polynomial functions.
- **Key terms:** المشتقة (the derivative)
- **What's taught:** Formalizes the slope-estimation idea from 6.1 into the derivative as a limit of the difference quotient, and derives/applies the power rule for differentiating polynomial terms (d/dx[xⁿ]=nxⁿ⁻¹) plus sum/difference rules for full polynomials.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A function-and-derivative dual-graph view where dragging a point along f(x) shows the tangent line's slope being plotted simultaneously as a point on the derivative curve f'(x), building the "derivative as slope-tracker" intuition.

##### Lesson 6.3: القيم العظمى والقيم الصغرى — Maximum and Minimum Values
- **Objective:** I find local maximum and minimum values of polynomial functions.
- **Key terms:** نقطة حرجة (critical point), قيمة عظمى (maximum value), قيمة صغرى (minimum value)
- **What's taught:** Uses the derivative from 6.2 to locate critical points (where the derivative equals zero or is undefined) and classifies them as local maxima or minima by examining the sign of the derivative on either side; applied to the unit's box-volume-maximization project.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A curve with a draggable point where the sign of the derivative (positive/negative, shown as an arrow or color) updates live, and critical points auto-highlight with a label of "local max" or "local min" once found.

#### Unit 7: المتجهات — Vectors

##### Lesson 7.1: المتجهات في المستوى الإحداثي — Vectors in the Coordinate Plane
- **Objective:** I recognize vectors, represent them in the coordinate plane, and find their magnitude.
- **Key terms:** المركّبة الأفقية (horizontal component), المركّبة الرأسية (vertical component), الصورة الإحداثية (coordinate form), الوضع القياسي (standard position), متّجه الموقع (position vector), مقدار المتجه (magnitude of a vector)
- **What's taught:** Introduces vectors as quantities with both magnitude and direction, representing them as coordinate pairs/arrows in the plane, decomposing into horizontal/vertical components, and computing magnitude via the Pythagorean theorem/distance formula.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable vector arrow on a coordinate grid where moving its tip live-updates its horizontal/vertical components and computed magnitude, with a toggle to view it in standard position from the origin.

##### Lesson 7.2: جمع المتجهات وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Vectors
- **Objective:** I perform operations on vectors.
- **Key terms:** المتجهات المتساوية (equal vectors), المتجهات المتوازية (parallel vectors), معكوس المتجه (inverse/opposite vector), المحصّلة (resultant), المتجه الصفري (zero vector)
- **What's taught:** Covers vector addition and subtraction both graphically (tip-to-tail / parallelogram method) and algebraically (component-wise), and introduces the resultant vector, equal/parallel vector conditions, and the zero vector.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A tip-to-tail vector adder where dragging two vectors' arrows auto-draws the resultant vector, with a synced numeric panel confirming the component-wise sum matches the graphical result.

##### Lesson 7.3: الضرب القياسي — The Scalar (Dot) Product
- **Objective:** I multiply vectors and find the angle between two vectors.
- **Key terms:** الضرب القياسي (scalar/dot product)
- **What's taught:** Defines the dot product of two vectors both algebraically (component-wise) and geometrically (|a||b|cos θ), and uses it to find the angle between two vectors and to test for perpendicularity (dot product = 0).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A two-vector tool where dragging either vector live-computes the dot product and the angle between them, with a visual indicator flashing when the vectors become perpendicular (dot product crosses zero).

#### Unit 8: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

##### Lesson 8.1: أشكال الانتشار — Scatter Plots
- **Objective:** I understand and describe scatter plots, and use the line of best fit to estimate one variable's value from the other.
- **Key terms:** شكل الانتشار (scatter plot), الارتباط الموجب (positive correlation), الارتباط السالب (negative correlation), المستقيم الأفضل مطابقة (line of best fit)
- **What's taught:** Introduces scatter plots for paired data, describing correlation strength/direction (positive, negative, none), and drawing/using a line of best fit to make estimates (interpolation) for one variable given the other. Includes a GeoGebra lab for drawing the line of best fit.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A scatter-plot builder where students place data points and drag a trial line, getting live feedback (e.g. sum-of-residuals or a fit-quality indicator) on how close their line is to the best fit.

##### Lesson 8.2: المنحنى التكراري التراكمي — The Cumulative Frequency Curve
- **Objective:** I identify quartiles and percentiles, and find them for data grouped in frequency tables using the cumulative frequency curve.
- **Key terms:** المنحنى التراكمي (cumulative frequency curve), المئينات (percentiles)
- **What's taught:** Builds a cumulative frequency (ogive) curve from a grouped frequency table by plotting running totals, then uses it to read off quartiles and percentiles graphically, extending the median/quartile work from grade 9's dispersion measures.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A grouped-data-to-ogive builder where changing class frequencies redraws the cumulative curve live, with draggable horizontal guide lines that read off quartile/percentile values at their intersection with the curve.

##### Lesson 8.3: مقاييس التشتت للجداول التكرارية ذات الفئات — Measures of Dispersion for Grouped Frequency Tables
- **Objective:** I find measures of dispersion for data organized in grouped frequency tables.
- **What's taught:** Extends variance/standard deviation calculation (grade 9) to grouped/binned data, using class midpoints as estimates for computing the mean and spread measures from a frequency table rather than raw individual values.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A grouped-table calculator where adjusting bin boundaries live-recomputes the estimated mean, variance, and standard deviation, showing how binning choices affect the dispersion estimate.

##### Lesson 8.4: احتمالات الحوادث المتنافية — Probabilities of Mutually Exclusive Events
- **Objective:** I calculate probabilities of mutually exclusive and non-mutually-exclusive events, and of an event's complement.
- **Key terms:** الحادث البسيط (simple event), الحادث المركّب (compound event), الحادثان المتنافيان (mutually exclusive events)
- **What's taught:** Formalizes the addition rule for probability: P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B) for mutually exclusive events, and P(A or B)=P(A)+P(B)−P(A and B) for overlapping events, plus the complement rule P(not A)=1−P(A).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A Venn-diagram probability calculator (extending grade 9's Venn work) where toggling event overlap on/off shows the addition-rule formula adjusting automatically to include or exclude the double-counted intersection term.

##### Lesson 8.5: احتمالات الحوادث المستقلة والحوادث غير المستقلة — Probabilities of Independent and Dependent Events
- **Objective:** I distinguish independent from dependent events and calculate their probabilities.
- **Key terms:** الحوادث المستقلة (independent events), الحوادث غير المستقلة (dependent events), الاحتمال المشروط (conditional probability), جداول الاتجاهين (two-way tables)
- **What's taught:** Distinguishes independent events (P(A and B)=P(A)×P(B)) from dependent events requiring conditional probability P(B|A), and uses two-way frequency tables to organize and compute these probabilities from real data sets.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive two-way table where clicking a row/column subset live-computes and displays the resulting conditional probability, paired with a tree diagram showing branch probabilities multiplying for dependent vs. independent scenarios.
