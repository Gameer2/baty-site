# Grade 8 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

## Semester 1

### Unit 1: الأعداد الحقيقية — Real Numbers

#### Lesson 1.1: الجذور التربيعية — Square Roots
- **Objective:** I find the square root of a number, and use it to solve real-life problems.
- **Key terms:** الجذر التربيعي (square root), الجذر التربيعي الرئيس (principal square root), المجذور (radicand)
- **What's taught:** Every positive number has two square roots (positive/principal and negative), notated ±√ with the radicand under the root. Students find principal and both square roots of whole numbers/decimals, and solve equations of the form x²=c by taking ±√c and verifying by substitution.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive "unsquare" tool where dragging a square's area slider shows its side length update live, plus a step-by-step equation solver visually splitting x²=c into ±√c with a substitution check.

#### Lesson 1.2: الجذور الصمّاء — Irrational (Surd) Roots
- **Objective:** I estimate the value of a square root.
- **Key terms:** الجذور الصمّاء (surds)
- **What's taught:** Surds (roots with no exact decimal value, e.g. √3) are distinguished from roots of perfect squares; students estimate a surd's value using a number line (bounding it between consecutive perfect squares) and a calculator, applied to a wave-speed physics formula.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable number-line tool that snaps a surd between its bounding perfect squares and compares the estimate to a live calculator readout.

#### Lesson 1.3: نظرية فيثاغورس — Pythagorean Theorem
- **Objective:** I use the Pythagorean theorem to find an unknown side length in a right triangle.
- **Key terms:** نظرية فيثاغورس (Pythagorean theorem), الوتر (hypotenuse), الساقان (legs)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity, c²=a²+b² is applied to find a missing leg or hypotenuse by solving the resulting equation and taking the square root (rejecting the negative root since lengths are positive).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A right-triangle tool where dragging two leg lengths (or a leg and hypotenuse) live-computes the third side and overlays the a², b², c² area squares.

#### Lesson 1.4: الأعداد الحقيقية — Real Numbers
- **Objective:** I distinguish between rational and irrational numbers.
- **Key terms:** العدد غير النسبي (irrational number), العدد الحقيقي (real number)
- **What's taught:** An irrational number cannot be written as a/b (integers, b≠0); students classify numbers as rational/irrational, applied to a real slope-length context (skate ramp base length) where a computed value must be tested for rationality.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive nested Venn diagram (naturals→integers→rationals→reals with irrationals branching off) where clicking a number sorts it into place.

#### Lesson 1.5: النسبة المئوية — Percentage
- **Objective:** I solve real-life problems involving percentages.
- **Key terms:** النسبة المئوية للتغير (percentage change), نسبة الزيادة/النقصان المئوية (percentage increase/decrease), النسبة المئوية العكسية (reverse percentage)
- **What's taught:** Percentages compare a number to 100 (over 100% if the number exceeds 100, under 1% if less than 1). Applied to olive-oil production year-over-year change (119% of a prior year's total), extending to percentage increase/decrease and reverse-percentage problems (finding an original value from a final percentage-adjusted one).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider-driven bar/strip diagram showing an "original" bar stretching or shrinking to a "new" bar as the percentage changes, with the change formula updating live.

### Unit 2: تحليل المقادير الجبرية — Factoring Algebraic Expressions

#### Lesson 2.1: التحليل بإخراج العامل المشترك الأكبر — Factoring by GCF
- **Objective:** I factor algebraic expressions by extracting the greatest common factor.
- **Key terms:** الصورة التحليلية (factored form), التحليل (factoring), التجميع (grouping)
- **What's taught:** The GCF concept extends from numbers to algebraic terms (shared numeric and variable factors), factored out via the distributive property in reverse. Applied to finding an unknown screen dimension from an area expression, and to grouping-based factoring for four-term expressions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive area-model rectangle where dragging out a common "width" factor from multiple terms visually reveals the remaining factor as the rectangle's other side.

#### Lesson 2.2: تحليل ثلاثيات الحدود x²+bx+c — Factoring Trinomials x²+bx+c
- **Objective:** I factor trinomials of the form x²+bx+c.
- **What's taught:** Since (x+m)(x+n) = x²+(m+n)x+mn, factoring reverses this: find two numbers whose product is c and sum is b, giving the binomial factors. Applied to finding a greenhouse's unknown side length from its area expression.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An area-model rectangle split into four labeled regions (x², mx, nx, mn) that a student rearranges to match a given trinomial, discovering m and n by trial.

#### Lesson 2.3: تحليل ثلاثيات الحدود ax²+bx+c — Factoring Trinomials ax²+bx+c
- **Objective:** I factor trinomials of the form ax²+bx+c where a≠1.
- **What's taught:** Find two numbers whose product is a×c and sum is b, using their signs (matching c's sign to determine whether both factors share or differ in sign, and b's sign to determine which), then use those numbers to split the middle term and factor by grouping.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A guided factor-pair search table where students test candidate number pairs against the required product and sum, with the tool marking a match and auto-completing the grouping.

#### Lesson 2.4: حالات خاصة من التحليل — Special Cases of Factoring
- **Objective:** I factor an expression representing a difference of two squares; I factor a perfect-square trinomial.
- **What's taught:** Two identities are applied in reverse to factor quickly without the general method: a²−b² = (a+b)(a−b) (difference of squares), and a²±2ab+b² = (a±b)² (perfect square trinomial) — applied to a physics expression for pressure difference across an airplane wing (½dv²−½du²).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "notched square" animation showing a²−b² as a large square with a smaller square removed, rearranging into an (a+b)×(a−b) rectangle to justify the identity visually.

### Unit 3: المعادلات الخطية بمتغيرين — Linear Equations in Two Variables

#### Lesson 3.1: المعادلة الخطية بالصورة القياسية — The Linear Equation in Standard Form
- **Objective:** I identify a linear equation, and write it in standard form.
- **What's taught:** A linear equation's standard form is Ax+By=C; students rearrange equations (like y=6−5x) into this form and determine whether a given equation qualifies as linear, applied to reading a water-draining tank's rate/intercept from a line graph.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live equation-rearranger where dragging terms between sides shows the equation morphing into standard form step by step.

#### Lesson 3.2: ميل المستقيم — Slope of a Line
- **Objective:** I find the slope of a line.
- **What's taught:** Slope can be positive, negative, zero, or undefined depending on the line's direction (visualized as "walking" left-to-right along it); computed from rise/run between two points or read from a graph.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable two-point line tool with a live rise/run triangle overlay and slope value, letting students drag toward horizontal/vertical to see zero and undefined slope emerge.

#### Lesson 3.3: معادلة المستقيم بصيغة الميل والمقطع — Equation of a Line in Slope-Intercept Form
- **Objective:** I write and use the equation of a line in slope-intercept form.
- **What's taught:** y=mx+b, where m is the slope and b the y-intercept; students write this form from a graph or given slope/intercept, and graph a line from its equation.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Slider controls for m and b that redraw the line live, visually separating the two parameters' individual effects (tilt vs. vertical shift).

#### Lesson 3.4: معادلة المستقيم بصيغة الميل ونقطة — Equation of a Line in Point-Slope Form
- **Objective:** I write the equation of a line given its slope and a point on it.
- **What's taught:** y−y₁=m(x−x₁) constructs a line's equation from one known point and the slope, useful when the y-intercept isn't directly known; convertible to slope-intercept or standard form.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable point-and-slope-handle tool where moving either live-rewrites the point-slope equation and redraws the line.

#### Lesson 3.5: المستقيمات المتوازية والمتعامدة — Parallel and Perpendicular Lines
- **Objective:** I write the equation of a line parallel or perpendicular to a given line through a given point.
- **What's taught:** Parallel lines share the same slope; perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals. Applied to a computer-controlled drill needing a path perpendicular to a marble surface line through a specified point.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive tool where dragging a point and toggling parallel/perpendicular mode auto-generates and draws the corresponding line relative to a fixed reference line, with slopes labeled live.

### Unit 4: المثلثات المتطابقة — Congruent Triangles

#### Lesson 4.1: تطابق المثلثات (SSS, SAS, HL) — Triangle Congruence (SSS, SAS, HL)
- **Objective:** I prove triangle congruence using SSS and SAS; I prove right-triangle congruence using HL.
- **Key terms:** المسلمة (postulate), النظرية (theorem), البرهان (proof), البرهان السهمي (flow proof), البرهان ذو العمودين (two-column proof), الزاوية المحصورة (included angle)
- **What's taught:** Formal introduction to postulates, theorems, and two proof formats (two-column and flow/arrow proofs). SSS (three sides match), SAS (two sides and the included angle match), and HL (hypotenuse-leg, for right triangles only) are used to prove two triangles congruent, applied to a tree-staking wire-support real-world setup.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-to-match triangle overlay tool where matching marked sides/angles between two triangles highlights which congruence postulate applies as pieces align.

#### Lesson 4.2: تطابق المثلثات (ASA, AAS) — Triangle Congruence (ASA, AAS)
- **Objective:** I prove triangle congruence using ASA and AAS.
- **What's taught:** ASA (two angles and the included side match) and AAS (two angles and a non-included side match) extend the congruence toolkit from lesson 4.1, applied through further two-column/flow proofs.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** The same overlay-matching tool extended to angle-side combinations, with a "which postulate applies" quiz mode across all five criteria (SSS/SAS/ASA/AAS/HL).

#### Lesson 4.3: المثلثات المتطابقة الضلعين والمثلثات المتطابقة الأضلاع — Isosceles and Equilateral Triangles
- **Objective:** I use properties of isosceles and equilateral triangles to solve problems.
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a hands-on activity (folding a paper isosceles triangle along its axis of symmetry) demonstrating that the two base angles are congruent; extended to equilateral triangles (all three angles congruent, each 60°). Students apply these properties in proofs and to solve for unknown angles/sides.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A fold-simulation animation matching the textbook's own paper-folding activity, plus a draggable isosceles triangle where the base angles stay locked equal as the apex angle changes.

## Semester 2

### Unit 5: المتباينات الخطية — Linear Inequalities

#### Lesson 5.1: كتابة المتباينات وتمثيلها — Writing and Representing Inequalities
- **Objective:** I recognize an inequality, and represent it on a number line.
- **Key terms:** المتباينة (inequality), حل المتباينة (solution of an inequality)
- **What's taught:** An inequality compares two quantities using <, >, ≤, or ≥; students translate real statements (a speed-camera's enforced limit) into inequality symbols and represent solution sets as shaded rays on a number line (open vs. closed circles for strict vs. inclusive bounds).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line tool where selecting an inequality symbol live-shades the correct region with the correct circle style (open/filled) at the boundary.

#### Lesson 5.2: حل المتباينات بالجمع والطرح — Solving Inequalities by Addition and Subtraction
- **Objective:** I solve one-step inequalities using addition or subtraction.
- **What's taught:** Adding or subtracting the same value from both sides of an inequality preserves the inequality direction, mirroring equation-solving, with solutions represented on a number line.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A balance-scale-style animator (tilted, not level, to represent inequality) that stays consistently tilted as equal amounts are added/removed from both sides.

#### Lesson 5.3: حل المتباينات بالضرب والقسمة — Solving Inequalities by Multiplication and Division
- **Objective:** I solve one-step inequalities using multiplication or division.
- **What's taught:** Multiplying or dividing both sides by a positive number preserves the inequality direction; doing so by a negative number flips it — the key rule distinguishing inequalities from equations.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sign-toggle demo where multiplying a sample inequality by a slider value (positive or negative) shows the inequality arrow flip in real time exactly when the multiplier crosses zero.

#### Lesson 5.4: حل المتباينات متعددة الخطوات — Solving Multi-Step Inequalities
- **Objective:** I solve inequalities requiring more than one step.
- **What's taught:** Combines the addition/subtraction and multiplication/division rules in sequence to isolate the variable, applied to a real weight-loss-rate problem (solving for number of weeks needed to reach a target).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step inequality solver highlighting each transformation applied to both sides, flagging the moment (if any) a sign-flip is triggered by a negative multiplier/divisor.

### Unit 6: أنظمة المعادلات الخطية — Systems of Linear Equations

#### Lesson 6.1: حل نظام من معادلتين خطيتين بيانيًّا — Solving a System of Two Linear Equations Graphically
- **Objective:** I solve a system of two linear equations by graphing.
- **Key terms:** نظام المعادلات الخطية (system of linear equations)
- **What's taught:** A system is two or more linear equations sharing the same variables; graphing both lines and finding their intersection point gives the solution, applied to finding when two trees growing at different constant rates reach the same height. A GeoGebra lab activity accompanies this lesson for graphing systems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual-line grapher where adjusting each equation's slope/intercept live-updates the intersection point, directly visualizing the system's solution.

#### Lesson 6.2: حل نظام من معادلتين خطيتين بالتعويض — Solving a System of Two Linear Equations by Substitution
- **Objective:** I solve a system of two linear equations by substitution.
- **What's taught:** Solve one equation for one variable, substitute that expression into the other equation to solve for the remaining variable, then back-substitute to find the first.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-by-step substitution animator showing an expression "swapping into" the second equation in place of the solved variable.

#### Lesson 6.3: حل نظام من معادلتين خطيتين بالحذف — Solving a System of Two Linear Equations by Elimination
- **Objective:** I solve a system of two linear equations by elimination.
- **What's taught:** Multiply one or both equations by constants (if needed) so a variable's coefficients match or are opposites, then add or subtract the equations to eliminate that variable, solve for the remaining one, and back-substitute.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A side-by-side equation-stacking tool where scaling one equation by a chosen multiplier live-shows matching coefficients align before the elimination (add/subtract) step executes.

### Unit 7: الأشكال ثنائية الأبعاد — Two-Dimensional Shapes

#### Lesson 7.1: إثبات توازي المستقيمات وتعامدها — Proving Lines Parallel and Perpendicular
- **Objective:** I identify parallel lines based on relationships between angle pairs formed by two lines cut by a transversal.
- **What's taught:** Reviews the parallel-line angle postulates/theorems (corresponding, alternate interior, co-interior, alternate exterior) from grade 7, then proves and applies their *converses*: congruent corresponding angles, congruent alternate angles, or supplementary co-interior angles each imply the two lines are parallel. Also covers the perpendicular-transversal theorem and its converse. Applied to a ladder's rungs and side supports.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive transversal-and-two-lines diagram where dragging one line's angle updates all 8 angles live, letting students toggle which angle-pair relationship forces parallelism and see it confirmed instantly.

#### Lesson 7.2: متوازي الأضلاع — Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I identify the properties of a parallelogram's sides, angles, and diagonals.
- **What's taught:** From the definition (opposite sides parallel), students derive and apply theorems: opposite sides congruent, opposite angles congruent, consecutive angles supplementary, diagonals bisect each other — used in two-column/flow proofs and to solve for unknown sides/angles.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex parallelogram where manipulating one vertex shows opposite sides/angles/diagonal segments update simultaneously while always staying equal.

#### Lesson 7.3: تمييز متوازي الأضلاع — Identifying (Proving) a Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I determine the conditions that confirm a quadrilateral is a parallelogram.
- **What's taught:** Teaches the converses of lesson 7.2's theorems as a multi-way test: a quadrilateral is a parallelogram if both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, or both pairs of opposite sides are congruent, or both pairs of opposite angles are congruent, or the diagonals bisect each other, or one pair of sides is both parallel and congruent.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A checklist-style interactive quadrilateral where students pick which condition to test on a draggable shape, with the tool visually confirming/denying parallelogram status.

#### Lesson 7.4: حالات خاصة من متوازي الأضلاع — Special Cases of a Parallelogram
- **Objective:** I determine the properties of the rectangle, rhombus, and square, and classify a given parallelogram.
- **What's taught:** Adds distinguishing theorems: a parallelogram is a rectangle iff its diagonals are congruent; a rhombus iff its diagonals are perpendicular (and iff each diagonal bisects the angles it connects); a square is both. Presented with a Venn-diagram hierarchy of parallelogram/rectangle/rhombus/square.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A single draggable parallelogram with live diagonal-length and diagonal-angle readouts that visually "snaps" a classification label as it's dragged toward each special condition.

#### Lesson 7.5: تشابه المثلثات — Similar Triangles
- **Objective:** I identify similar triangles using the AA, SSS, and SAS similarity criteria.
- **What's taught:** Extends grade 7's similar-polygon definition to formal triangle-similarity shortcuts: AA (two matching angle pairs suffice), SSS similarity (all three side ratios equal), SAS similarity (two side ratios equal with the included angle congruent). Applied to indirect-measurement problems like finding a house facade's height.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A pair of triangles where resizing/rotating one overlays matching side-ratio labels and angle-arc highlights in real time, showing AA/SSS/SAS conditions being satisfied or broken.

#### Lesson 7.6: التمدّد — Dilation
- **Objective:** I draw the image of a polygon under a dilation in the coordinate plane.
- **Key terms:** التمدّد (dilation), مركز التمدّد (center of dilation), معامل التمدّد (scale factor)
- **What's taught:** A dilation scales a figure from a fixed center by a scale factor k (k>1 enlargement, 0<k<1 reduction); for dilations centered at the origin, (x,y)→(kx,ky). Applied to redrawing a kite shape after a 2.5-factor enlargement in the coordinate plane.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A coordinate-plane tool with a scale-factor slider and draggable center point where a polygon's dilated image redraws live as k changes.

### Unit 8: الأشكال ثلاثية الأبعاد — Three-Dimensional Shapes

#### Lesson 8.1: رسم الأشكال ثلاثية الأبعاد — Drawing Three-Dimensional Shapes
- **Objective:** I draw 3D shapes using isometric drawing and orthographic views.
- **Key terms:** الرسم المتساوي (isometric drawing), المسقط العلوي/الأمامي/الجانبي (top/front/side projection view)
- **What's taught:** Isometric drawing represents a 3D shape on 2D triangular/dotted paper; students draw and interpret unit-cube structures this way, and separately produce top/front/side orthographic views of the same solid, plus count the unit cubes composing a given structure.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A 3D cube-stacking builder synced to a live isometric-grid drawing and to auto-generated top/front/side view panels, so students see all representations of the same solid update together.

#### Lesson 8.2: المقاطع والمجسّمات الدورانية — Cross-Sections and Solids of Revolution
- **Objective:** I identify the cross-section resulting from a plane intersecting a solid; I recognize solids formed by rotation.
- **Key terms:** المقطع (cross-section), المقطع العرضي (cross-section parallel to the base)
- **What's taught:** The shape formed where a plane slices through a solid is a cross-section; when that plane is parallel to the base it's specifically called a cross-section (as opposed to an angled slice), illustrated with prisms yielding triangular/rectangular/hexagonal cross-sections depending on cut angle. Also covers solids of revolution (a 3D solid generated by rotating a 2D shape around an axis).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slicing-plane tool where dragging a plane through a 3D solid at any angle live-reveals the resulting 2D cross-section shape, plus a spin-animation showing a 2D profile sweeping into a 3D solid of revolution.

#### Lesson 8.3: حجم الكرة ومساحة سطحها — Volume and Surface Area of a Sphere
- **Objective:** I find the volume and surface area of a sphere.
- **What's taught:** Surface area S.A=4πr² and volume V=(4/3)πr³, applied directly to compute a sphere's measurements from its radius, rounded to a given precision.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive sphere with a radius slider showing surface area and volume recompute live, alongside a visual comparison to a cylinder of the same radius/height to hint at the 2/3 volume relationship.

### Unit 9: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

#### Lesson 9.1: الرُّبَيعيات — Quartiles
- **Objective:** I find the interquartile range and relate it to data spread; I represent data with a box-and-whisker plot.
- **Key terms:** مقاييس التشتت (measures of spread), الرُّبَيعيات (quartiles), المدى الرُّبَيعي (interquartile range), الرُّبَيع الأدنى/الأعلى (lower/upper quartile)
- **What's taught:** Data is split at the median into lower and upper halves, each with its own median (the lower/upper quartile); the interquartile range (upper−lower quartile) measures spread resistant to outliers, represented visually as a box-and-whisker plot. Applied to testing a hypothesis about daily water-cup consumption.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive dot-plot-to-box-plot builder where dragging data points live recomputes and redraws the five-number summary (min, Q1, median, Q3, max) as a box-and-whisker diagram.

#### Lesson 9.2: اختيار التمثيل الأنسب — Choosing the Most Suitable Representation
- **Objective:** I choose the most appropriate graphical representation for a given data set.
- **What's taught:** Compares representations learned across grades (bar graph, line graph, pie chart, box-and-whisker plot, etc.) and gives criteria for which suits a given data type/question — e.g. box plots for spread and outlier-resistant comparison, pie charts for part-to-whole, line graphs for change over time.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "same data, every representation" toggle where one data set renders simultaneously as a bar graph, line graph, pie chart, and box plot, letting students see which reveals the requested insight most clearly.

#### Lesson 9.3: عَدّ النواتج — Counting Outcomes
- **Objective:** I determine the outcomes of a sample space and their count.
- **Key terms:** النواتج (outcomes), الحادث (event), الفضاء العيني (sample space), مخطط الشجرة (tree diagram), مخطط الاحتمال (probability chart)
- **What's taught:** All possible outcomes of a random experiment form the sample space; students count combined-choice outcomes (e.g. major × university combinations) using tree diagrams and the fundamental counting principle (multiplying the number of choices at each stage).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive tree-diagram builder where adding choices at each branching level auto-expands the tree and tallies the total outcome count, cross-checked against the multiplication shortcut.

#### Lesson 9.4: احتمال الحوادث المركّبة — Probability of Compound Events
- **Objective:** I find the probability of compound events.
- **Key terms:** الحادث البسيط (simple event), الحادث المركّب (compound event)
- **What's taught:** A simple event has one outcome; a compound event combines two or more simple events (e.g. guessing a two-digit code with a parity constraint on each digit). Students compute compound-event probabilities by combining the sample-space-counting techniques from 9.3 with the basic probability ratio P(A) = favorable/total outcomes.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A two-stage random-choice simulator (e.g. spinner + coin) where students build the compound sample space via a tree diagram and watch the theoretical probability compare against many simulated trials.
