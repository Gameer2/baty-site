# Cambridge Primary & Lower Secondary Mathematics — What's Actually Available

**Important limitation, read first:** unlike the Jordan NCCD textbooks and the Cambridge IGCSE syllabus, the full Cambridge Primary (ages 5–11, "0096") and Cambridge Lower Secondary (ages 11–14, "0862") **curriculum frameworks are not publicly downloadable** — Cambridge gates them behind a password-protected portal for registered schools only. The `cambridge-lower-secondary-maths-curriculum-outline.txt` source file says this explicitly:

> "Only schools offering Cambridge Lower Secondary can access the full curriculum framework."

What was actually downloaded for these two programmes is:
- `cambridge-primary-brochure.txt` — a marketing brochure with **one example learning objective per strand per stage** (a curated taster, not the full objective list)
- `cambridge-lower-secondary-brochure.txt` — same format, marketing brochure with one example per strand per stage
- `cambridge-lower-secondary-maths-curriculum-outline.txt` — a short outline giving slightly more detail (multiple bullet objectives) but only for the **Number strand**, and only for **Stages 7 and 9** (Stage 8 and all other strands are not covered)
- `cambridge-vs-national-curriculum-england.txt` — a comparison document, not curriculum content

So there is **no lesson-by-lesson or even complete-objective-list source material** here the way there was for the Jordan textbooks (which are full narrative lesson books) or the Cambridge IGCSE syllabus (which is a complete exam specification). Building a "detailed topic list, not just heads" document at the same depth as the other files would mean inventing content that isn't in these sources — so instead, below is everything that genuinely exists in the downloaded material, organized clearly, plus what you'd need to get the real thing.

## Cambridge Primary Mathematics (0096) — ages 5–11, Stages 1–6

**Strands:** Number · Geometry and Measure · Statistics and Probability (all threaded through with "Thinking and Working Mathematically")

**Number sub-strands:** Counting and sequences · Money · Integers and powers · Place value, ordering and rounding · Fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio and proportion

**Geometry and Measure sub-strands:** Geometrical reasoning, shapes and measurements · Position and transformations · Time

**Statistics and Probability sub-strands:** (not broken out further in the source)

### Sample objective progression (the only concrete content given)

| Strand | Stage 1 | Stage 2 | Stage 3 | Stage 4 | Stage 5 | Stage 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number | Understand and visualise that halves can be combined to make wholes. | Understand and visualise that wholes, halves and quarters can be combined to create new fractions. | Estimate, add and subtract fractions with the same denominator (within one whole). | Estimate, add and subtract fractions with the same denominator. | Estimate, add and subtract fractions with the same denominator and denominators that are multiples of each other. | Estimate, add and subtract fractions with different denominators. |
| Geometry and Measure | Differentiate between 2D and 3D shapes. | Identify 2D and 3D shapes in familiar objects. | Recognise pictures, drawings and diagrams of 3D shapes. | Match nets to their corresponding 3D shapes. | Identify and sketch different nets for a cube. | Identify and sketch different nets for cubes, cuboids, prisms and pyramids. |
| Statistics and Probability | Describe data using familiar language (more, less, most, least) to answer non-statistical questions and discuss conclusions. | Describe data, identifying similarities and variations, to answer non-statistical and statistical questions and discuss conclusions. | Interpret data, identifying similarities and variations within data sets, to answer non-statistical and statistical questions and discuss conclusions. | Interpret data, identifying similarities and variations within and between data sets, to answer statistical questions; discuss conclusions considering sources of variation. | Interpret data, identifying patterns within and between data sets, to answer statistical questions; discuss conclusions considering sources of variation. | Interpret data, identifying patterns within and between data sets; discuss conclusions considering sources of variation, and check predictions. |

**Teaching approach noted in the source (useful for the lab's design):** Cambridge Primary uses a three-step **concrete → representational → abstract (CRA)** progression — learners first manipulate physical/virtual objects, then work with pictorial representations, then move to standard mathematical notation. This is a strong hint for how an interactive math lab tool should scaffold each concept: start with a manipulable visual model, only later introduce the symbolic form.

## Cambridge Lower Secondary Mathematics (0862) — ages 11–14, Stages 7–9

**Strands:** Number · Algebra · Geometry and Measure · Statistics and Probability (all threaded through with "Thinking and Working Mathematically")

**Number sub-strands:** Integers, powers and roots · Place value, ordering and rounding · Fractions, decimals, percentages, ratio and proportion · Calculation
**Algebra sub-strands:** Expressions, equations and formulae · Sequences, functions and graphs
**Geometry sub-strands:** Shapes and geometric reasoning · Position and movement
**Measure sub-strands:** Length, mass and capacity · Time and rates of change · Area, perimeter and volume
**Handling data sub-strands:** Planning and collecting data · Processing and presenting data · Interpreting and discussing results · Probability
**Problem solving:** Using techniques and skills in solving mathematical problems · Using understanding and strategies in solving problems

### Sample objective progression (brochure — one example per strand)

| Strand | Stage 7 | Stage 8 | Stage 9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Number | Understand the relationship between squares and corresponding square roots, and cubes and corresponding cube roots. | Recognise squares of negative and positive numbers, and corresponding square roots. | Use knowledge of square and cube roots to estimate surds. |
| Algebra | Understand and describe nth term rules algebraically (in the form n±a, a×n where a is a whole number). | Understand and describe nth term rules algebraically (in the form n±a, a×n, or an±b, where a and b are positive or negative integers or fractions). | Understand and describe nth term rules algebraically (in the form an±b, and in the form n², n³ or n²±a, where a is a whole number). |
| Geometry and Measure | Derive and use a formula for the volume of a cube or cuboid; calculate the volume of compound shapes made from cuboids (m³, cm³, mm³). | Use knowledge of area and volume to derive the formula for the volume of a triangular prism; calculate the volume of triangular prisms. | Use knowledge of area and volume to derive the formula for the volume of prisms and cylinders; calculate the volume of prisms and cylinders. |
| Statistics and Probability | Understand and explain that probabilities range from 0 to 1, and can be represented as proper fractions, decimals and percentages. | Understand that complementary events are two events with a total probability of 1. | Understand that the probability of multiple mutually exclusive events can be found by summation, and that all mutually exclusive events have a total probability of 1. |

### Extra detail available for Number only, Stages 7 and 9 (from the curriculum outline file)

**Stage 7 — Number**
- Integers, powers and roots: recognise negative numbers as positions on a number line, and in order, add and subtract positive and negative integers in context; recognise multiples, factors, common factors, primes (all less than 100), using simple divisibility tests; find the lowest common multiple in simple cases; use the Sieve of Eratosthenes for generating primes; recognise squares of whole numbers to at least 20×20 and corresponding square roots, using notation 7² and √49.
- Multiplication and division: multiply/divide decimals with 1–2 places by single-digit numbers (e.g. 13.7×8, 4.35÷5); understand that division with a non-multiple dividend leaves a remainder, expressible as a fraction of the divisor (e.g. 157÷25 = 6 remainder 7 = 6 7/25); know when to round up or down after division depending on context.

**Stage 9 — Number**
- Integers, powers and roots: add, subtract, multiply and divide directed (signed) numbers; estimate square roots and cube roots; use positive, negative and zero indices and the index laws for multiplication/division of positive integer powers.
- Multiplication and division: multiply by decimals, understanding decimal-point placement via equivalent calculations; divide by decimals by transforming to division by an integer; recognise the effects of multiplying/dividing by numbers between 0 and 1.

## What this means for building the math lab

For Primary and Lower Secondary, you don't have enough source depth here to design lesson-specific visualizations the way the Jordan and IGCSE files allow — you only have strand names, sub-strand names, and a handful of example objectives per stage. Two ways to close the gap if you want full detail:

1. **Register as a Cambridge school (or get someone who is one) to download the actual password-protected curriculum framework PDFs** for 0096 and 0862 — these contain the complete stage-by-stage objective lists Cambridge references above.
2. **Source the endorsed Cambridge Primary/Lower Secondary Mathematics textbooks** (e.g. the Cambridge University Press / Collins series built for syllabuses 0096 and 0862) the same way the Jordan NCCD textbooks were sourced — these are narrative lesson books with worked examples and would extract the same way the Jordan grade files did.

Until then, the strand/sub-strand structure above is still useful as a **coverage checklist**: it tells you which content areas (e.g. "Position and movement," "Time and rates of change") your lab needs to eventually support, even without lesson-level detail yet.
