# Grade 5 — Detailed Math Topic List (Jordan National Curriculum)

## Semester 1

### Unit 1: الأعداد الكلّية — Whole Numbers

#### Lesson 1.1: القيمة المنزلية ضمن الملايين — Place Value Within Millions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I determine the place value of a digit in a number within the millions; I read and write numbers within the millions.
- **Key terms:** دورة الملايين (millions period)
- **What's taught:** Extends the ones/thousands "period" grouping (each group of 3 digits) to a third period, millions, using a place-value chart with ones/tens/hundreds columns repeated per period. Students identify a marked digit's place value using the chart, and convert numbers between standard form, word form (الصيغة اللفظية), and expanded form (الصيغة التحليلية) — including real 7-8 digit numbers like Jordan's population or planetary distances.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive place-value chart where dragging digits into columns builds a number live, with each period visually grouped/colored and a toggle that reveals the number simultaneously in standard, word, and expanded form.

#### Lesson 1.2: مقارنة الأعداد وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I compare numbers within the millions, and order them.
- **What's taught:** Students compare multi-digit numbers by stacking them vertically, aligning place values, and scanning digit-by-digit from the left until a difference is found (using >, <, =), then extend this to sorting three or more numbers ascending or descending. Applied to real data (pilgrim counts by year, tourist arrivals, ocean areas).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A digit-by-digit "race" animation that highlights matching leftmost digits as ties and stops/highlights the first differing column that decides the comparison, making the left-to-right scanning rule visible rather than abstract.

#### Lesson 1.3: جمع الأعداد الكلّية وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Whole Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I add or subtract numbers within 7 digits.
- **What's taught:** Vertical addition/subtraction of large numbers using the place-value chart, with explicit regrouping (carrying/borrowing) steps shown per column, plus front-end estimation (rounding each number to its leading digit before adding/subtracting) used both to sanity-check the exact answer and as a real skill in its own right. Applied to profit totals, trade/export data, animal-population counts.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A column-by-column animated addition/subtraction that visibly "carries" or "borrows" a regrouped unit into the next column, synced with a running estimate shown alongside the exact computation so students see both threads at once.

#### Lesson 1.4: الأعداد السالبة — Negative Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I recognize negative numbers, and place them on a number line.
- **Key terms:** العدد السالب (negative number)
- **What's taught:** Introduces negative numbers as representing values below a zero reference point (sea level, ground floor, temperature below freezing), using a vertical number-line model (fish below sea level, a building's basement floors, temperatures). Students read a position as a signed number from a diagram, and place given numbers on a number line, distinguishing negative from positive by their side of zero.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A vertical "sea level" scene (or building floor indicator) where dragging a marker up/down live-updates its signed-number readout, directly linking real-world "below a reference point" situations to negative-number notation.

#### Lesson 1.5: خطة حل المسألة: أنشئ جدولاً — Problem-Solving Strategy: Make a Table
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I solve real-life problems using the "make a table" strategy.
- **What's taught:** A dedicated problem-solving-strategy lesson (Understand/Plan/Solve/Check framework) where students build an explicit table of paired quantities (e.g. green marbles vs. matching red marbles at a fixed ratio, or coin/combination counts) to systematically find a target value or count outcomes, rather than computing directly.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An auto-growing table tool where entering a pattern rule (e.g. "3 red per green") fills in successive rows live, letting students watch the table extend toward the target row instead of manually recomputing each one.

### Unit 2: الضرب والقسمة — Multiplication and Division

#### Lesson 2.1: الضرب الذهني — Mental Multiplication
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the product of whole numbers mentally using doubling and halving.
- **Key terms:** المضاعفة (doubling), التنصيف (halving)
- **What's taught:** Doubling and halving are introduced as inverse operations that can simplify a multiplication: if one factor is even, halve it and double the other factor repeatedly until the product becomes an easy "basic fact" (e.g. 35×16 → 70×8 → 560). Both doubling and halving of two-digit numbers are also taught via place-value decomposition (splitting into tens+ones and distributing).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "balance" animation showing one factor shrinking by half while the other grows by double, with the product bar staying visually constant in area/length throughout — making concrete why the trick preserves the product.

#### Lesson 2.2: تقدير نواتج الضرب — Estimating Products
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I estimate multiplication products using rounding.
- **Key terms:** الأعداد المتناغمة (compatible numbers)
- **What's taught:** Two estimation strategies: rounding one or both factors to a leading place value before multiplying with basic facts, and choosing "compatible numbers" (nearby numbers that are easy to multiply mentally, e.g. rounding 2491 to 2500 since 2500×4 is easy). Students also reason about whether an estimate is an over- or under-estimate based on rounding direction.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A rounding slider on each factor that shows the estimate updating live alongside the exact product, with a visual "over/under" indicator showing which direction the estimate drifted and why.

#### Lesson 2.3: ضرب الأعداد — Multiplying Whole Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I multiply a whole number by a 1-digit number; I multiply a whole number of up to 3 digits by a 2-digit number.
- **What's taught:** The standard multiplication algorithm, place value column by column (ones, tens, hundreds, thousands) with regrouping/carrying, extended to two-digit multipliers (partial products for tens and ones, then summed). Each worked example opens with an estimate for a plausibility check against the exact answer. Applied to real contexts (recycling collection totals, theater seating, distance × trips).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A step-through algorithm animator that highlights each partial product being computed and placed, then visually sums the shifted partial products — with the pre-computed estimate shown alongside as a running sanity check.

#### Lesson 2.4: تقدير ناتج القسمة — Estimating Quotients
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I estimate quotients using compatible numbers.
- **What's taught:** Estimating a division result by rounding the divisor (and/or dividend) to a compatible pair that divides evenly and mentally (e.g. 418÷82 → 400÷80=5), including choosing between two possible compatible roundings and comparing which is closer to the actual divisor.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual-number-line tool showing the actual divisor and nearby "easy" compatible numbers, letting students drag to pick a rounding and see the resulting quick estimate compute live.

#### Lesson 2.5: قسمة الأعداد — Dividing Whole Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I divide a whole number of up to 3 digits by a number of 1 or 2 digits, using long division; I interpret a remainder.
- **Key terms:** باقي القسمة (remainder)
- **What's taught:** The standard long-division algorithm (estimate the first quotient digit's place, divide, multiply, subtract, bring down, repeat) is taught step by step, always preceded by a compatible-number estimate for a plausibility check, and followed by verifying the answer via multiplication-plus-remainder. A key applied skill is interpreting what a remainder means in context — sometimes it's discarded, sometimes it forces rounding the quotient up by one (e.g. needing one more bus to carry leftover passengers).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated long-division "staircase" that highlights each divide/multiply/subtract/bring-down cycle in sequence, paired with a real-world remainder-interpretation prompt (round up, keep as leftover, or discard) that the student must answer correctly before proceeding to the next problem.

### Unit 3: خصائص الأعداد — Properties of Numbers

#### Lesson 3.1: قابلية القسمة على 4, 6, 9 — Divisibility by 4, 6, and 9
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I test numbers for divisibility by 4, 6, and 9.
- **Key terms:** قواعد قابلية القسمة (divisibility rules)
- **What's taught:** Building on prior rules for 2, 3, 5, 10, students learn: divisible by 4 if the last two digits (as a two-digit number) are divisible by 4; divisible by 6 if divisible by both 2 and 3 simultaneously; divisible by 9 if the digit sum is divisible by 9. Applied to real equal-sharing contexts (dividing seeds into containers, distributing microscope slides among lab groups) and open-ended digit-completion problems.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live checker where typing a number highlights the relevant digits per rule (last two digits for 4, all digits summing for 9, both parity and digit-sum checks for 6) and shows an instant pass/fail per rule.

#### Lesson 3.2: تحليل العدد إلى عوامله الأولية — Prime Factorization
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I factor a number into its prime factors.
- **Key terms:** التحليل إلى العوامل الأولية (prime factorization), شجرة العوامل (factor tree)
- **What's taught:** A composite number is broken into a product of primes via two methods shown side by side on the same number (60=2×2×3×5 either way, just different branch order): the factor tree, splitting into a factor pair repeatedly until every branch is prime; and repeated division, dividing successively by prime factors until reaching 1. Preceded by a concept activity using Venn diagrams to find common factors of two numbers.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive factor tree where tapping a composite node splits it into a chosen factor pair (with immediate feedback if a non-prime pair is chosen for a branch meant to terminate), alongside a toggle showing the same number's repeated-division ladder.

#### Lesson 3.3: العامل المشترك الأكبر — Greatest Common Factor
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the greatest common factor of two numbers.
- **Key terms:** العوامل المشتركة (common factors), العامل المشترك الأكبر (greatest common factor, ع.م.أ)
- **What's taught:** Two methods: listing every factor of each number and picking the largest one they share (reinforced with a Venn-diagram organizer), and — building on the prior lesson — prime-factorizing both numbers and multiplying only the shared prime factors (taking one instance of each matched pair). Applied to real equal-portioning problems (packing dates and water cups into identical boxes, cutting two different-length wood pieces into equal-length segments).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-and-drop Venn diagram where each number's factor list is sorted into overlapping circles, auto-highlighting the shared factors and flagging the largest as the GCF.

#### Lesson 3.4: المضاعف المشترك الأصغر — Least Common Multiple
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find multiples of whole numbers; I find the least common multiple of two numbers.
- **Key terms:** المضاعف (multiple), المضاعفات المشتركة (common multiples), المضاعف المشترك الأصغر (least common multiple, م.م.أ)
- **What's taught:** A multiple is any product of a number with a whole number (excluding zero); students list a number's first several multiples, then find the LCM either by listing both numbers' multiples until the first shared value appears, or by prime-factorizing both and multiplying each prime at its highest occurring power. Real contexts include two interlocking gears realigning at a mark, and stacked boxes of two different heights reaching equal column height.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Animated interlocking gears with different tooth counts that visibly realign at the LCM number of rotations, paired with a dual skip-counting number line highlighting the first shared multiple.

#### Lesson 3.5: مربع العدد والجذر التربيعي — Square of a Number and Square Root
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the square of a number, and the square root of a number.
- **Key terms:** مربع العدد (square of a number), المربع الكامل (perfect square), الجذر التربيعي (square root)
- **What's taught:** Squaring is introduced via a growing dot-pattern (side length increasing 1,2,3,4...), formalized as multiplying a number by itself, with "perfect square" defined for whole-number squares. Square root is the inverse — the number which, multiplied by itself, gives the perfect square — applied to finding a square garden's side length from its area. A multiplication table doubles as a perfect-square/root finder.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive n×n growing tile grid where dragging a side-length slider shows n² tiles building up live, with a reverse mode where entering an area highlights the matching square's side length (its root).

### Unit 4: الكسور والعمليات عليها — Fractions and Operations on Them

#### Lesson 4.1: الأعداد الكسرية — Mixed Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I write an improper fraction as a mixed number; I write a mixed number as an improper fraction.
- **Key terms:** العدد الكسري (mixed number), الكسر الفعلي (proper fraction), الكسر غير الفعلي (improper fraction)
- **What's taught:** Distinguishes proper fractions (numerator < denominator), improper fractions (numerator ≥ denominator), and mixed numbers (whole + proper fraction). Converting improper→mixed is shown two ways: long division (quotient becomes the whole number, remainder over the original denominator) and marking the value on a number line and counting whole segments. Converting mixed→improper uses multiply-then-add (whole×denominator+numerator, over the same denominator) or repeated addition of whole-number fractions.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line tool where dragging a point shows both its improper-fraction and mixed-number label simultaneously updating, plus a "pie stack" model where whole circles combine with a partial circle to visually total the improper fraction.

#### Lesson 4.2: مقارنة الكسور والأعداد الكسرية وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Fractions and Mixed Numbers
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I compare fractions and mixed numbers, and order them.
- **What's taught:** For same-denominator fractions, the larger numerator wins; for same-numerator fractions, the smaller denominator wins (fewer, bigger pieces). For unlike fractions, students find the LCM of the denominators, convert both to equivalent fractions with that common denominator, then compare numerators. For mixed numbers, whole-number parts are compared first, with fractional parts as a tiebreaker. Concept activities using colored fraction-bar shading precede the formal rules.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** Stacked fraction bars of adjustable denominator that shade to represent each fraction, auto-aligning to a common denominator grid so the size comparison is directly visible before the symbolic answer is shown.

#### Lesson 4.3: جمع الكسور — Adding Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I add unlike fractions; I add unlike mixed numbers.
- **What's taught:** For two fractions where one denominator is a multiple of the other, students convert the smaller-denominator fraction to an equivalent fraction with the larger (shared) denominator, then add straightforwardly. For mixed numbers, fractional parts are converted and added first, then whole-number parts are added, with the final answer simplified. Circle-model diagrams verify each result visually.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A dual-circle "pie" addition animator where one circle's slices resize to match the other's denominator before the two pie-fills visually combine into the sum.

#### Lesson 4.4: طرح الكسور — Subtracting Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I subtract unlike fractions and unlike mixed numbers, where one denominator is a multiple of the other.
- **What's taught:** Mirrors the addition lesson's method — rewrite one fraction using an equivalent fraction sharing the other's denominator, then subtract like fractions (and whole-number parts separately for mixed numbers), simplifying the result.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "take away" pie-slice animator where slices of the resized fraction are removed one by one from the larger pie, visually landing on the difference.

#### Lesson 4.5: ضرب الكسور — Multiplying Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I find the product of fractions in simplest form.
- **What's taught:** Multiply numerators together and denominators together, discovered via a concept activity beforehand; students learn to simplify either fraction first (canceling a shared factor between a numerator and the other fraction's denominator) before multiplying, to keep numbers small, then simplify the final product. Applied to real proportional-quantity contexts (fraction of a watermelon's mass that's water, scaling a glue recipe, splitting land between two crop types).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An overlapping-rectangle area model where one fraction's shaded rows cross another's shaded columns, and the doubly-shaded overlap region visually is the product — directly showing why numerators and denominators multiply.

#### Lesson 4.6: قسمة الكسور — Dividing Fractions
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I divide a fraction by a fraction.
- **Key terms:** المقلوب (reciprocal)
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity dividing fractions using shaded models (asking "how many sixths fit in two-thirds?"), which is then connected to the "invert and multiply" shortcut: two numbers are reciprocals if their product is 1, and dividing by a fraction equals multiplying by its reciprocal.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A "how many fit" tiling animation where a divisor fraction's unit is repeatedly laid inside the dividend's shaded region, counting how many copies fit (including partial copies) to build the division-as-measurement intuition before showing the invert-and-multiply shortcut as a faster equivalent.

### Unit 5: تمثيل البيانات وتفسيرها — Representing and Interpreting Data

#### Lesson 5.1: السؤال الإحصائي — The Statistical Question
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I distinguish the statistical question.
- **Key terms:** السؤال الإحصائي (statistical question), السؤال غير الإحصائي (non-statistical question)
- **What's taught:** A statistical question is one that produces varied answers across different people (e.g. "how do you get to school?"); a non-statistical question has one fixed answer for everyone (e.g. "does Earth orbit the Sun?"). Students classify given questions, write their own statistical and non-statistical questions about a scenario (zoo visitors, sports fans), and distinguish subtly similar question pairs (e.g. "how many trees in the school garden" vs. "how many trees in your home garden" — one is fixed once posed to a specific place, one varies by respondent).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sorting activity where sample questions are dragged into "statistical" vs. "not statistical" bins with instant feedback and a reason shown; a simulated set of virtual respondents that "answer" a typed-in question live to reveal whether responses vary.

#### Lesson 5.2: المستوى الإحداثي — The Coordinate Plane
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I read points in the coordinate plane, and plot them.
- **Key terms:** المستوى الإحداثي (coordinate plane), المحور x / المحور y (x-axis / y-axis), نقطة الأصل (origin), زوج مرتب (ordered pair)
- **What's taught:** First-quadrant-only coordinate plane: two perpendicular numbered axes meeting at the origin (0,0); any point corresponds to an ordered pair (x,y) reached by moving right along x then up along y from the origin. Students read coordinates from labeled points (including reading a map-style scene — lake, forest, hospital, house), plot given ordered pairs, and are cautioned that order matters (x always comes before y).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive grid where clicking places a point and its (x,y) label appears live, or dragging an existing point updates its coordinate readout in real time; a clickable "map" where landmarks reveal their coordinates on click.

#### Lesson 5.3: التمثيل بالخطوط — Line Graph Representation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I represent data with line graphs, then read and interpret them.
- **Key terms:** التمثيل بالخطوط (line graph)
- **What's taught:** A line graph shows how data changes over time: time goes on the horizontal axis, the measured quantity on the vertical axis, one point per time period, points connected by straight segments. Students build a graph from a data table (child's height by age, population by year, temperature over a week, plant growth, rainfall by month) and read it back — identifying max/min, describing change between two points, comparing specific readings. A "jagged break" convention for an axis that doesn't start near zero is introduced.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-to-plot line-graph builder where placing points from a data table auto-draws the connecting line; a playback animation that "draws" the line left to right over time with a readout marker showing the exact value at any point along it.

#### Lesson 5.4: التمثيل بالأعمدة المزدوجة — Double Bar Graph Representation
- **Objective (فكرة الدرس):** I represent two sets of data with double bars, then read and interpret them.
- **Key terms:** التمثيل بالأعمدة المزدوجة (double bar graph)
- **What's taught:** Extends single bar graphs to represent two related data sets side by side per category (e.g. favorite hobbies of 4th vs. 5th graders, homework minutes on two different days) for direct comparison: choose a scale spanning both data sets, draw two adjacent differently-colored bars per category, add a legend. Students read values from both bars in a category, identify which is higher/lower, and sum values across categories.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive builder where assigning two data series to categories renders paired colored bars with a live legend, and hovering/clicking a category surfaces the numeric gap between its two bars.

## Semester 2

### Unit 6: الكسور العشرية والنسبة المئوية — Decimals and Percentage

#### Lesson 6.1: أجزاء الألف — Thousandths
- **Objective:** I determine the place value of a digit in a decimal within the thousandths; I write decimals in standard, word, and expanded form.
- **What's taught:** Extends the place-value chart rightward past hundredths to thousandths, with the decimal point sitting between ones and tenths. Students identify a marked digit's value and convert between standard/word/expanded forms for numbers with up to three decimal places.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An extended place-value chart where dragging digits past the decimal point live-builds the number and shows all three representations simultaneously.

#### Lesson 6.2: التحويل بين الكسور والأعداد العشرية — Converting Between Fractions and Decimals
- **Objective:** I convert fractions to decimals and decimals to fractions.
- **What's taught:** Fraction→decimal uses either scaling to an equivalent fraction with denominator 10/100/1000, or long division. Decimal→fraction writes the decimal directly over the matching power of ten and simplifies. Applied to real mixed-unit contexts (flag dimensions, alloy composition, stadium seating).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A live dual-display where typing a fraction shows its long-division steps animate into a decimal, and vice versa, with a simplification step highlighted.

#### Lesson 6.3: مقارنة الأعداد العشرية وترتيبها — Comparing and Ordering Decimals
- **Objective:** I compare decimals and order them.
- **What's taught:** Extends whole-number left-to-right digit comparison to decimals, using the place-value chart or a number line, including comparing decimals with different numbers of decimal places (padding with trailing zeros conceptually).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A zoomable number line that lets students place two close decimals and visually see which is further right (larger).

#### Lesson 6.4: تقريب الأعداد العشرية — Rounding Decimals
- **Objective:** I round decimals to a given place value.
- **What's taught:** Standard rounding rule (look at the digit to the right; 5+ rounds up) applied to tenths/hundredths/whole numbers, in real contexts like chemical composition percentages.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider on a zoomed number line showing a decimal's exact position between two rounding targets, snapping to whichever it's closer to.

#### Lesson 6.5: تقدير نواتج جمع الأعداد العشرية وطرحها — Estimating Sums and Differences of Decimals
- **Objective:** I estimate sums and differences of decimals by rounding.
- **What's taught:** Round each decimal to a convenient place value (usually whole numbers) before adding/subtracting mentally, used for plausibility-checking exact answers, in contexts like Earth's rotation speed.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A rounding slider on each addend showing the estimate updating live alongside the exact sum.

#### Lesson 6.6: جمع الأعداد العشرية وطرحها — Adding and Subtracting Decimals
- **Objective:** I add and subtract decimals.
- **What's taught:** Vertical addition/subtraction aligning decimal points (padding with zeros so both numbers have equal decimal places), carrying/borrowing as with whole numbers, decimal point dropped straight down into the answer. Applied to melting-point differences and similar comparisons.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A column-aligned animator that snaps two decimals' points into alignment before stepping through the addition/subtraction, visually padding missing decimal places with zero-tiles.

#### Lesson 6.7: ضرب الأعداد العشرية وقسمتها على 10, 100, 1000 — Multiplying and Dividing Decimals by 10, 100, 1000
- **Objective:** I multiply and divide decimals by 10, 100, and 1000.
- **What's taught:** The decimal point shifts right (multiplying) or left (dividing) by as many places as there are zeros in the power of ten, adding zeros as placeholders when digits run out. Applied to unit-price and packaging contexts (bottles per box).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated decimal point that visibly "jumps" the correct number of places across a digit strip as the power of ten is dialed in.

#### Lesson 6.8: النسبة المئوية — Percentage
- **Objective:** I recognize percentage, and solve problems involving it.
- **Key terms:** النسبة المئوية (percentage)
- **What's taught:** A percentage is a fraction out of 100, written with the % symbol. Students convert a shaded portion of a 100-grid to a percentage and back, and apply percentages to real proportions (test scores out of 100, sport preferences, crop composition, gender ratios).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A 100-cell grid where clicking cells shades them and the percentage/fraction/decimal readout updates live in all three forms simultaneously.

### Unit 7: المقادير الجبرية والمعادلات — Algebraic Expressions and Equations

#### Lesson 7.1: إيجاد قيمة المقدار الجبري — Evaluating an Algebraic Expression
- **Objective:** I write an algebraic expression using symbols, and find its value.
- **Key terms:** التعويض (substitution)
- **What's taught:** An algebraic expression (e.g. x−2, 2x) stands for an unknown quantity; substituting a given value for the variable evaluates it, modeled physically with algebra tiles (unit squares + a variable rectangle). Students translate word phrases into expressions and back, and apply this to real quantity-tracking contexts (calorie totals, ages, running totals during a pandemic case count).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A slider on the variable's value that live-updates the expression's computed result and a matching tile-count animation.

#### Lesson 7.2: معادلات الجمع والطرح — Addition and Subtraction Equations
- **Objective:** I solve addition and subtraction equations.
- **What's taught:** Preceded by a concept activity using an "equation mat" and algebra tiles to physically balance both sides. Equations like x+4=9 or x−11=7 are solved via mental math (fact families) or the inverse addition/subtraction relationship, always checked by substitution. Real contexts: donated toys, leftover ribbon length, river-length comparisons.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A balance-scale/equation-mat animator where removing or adding the same number of unit tiles from both sides visually isolates the variable.

#### Lesson 7.3: معادلات الضرب والقسمة — Multiplication and Division Equations
- **Objective:** I write and solve an equation involving multiplication or division.
- **What's taught:** Mirrors lesson 7.2 for equations like 8x=32 or x÷10=4, solved via mental math or the inverse multiplication/division relationship, with the equation-mat/algebra-tile model (grouping unit squares under each variable tile) as the concept bridge. Applied to production-rate and equal-distribution contexts (candy packaging, computer lab allocation).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive equation mat where dragging to "split" the variable-side tiles into equal groups matching the constant side visually solves for x.

#### Lesson 7.4: خطة حل المسألة: أرسم نموذجًا — Problem-Solving Strategy: Draw a Model
- **What's taught:** A dedicated problem-solving-strategy lesson (Understand/Plan/Solve/Check) where students draw a bar/tape-diagram model of a word problem (e.g. two people's donation counts, one a known amount more/less than the other) to derive the equation, rather than jumping straight to symbols.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A drag-to-build tape-diagram tool where students construct bar segments representing each quantity and their relationship, which then auto-generates the corresponding equation.

### Unit 8: الأشكال الهندسية والتحويلات — Geometric Shapes and Transformations

#### Lesson 8.1: مجموع الزوايا على مستقيم وحول نقطة — Sum of Angles on a Line and Around a Point
- **Objective:** I recognize that angles on a straight line sum to 180° and angles around a point sum to 360°.
- **What's taught:** Angles forming a straight line always sum to 180°; angles sharing a vertex and forming a full turn sum to 360°. Students solve for an unknown angle given the others (using the addition/subtraction relationship), and classify whether a given set of angle measures could represent angles on a line or around a point.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-angle diagram on a line or around a point where one angle's measure updates live and the remaining unknown angle recalculates automatically, visually reinforcing the fixed-sum constraint.

#### Lesson 8.2: المضلَّعات — Polygons
- **Objective:** I recognize a polygon and a regular polygon.
- **Key terms:** المضلَّع (polygon), المضلَّع المنتظم (regular polygon)
- **What's taught:** A polygon is a closed 2D shape with 3+ non-crossing straight sides. Students sort shapes into polygon/non-polygon, name polygons by side count, and distinguish regular (all sides and angles congruent) from irregular polygons.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sorting game dragging shapes into polygon/non-polygon bins with an explanation of which rule was broken (curved side, open shape, crossing sides).

#### Lesson 8.3: تصنيف المثلَّثات حسب أطوال أضلاعها — Classifying Triangles by Side Length
- **Objective:** I classify triangles by their side lengths.
- **Key terms:** متطابق الأضلاع (equilateral), متطابق الضلعين (isosceles), مختلف الأضلاع (scalene)
- **What's taught:** Triangles are sorted into equilateral (all 3 sides congruent), isosceles (2 sides congruent), and scalene (no sides congruent) based on given side-length markings or measurements.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex triangle where side lengths are shown live and the triangle auto-labels itself equilateral/isosceles/scalene as vertices are moved.

#### Lesson 8.4: تصنيف المثلَّثات حسب قياسات زواياها — Classifying Triangles by Angle Measure
- **Objective:** I classify triangles by their angle measures.
- **Key terms:** حاد الزوايا (acute), قائم الزاوية (right), منفرج الزاوية (obtuse)
- **What's taught:** Every triangle has at least two acute angles; the third angle determines its class (acute, right, or obtuse). When one angle is unknown, students first use the 180° angle-sum (from lesson 8.1's underlying fact, introduced via a concept activity) to find it, then classify.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex triangle with live angle readouts that auto-classifies as acute/right/obtuse, letting students drag toward each boundary case to feel where classification flips.

#### Lesson 8.5: تصنيف الأشكال الرباعية — Classifying Quadrilaterals
- **Objective:** I classify quadrilaterals by their sides and angles.
- **Key terms:** شبه المنحرف (trapezoid), متوازي الأضلاع (parallelogram), المستطيل (rectangle), المعين (rhombus), المربع (square)
- **What's taught:** Quadrilaterals are classified hierarchically by side/angle properties: trapezoid (exactly one pair of parallel sides), parallelogram (both pairs parallel and congruent), rectangle (parallelogram with right angles), rhombus (parallelogram with all sides congruent), square (both). Students classify shapes into every applicable category (not just one), and evaluate true/false relationship statements (e.g. "every rhombus is a rectangle" — false) using a hierarchy diagram/Venn diagram.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable-vertex quadrilateral that live-labels itself with every applicable category as its shape changes, plus an interactive nested Venn/hierarchy diagram that highlights which region a dragged shape currently falls into.

#### Lesson 8.6: الانسحاب — Translation
- **Objective:** I recognize translation of a shape.
- **Key terms:** الانسحاب (translation)
- **What's taught:** Translation moves every point of a shape the same direction and distance while preserving side lengths and angle measures, without rotating it — demonstrated by sliding a rectangle's vertices a fixed number of grid squares and confirming the image is congruent to the original.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A grid-based drag tool where sliding a shape shows its image tracing behind it in real time, with side-length and angle readouts confirming they stay unchanged throughout the slide.

#### Lesson 8.7: المنشور والهرم — Prism and Pyramid
- **Objective:** I recognize prisms and pyramids and their nets.
- **Key terms:** الشكل ثلاثي الأبعاد (3D shape), المنشور (prism), الهرم (pyramid), الوجه (face), الحرف (edge), الرأس (vertex), الشبكة (net)
- **What's taught:** A prism has two parallel congruent polygonal bases connected by rectangular side faces, named by its base shape; a pyramid has one base with triangular side faces meeting at a single apex point, also named by its base. Students count faces/edges/vertices for given solids, and identify which solid results from folding a given 2D net (or which net is invalid for forming any closed solid).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An animated fold/unfold tool where a 3D prism or pyramid unfolds into its flat net and refolds on demand, with face/edge/vertex counts tallied live as the student clicks each part.

### Unit 9: القياس — Measurement

#### Lesson 9.1: وحدات قياس الكتلة — Units of Mass
- **Objective:** I recognize compound mass units, and convert between them.
- **Key terms:** الوحدة المركَّبة (compound unit)
- **What's taught:** Grams, kilograms, and tonnes relate by factors of 1000 (×1000 going down, ÷1000 going up). Students convert between units, and handle "compound" mixed-unit measurements (e.g. 2 kg, 84 g) by converting to a single unit before comparing, adding, or ranking masses — applied to comparing twin birth weights and truck cargo loads.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A digital scale readout that toggles between single-unit and compound-unit display live as a slider changes the measured mass, reinforcing the conversion factor visually.

#### Lesson 9.2: وحدات قياس السعة والطول — Units of Capacity and Length
- **Objective:** I convert between units of capacity and length, and solve problems with compound units.
- **What's taught:** mL↔L (×1000/÷1000) and the km/m/cm/mm chain (×1000/×100/×10 stepping down) are converted and combined as compound measurements (e.g. 7 m, 56 cm), applied to real perimeter problems (fencing a garden) requiring unit unification before computing.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A unit-ladder slider (km→m→cm→mm) where dragging a value up or down the ladder animates the decimal point shifting by the correct power of ten at each step.

#### Lesson 9.3: الزمن — Time
- **Objective:** I calculate time using its different units.
- **Key terms:** توقيت 24 ساعة (24-hour time)
- **What's taught:** Year/day/hour/minute/second relate via ×365/×24/×60/×60 (and reverse), used to express a duration in compound units (e.g. 88 hours = 3 days, 16 hours, found via long division with the remainder as leftover hours) and to compute event end-times (e.g. a football match's total duration including halves and break).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive digital/analog clock-and-calendar combo where dragging an elapsed-time slider live-converts the duration into compound day/hour/minute form.

#### Lesson 9.4: محيط الشكل المركَّب ومساحته — Perimeter and Area of a Compound Shape
- **Objective:** I find the perimeter and area of compound shapes.
- **Key terms:** الشكل المركَّب (compound shape)
- **What's taught:** A compound shape results from combining or cutting rectangles/squares. Perimeter of an L-shaped cut corner equals the original rectangle's perimeter (a non-obvious shortcut shown visually). Area is found either by decomposing the shape into rectangles/squares and summing their areas, or by subtracting a "missing" rectangular region from a larger enclosing rectangle — both methods demonstrated on the same shape for comparison.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** An interactive compound-shape tool where dragging a dividing line splits the shape into named sub-rectangles with live individual and running-total area labels, toggleable against a "complete the rectangle and subtract" overlay mode.

### Unit 10: الإحصاء والاحتمالات — Statistics and Probability

#### Lesson 10.1: الوسط الحسابي — The Mean
- **Objective:** I calculate the mean of single-value data.
- **Key terms:** الوسط الحسابي/المعدَّل (mean)
- **What's taught:** Mean = sum of values ÷ count of values. Students compute it for raw data lists and for frequency-table data (multiplying each value by its frequency before summing), applied to test scores, shoe sizes, newborn masses.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A draggable set of value-tiles on a number line where the mean is shown as a computed marker that recalculates live as tiles are added, removed, or moved.

#### Lesson 10.2: الوسيط والمنوال — The Median and Mode
- **Objective:** I calculate the median and mode of single-value data.
- **Key terms:** الوسيط (median), المنوال (mode)
- **What's taught:** Median found by sorting data and crossing off from both ends toward the middle (averaging the two middle values if the count is even); mode is the most frequent value(s) — a data set can have one, several, or no mode. Real contexts: contest participant ages, favorite colors/sports (no numeric mean possible, so mode is the only usable measure).
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A sortable row of data tiles where clicking "find median" animates pairs being crossed off simultaneously from each end until the middle is reached; a frequency-count overlay that highlights the tallest stack(s) as the mode.

#### Lesson 10.3: المدى — The Range
- **Objective:** I find the range of single data and of data represented graphically or in a frequency table.
- **Key terms:** المدى (range)
- **What's taught:** Range = largest value − smallest value, describing how spread out data is. Applied to weekly temperature swings, farm production variation, COVID case counts, and reading range directly off a bar graph or frequency table.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A number-line span tool where dragging endpoints to the data's min and max shows the range as the highlighted interval length, live-updating as data changes.

#### Lesson 10.4: فرص الحدوث — Chances of Occurrence
- **Objective:** I distinguish between random-experiment outcomes based on whether their chances of occurring are equal or unequal.
- **Key terms:** الحادث (event/outcome), فرص متساوية (equal chances), فرص غير متساوية (unequal chances)
- **What's taught:** Precursor to formal probability: outcomes of a random experiment (dice roll, spinner, drawing from a bag/collection) have equal chance if their share of the total is equal (e.g. a fair die's 6 equally-sized faces) and unequal chance otherwise (e.g. a bag with more red candies than others). Students rank outcomes by likelihood without computing numeric probabilities yet.
- **Visualization/interaction potential:** A configurable spinner/bag builder where changing the proportion of each color/outcome instantly shows (via repeated random draws) whether outcomes feel equally or unequally likely, bridging into formal probability.
