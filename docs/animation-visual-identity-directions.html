Same curve, three signatures

What the math itself looks like, not the editor around it

The editor chrome already has an identity from the site's design system. This is about the rendered content — the curve, the fill, the tangent line — which right now is still plain strokes and flat fills, Manim's exact look. Same example (a curve, a shaded region, a tangent line) rendered three different ways below.

Direction A

Instrument

Precision-lab-equipment feel. Thin crisp lines, no softness, crosshair reticles at key points, values shown as readouts rather than labels. Extends the site's existing dark/teal/infrared identity directly — the most conservative of the three.

Line
1.5px crisp, no glow
Fill
Flat translucent, hairline top edge
Markers
Crosshair reticles, not dots
Labels
Mono readout chips, tabular-nums
Direction B

Ink & Light

Warm, editorial, alive — like a beautifully kept mathematician's notebook, not a printout. Slightly irregular line quality (still mathematically precise, just not ruler-perfect), serif captions as margin notes, a soft candlelight bloom at the point that matters.

Line
Layered ink strokes, soft variance
Fill
Warm wash, textured not flat
Markers
Soft bloom, no hard dot
Labels
Serif captions, dotted leader lines
Direction C

Depth Field

Cinematic — teal-to-infrared grade, soft depth-of-field on the background grid, volumetric glow along the curve itself. The boldest departure from Manim's flat vector look. No ambient decoration — everything rendered here is either the curve, its glow, or the frame around it.

Line
Glow halo + crisp core, gradient stroke
Fill
Volumetric vertical gradient
Background
Blurred grid, vignette — nothing that isn't the math
Labels
Glowing accent text, minimal

Note on cost: all three are now static-render cheap — none of them redraw on their own once painted. C's blur and gradients are still the most expensive per-pixel, but with no ambient animation forcing a continuous redraw loop, that cost is paid once and then nothing, not every frame forever.
