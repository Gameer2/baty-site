Depth Field · adaptive rendering, end to end

Full quality on capable devices, automatic and invisible everywhere else

Nothing here is a lower-quality fallback you'd choose — it's one system that starts at full richness and measures the real device it's running on, stepping down only what's needed, only if needed, and stepping back up the moment it can. Below: the exact architecture, the exact numbers, and a working version you can stress-test yourself.

1 · The two-layer architecture

the actual fix for most of the per-frame cost — draw what's static once, only redraw what's actually moving

once

Background layer

Grid + its blur, pre-rendered to an offscreen canvas at load. Never touched again unless the viewport resizes.

once

Vignette layer

Radial darkening at the edges. Painted once, sits on top of everything, ignored by every future frame.

→
once per tier

Curve bitmap

The curve itself never moves, so its glow is baked in once — soft blur along the whole curve, paid one time, free forever after.

idle = free

Whatever isn't moving

Nothing decorative exists here. If the scene isn't being scrubbed, the entire picture — curve, glow, everything — is one cached bitmap. Zero per-frame cost, at every tier.

→
per frame, only while moving

The tangent point

The one thing that's actually tied to real content: the point of tangency sliding as the timeline scrubs. Its glow can't be pre-cached, because its position changes — this is the only genuine per-frame cost in the whole scene.

skipped when idle

Render loop

Paused entirely when nothing is playing or being dragged, and when the scene scrolls off screen. Most of the time, this box does nothing at all.

paid once, reused forever the only real per-frame cost — tied to actual motion, not decoration

This isn't scripted — the slider adds real per-frame work, the monitor measures real frame time, and the tier system reacts exactly as it would on an actual weak device. The curve itself is one cached bitmap; only the tangent point is ever redrawn.

What to try: drag the slider to ~70% and hold — within about half a second it should drop to Tier 2 or 3, and you'll see the tangent's glow disappear (crisp line only) while the curve's own glow stays, because the curve was never the cost. Release it back to 0% and it'll hold at the lower tier for a couple of seconds before climbing back to Tier 0.

Agree or edit

If the tier thresholds or the timing (0.5s to degrade, 2s to recover) feel wrong once you've tried the slider — those are the exact numbers to push back on, everything else follows from them.
