Three ways to merge every topic into engines

All math topics, grades 4–10, both systems (Jordan national + Cambridge) — collapsed into a set of math-lab engines. Three philosophies, each with a different center of gravity. Pick one, or run the hybrid at the bottom.

+ Strengths

  • Direct syllabus map — instantly legible to teachers & parents
  • Every topic has one home — no ambiguity where something lives
  • Matches how textbooks & exams are organised

− Trade-offs

  • 5 engines to build & maintain; little reuse across pillars
PROPOSAL B

By grade band — the “staircase” engines

Three engines, one per cognitive stage. A student climbs the staircase: Foundations → Transition → Advanced. Each band spans all domains but only at that maturity level — fewer top-level engines, and “what year am I in?” is answered by design.

Foundations

Grades 4–6 · ages 9–12
Number
Place value, four operations, fractions, decimals, %, ratio, primes/LCM/GCD
Algebra

Shapes, angles, area/perimeter, first measurement

Transition

Grades 7–8 · ages 12–14

Linear equations, expressions, factorising, simultaneous eqns
Geometry
Congruence/similarity, Pythagoras, solids, constructions
Stats & Prob
Probability scale, combined events, data handling
Functions/Trig
First trig ratios, first functions

Advanced

Grades 9–10 · ages 14–16
Algebra
Quadratics, inequalities, algebraic fractions, sequences
Functions/Trig
Trig identities & rules, analytic geometry, periodic fns, poly division
Geometry
Circle theorems, vectors, 3D space geometry
Statistics
Cumulative freq, histograms, IQR, std deviation, conditional prob

+ Strengths

  • Age-appropriate by construction — assign a student their band
  • Cognitive progression is the whole point — visible & motivating
  • Only 3 top-level engines to navigate

− Trade-offs

  • Topics repeat across bands (fractions in all three) — duplication risk
  • A bright G6 student can’t easily reach ahead; remediation crosses bands
  • Doesn’t match the lab’s existing “instrument” identity — feels like a textbook
PROPOSAL C

By capability — the “instrument” engines

One engine per verb — what the student is doing, not which strand they’re in. A G4 pupil and a G10 pupil both use the Compute engine; one feeds it arithmetic, the other feeds it quadratics. Maximum reuse, and it matches the lab you’ve already built.

Recommendation

Build instruments once. Present them three ways.

You don’t have to pick. Use Proposal C (instruments) as the engine architecture — that’s what gets built and what matches your lab. Then layer A (strands) and B (grade bands) on top as views — filters a teacher or student toggles. One codebase, three front doors.

Reason
Why this wins

You get C’s reuse and identity-match (build five instruments, not fifteen chapter-pages), plus A’s teacher-legibility and B’s student-assignment as cheap view layers on top. The only real cost is tagging each topic with three labels instead of one — and you already have the strand and grade data from the curriculum-scope doc. The instruments are the durable asset; the filters are swappable when Jordan updates its syllabus or you add IB.
