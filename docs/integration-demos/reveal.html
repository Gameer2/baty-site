Definition

The derivative is a limit of slopes.

It gives the slope of the tangent line at a single point — the instantaneous rate of change.

Worked example

Power rule, on a parabola.

For f(x) = x², the slope at any point is 2x. The chart traces both.

Recap

Three things to remember.

1 — The derivative is a limit of secant slopes as the gap shrinks to zero.

2 — For xⁿ, the power rule gives n·xⁿ⁻¹ in one line.

3 — Geometrically, it's the slope of the tangent line at the point.
